# Issue #366: FEAT-042: a drawn circuit leaves JLS as a schematic and netlist a real PCB tool opens without hand editing, and a named-rule gate says whether the board can actually be built
- Lens: visionary
- Date: 2026-08-08
- Reviewer: Claude Opus 5
- Verdict: endorse-with-reframing

## What this is really for

Strip the four tasks away and the claim is: *a drawn circuit should be able to
become a physical thing without being drawn a second time*. That is a good claim
and it sits on the right side of the project's own line. `docs/capability-roadmap/sweep-06-physical-boundary.md`
separates "computing physical data" (declined, correctly) from "being a
legitimate front end to somebody else's physical flow", and cites
`src/jls/hdl/board/PcfEmitter.java` as proof JLS already does the FPGA analogue.
#366 is the PCB instance of that third category. The feature belongs.

Two of its ideas are better than the feature they serve and should outlive it:

- **"it exported" and "it can be built" are different claims.** This is the
  sharpest sentence in the tracker. JLS makes the fused claim everywhere:
  `-export` writes Verilog whose `reg [3:0] count = 4'h0;` (`test/resources/hdl/counter.v:19`)
  synthesizes on FPGA and evaporates on ASIC, with no warning; VCD export;
  `-i` image export. Nowhere does JLS say *what it could not promise*.
- **The disposition table must be total over the element registry.** `ElementRegistry.ALL`
  is 35 types; totality-with-a-reason is exactly the discipline `HdlExporter`
  arrived at the hard way (22 EXPORTED, 6 SKIPPED, 4 TOPOLOGY, 4 rejected —
  SubCircuit, Memory, RegisterFile, FieldExtend — enumerated in one message).

Those two ideas are *general*. #366 packages them as PCB-only. That is the core
of what follows.

## Reframing 1 — the critical path funds the route the governing capstone rates dominated

§6 declares `TASK-0085 → TASK-0089 → TASK-0091` the critical path, i.e. the
package schema and the KiCad `.net` emitter first. Open Question 1 then says the
route decision is unmade and belongs to #307. It is not unmade. #307's OQ-1 reads
*"Recommended default: the schematic. … Fund the `.net` emitter second, over the
same partition, once package data exists"*, and its KC-13-2 states the electrical
reason: `pcbnew` **drops unfootprinted components individually**, so a design at
90% footprint coverage yields "a partially populated board that still imports,
which is worse than a refusal because nothing announces the loss."

So §6's ordering funds 3.5 weeks (TASK-0085 + TASK-0089) on the route its own
capstone rates second, ahead of the 2-week route rated first. Invert it:
**TASK-0090 first, TASK-0085 and TASK-0089 held.**

The inversion is not cosmetic — it can delete work. #366's own evidence row notes
lepton-netlist's **30+ netlist backends** read gEDA `.sch`. If JLS emits an
annotated gEDA schematic (symbols embedded, `device=`/`footprint=`/`refdes=`
attributes on the symbols), then the KiCad `.net` is *generated by lepton-netlist*
rather than by TASK-0089 — and so are SPICE, PADS, and everything else, forever,
without JLS ever learning KiCad's netlist syntax. That syntax is a maintenance
liability #366 never prices: KiCad's netlist format has moved with major
releases, and #366 commits to a byte-golden over it.

Under that framing Open Question 1 does not need answering. It dissolves: there is
one emitter and one artifact, and "netlist" is a downstream verb.

## Reframing 2 — TASK-0085 is change D with the ambition amputated

Compare, side by side:

| TASK-0085 (as filed) | sweep-06 change D |
|---|---|
| part number, family, pin count, ordered sections, power pins, footprint name, electrical loading, cascade descriptor, provenance | name, pin list (name/direction/width), logic function, timing figure, area, loaded from a library file |
| unlocks: this feature | unlocks #100 LEF, #87 Liberty, #109 open PDKs, #110 flow conformance, #111 SPICE/LVS, and re-opens #89 SDF — whose *own recorded re-open condition* (`11-costed-rejections.md:810`) D literally is |
| 2 wk, shared with #349 | 10–16 wk |

These are the same seam cut at different depths. A 74-series package record *is* a
technology-cell record plus three realization fields (sections, power pins,
footprint). Filing them as unrelated schemas guarantees JLS ends up with two
part/cell tables, two loading models, and two answers to "what does this element
physically become" — which is precisely the failure §4 invariant 1 forbids for
net partitions and then permits for parts.

Concretely: **re-seat TASK-0085 as `realization` on one cell/part record**, where a
SKY130 standard cell and a 74HC00 are the same row type with different realization
payloads. The PCB feature then consumes a slice, and change F (Tiny Tapeout) and
change G (fanout rules) consume other slices of the same data. Cost to #366:
approximately zero at the 2-week scale, because the *schema shape* is the decision
and it is being made once either way. #307's OQ-5 already worries at this from the
other side ("both sibling surveys route packages to the silicon program, which
owns cells, not packages — so the work is unowned"). The answer is not "a
standalone item"; it is that packages and cells are one item.

## Reframing 3 — the gate should not be a sub-task of a PCB export

TASK-0091 is sweep-06's **change G** (electrical rule checking, 3–5 wk) narrowed
to whatever a board needs, and buried three features deep behind #336, #349 and
#365. `grep -rln "RuleId\|Diagnostic\|DesignRule" src/` returns nothing — JLS has
no named-rule checking mechanism at all, and sweep-06 calls that "the one thing
JLS most conspicuously lacks against Digital and Logisim-evolution."

The checks a student most needs — undriven inputs, multiply-driven non-tri-state
nets, combinational loops, width mismatch, fanout — are *not* PCB checks. They
should run in the editor, on every design, on day one, for students who will never
export a board. As filed, they arrive last and only for the students who do.

Invert: build the mechanism first as a general `Check` surface (stable rule id,
severity, one-line rationale, machine-readable report, `TellUser`/`LoadError`-shaped
diagnostics), and make "manufacturability" a **rule pack** over it. Effects:

- TASK-0091 stops being blocked by three features and starts delivering immediately.
- §5 criterion 6 ("the gate gates") becomes near-free.
- The same mechanism serves the HDL export's honesty gap (register-init-on-ASIC),
  the shuttle target, and the collab/import surfaces.
- The 2026-08-04 dedup comment notes JLS is on course to acquire "two answers to
  'is this netlist the circuit we simulated'" (#523's isomorphism vs #321's I3).
  A shared rule/report vocabulary is where that consolidates.

## Reframing 4 — the out-of-the-box one: bind through synthesis, not by hand

#366 binds *drawn element → package* directly. Count what that costs against the
registry. Roughly seventeen types have a plausible 74-series part. The other
eighteen are `CANNOT_PLACE` — including **StateMachine and TruthTable**, JLS's two
most expressive elements, which have no 74-series chip and never will. But both are
already in `HdlExporter.EXPORTED`: JLS can render them as Verilog *today*.

So there is a second route the issue never considers, and JLS already owns most of
it (`YosysLocator`, `YosysNetlist`, `CellValidator`, `NetlistImporter`,
`VerilogEmitter`, `test/jls/hdl/ToolLocator.java` with the skip-if-absent idiom):
**drawn circuit → Verilog → Yosys techmap against a 74-series library → netlist**.
On that route, StateMachine and TruthTable become buildable, word-wide registers
get decomposed by the techmapper rather than by FEAT-041's hand-written width
decomposition, and packing/cascade is `abc`'s problem.

I am not recommending this route wholesale, because it costs the thing #366's route
buys: synthesis destroys the 1:1 drawing↔instance correspondence, which is exactly
what killed #89 SDF and would break #523's stable-id partition isomorphism and
#307's claim 2. That is a real trade, not a free win. But it must be *recorded* as
a trade, and one cheap change keeps the door open:

> The disposition function must distinguish `PLACEABLE_DIRECTLY` from
> `PLACEABLE_VIA_SYNTHESIS` from `CANNOT_PLACE(reason)`. A student told "your state
> machine cannot be built" when the true answer is "not without synthesis" has been
> mis-taught by the tool.

## What I would keep untouched

§4 invariants 1–8 are the best part of the issue and generalize as-is; invariant 5
(attribution and license per part record) is the kind of thing that is only ever
added early. The insistence that the schematic emitter projects the `Circuit` graph
rather than the HDL model is right for geometry — though note it permanently forks
JLS into two element→reality mappings with two coverage tables, and §3 should say so.
Open Question 2 (the ten-line embedded-symbol experiment) is correctly identified as
the highest-value hour in the feature; nothing above changes that it comes first.

## The risk if none of this is taken

Seven weeks are spent producing a board path for the half of the element registry
that was already the easy half, using a bespoke part schema that the silicon
program must then either duplicate or refactor, gated by a rule engine that only
board-exporting students ever see. Every individual task lands. The arc does not
move.

## Verdict

**endorse-with-reframing.** Keep the outcome, the invariants, and the "exported ≠
buildable" insight. Re-sequence to schematic-first per #307's standing
recommendation and hold TASK-0089 pending whether lepton-netlist makes it
unnecessary; re-seat TASK-0085's schema as the cell/part layer with packaging as one
realization; lift TASK-0091 out of this feature into a general named-rule check
mechanism and consume a rule pack here; record the synthesis route as a costed
alternative and split `CANNOT_PLACE` accordingly.
