# Issue #772: TASK-C578-3: kit content carries its own open license and an authoring doc a stranger can follow — reviewed by a named external instructor whose feedback is addressed or refused by name
- Lens: adversarial
- Date: 2026-08-08
- Reviewer: Claude Sonnet 5
- Verdict: needs-rework

## Summary of the ask

TASK-C578-3 (task tier, part of FEAT-C33-4 / #578) wants three things: (1) an open content license for kit material, distinct from JLS's GPLv3 code license, applied to "every in-tree kit"; (2) a third-party authoring doc; (3) a review of the kit and the doc by "a named external instructor, starting from #509," with every feedback item addressed or refused by name. `ordering_after: ["TASK-C578-2", 509]`.

## Findings, most severe first

**1. The thing AC-3 requires you to review does not exist, and the issue does not depend on the work that creates it.**
The acceptance criteria require "A named external instructor reviews the kit and the authoring doc" (singular, definite "the kit"). But `find /home/user/JLS -iname "*kit*" -o -iname "*donzellini*"` turns up nothing — there is no in-tree course kit today. The kit that #578's own boundary designates as the worked instance is #575 (the Donzellini pack), which is itself open and unstarted (AC-1 "at least 8 labs ship" unmet). #772's `ordering_after` lists only `["TASK-C578-2", 509]` — it does **not** order after #575. So the stated prerequisites (#769 validator, #509 relationship) can clear while there is still no kit in the tree to hand to a reviewer. Recommendation: add #575 (or whichever issue lands the first shippable kit) to `ordering_after`, or explicitly state AC-3 is conditional on a kit existing.

**2. Which kit gets reviewed is an open question the issue doesn't acknowledge.**
The parent issue #578 carries a same-day maintainer comment (2026-08-04) that flags exactly this: "the set now has two plausible referents for 'the flagship kit' — #575's pack ... and #577's CSE 260M corpus ... Both readings are defensible and the two issues currently answer differently. That belongs to CAP-33 (#517) to settle; it is escalated rather than resolved here." #772 was filed the same day as that comment and inherits the ambiguity without mentioning it: it just says "the kit." If the escalation to #517 isn't resolved before this task starts, whoever executes #772 has to guess which kit(s) go in front of the reviewer, and could satisfy the letter of AC-3 by picking whichever one is more convenient rather than whichever the project actually needs reviewed. Recommendation: either block explicitly on #517's resolution, or state in this issue which kit(s) are in scope for review.

**3. "Starting from #509" borrows a relationship that is itself unconfirmed, unrelated in scope, and possibly conflicted as a reviewer choice.**
#509's own body: Dr. Siever "is interested in this fork if it becomes well enough matured" — a conditional statement about migrating an entire course, not an offer to review a licensing doc. #509 AC-1 ("A written criteria list exists ... confirmed by Dr. Siever as sufficient for migration") is unmet, and a maintainer comment dated the same day as this review (2026-08-08) is still actively re-deriving #509's dependency graph, which signals the relationship is unsettled, not a stable resource #772 can draw an external reviewer from on demand. Two compounding problems:
 - **Content mismatch:** if "the kit" under review is #575's Donzellini pack (the designated worked instance per finding 2), Dr. Siever's course (WashU CSE 260M) has no connection to the Donzellini text or that pack's content — he's not an obviously qualified reviewer for it.
 - **Incentive mismatch:** Dr. Siever has a stated interest in this fork "becoming well enough matured" for his own migration. Asking him to serve as the independent outside reviewer whose approval is supposed to prove the kit convention "works for a stranger" (the issue's own framing: "tested the only way that counts") puts the one available external reviewer in a position where a positive, low-friction review serves his own interest. That undercuts the outcome statement's claim that this is real outside validation. Recommendation: name the actual reviewer explicitly and confirm (in writing, on-issue) that they've agreed to this specific, scoped ask before treating #509 as a source of it; consider a reviewer with no separate adoption stake.

**4. AC-4 ("applied to every in-tree kit") is vacuously satisfiable.**
Because zero kits currently exist in tree (see finding 1), "every in-tree kit carries a license" is true by emptiness the moment this task starts, regardless of whether the licensing scheme is any good. Nothing in the acceptance criteria forces at least one real kit to exist and demonstrably carry the license before the checkbox can be honestly ticked. Recommendation: tie AC-4 to a concrete artifact ("the #575 pack, once shipped, carries license X") rather than a universally-quantified claim over a possibly-empty set.

**5. "Open license" is undefined, and the project has no precedent to anchor it.**
`grep -n -i license CONTRIBUTING.md` shows only the code license (GPLv3-or-later, per README.md:347-350 and CONTRIBUTING.md:136-139); there is no existing content-licensing convention anywhere in the tree (no docs/, no LICENSE-CONTENT, nothing) to inherit from. #772 says only "a stated open license" — no requirement that it be OSI-approved, Creative-Commons "approved for free cultural works," or otherwise checked against a defined bar. A maintainer could label content under a restrictive "source available, no redistribution" scheme and describe it as "open" in prose, technically satisfying the AC as written. Recommendation: name the license or license family (e.g., CC-BY-4.0) or at minimum define "open" by reference to an external standard (OSI/Definition of Free Cultural Works).

**6. AC-3/AC-5 review quality is gameable — "reviewed" and "addressed" have no floor.**
"Their feedback is recorded ... with each item addressed or refused by name" is satisfiable by a single one-line "looks fine, no notes" comment from the reviewer, and "addressed" is satisfiable by a reply comment with no actual change. Nothing requires the reviewer to actually attempt authoring a kit from the doc (the load-bearing claim the issue itself makes — "kit content ... an authoring doc a stranger can follow" and "so a third-party author gets a list rather than a rejection" is TASK-C578-2's language, but the spirit carries here too). Without a minimum-substance bar (e.g., "the reviewer attempts to build a stub kit using only the doc"), the review can be a rubber stamp that formally closes AC-3/AC-5 while leaving the doc's real usability by a stranger untested — which is precisely the failure mode the issue's own title claims to guard against ("an authoring doc a stranger can follow"). Recommendation: require the reviewer to actually attempt kit authoring from the doc as a raw exercise, not just read and comment.

**7. Effort band (0.5-1 mw) doesn't price in the external-dependency wait.**
The task bundles a licensing decision, a full tutorial-grade authoring doc, and coordinating + waiting on a third-party professor's calendar for a substantive review — three different work types, one of which (external human review turnaround) is not bounded by engineer-hours at all. A single-maintainer project depending on one named external party's responsiveness, with no fallback reviewer named, risks this task stalling indefinitely inside a 0.5-1 mw estimate that assumes the review happens promptly. Recommendation: split the engineering deliverable (license + doc) from the external-review milestone, or explicitly flag the review as clock-time-bound rather than effort-bound.

**8. Minor scope overlap risk with TASK-C578-1.**
TASK-C578-1 (#767) already commits to a spec "[w]ritten well enough that an instructor outside this project can author one without asking a maintainer what goes where." TASK-C578-3's "authoring doc [that] walks a third-party author from empty directory to a validating kit" covers very similar ground (reference spec vs. tutorial walkthrough). The two are plausibly distinct (spec vs. tutorial) but #772 doesn't state the distinction, risking either duplicated content or a stranger not knowing which document to start from. Recommendation: one sentence in the Boundary section clarifying doc-vs-spec roles would remove the ambiguity cheaply.

## What's solid

- The Boundary section correctly scopes out layout (TASK-C578-1) and validation (TASK-C578-2), keeping this task to licensing/doc/review — good task decomposition in principle.
- "A refusal carries its reason; an unaddressed item is not left silently unresolved" is a genuinely good anti-silent-drop criterion, assuming finding 6's gameability is closed.
- The dependency on TASK-C578-2 (validator must exist first, so kits can be mechanically checked before a human reviews them) is sound ordering.

## Verdict rationale

The task's core internal contradiction — requiring review of "the kit" while neither declaring a dependency on the issue that produces a kit, nor resolving which of two candidate kits is meant — plus borrowing an unconfirmed, differently-scoped, potentially conflicted external relationship from #509, are structural problems that should be fixed before work starts, not discovered mid-execution. Not "should-not-proceed": the licensing and authoring-doc halves are well-formed and can proceed independently. Hence needs-rework rather than a harder verdict.
