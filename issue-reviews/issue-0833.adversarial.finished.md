# Issue #833: TASK-C572-1: the jar is CheerpJ-wrapped and served from static files, and click-to-interactive is timed on the three biggest examples against the 15-second line
- Lens: adversarial
- Date: 2026-08-08
- Reviewer: Claude Sonnet 5
- Verdict: needs-rework

## Summary

#833 is the measurement task under FEAT-C32-1 (#572), itself PF-1 of CAP-32 (#516, the browser-demo capstone). It is well-scoped as an *apparatus* spec — build the wrapper, measure honestly, commit the recipe — and several of its ACs are genuinely solid engineering discipline. But it inherits an unresolved dependency the issue never names: there is no curated "example circuit" corpus to measure against yet, and the go/no-go framework it feeds has no place to record the licensing findings AC-5 asks it to collect. Both defects let the spike report a clean "go" while leaving the real feasibility question — and the real deployability question — unanswered.

## Findings, most severe first

**1. "The three biggest example circuits" have no defined corpus, and the missing dependency on #548 is not declared.**
Confirmed against the checkout: the entire repository contains four `.jls` files total — `test/fixtures/headless-canary-gate.jls` (4K), `test/fixtures/fork-4.6-shiftregister.jls` (8K), `test/fixtures/riscv-sum1to10.jls` (120K), and `riscv/gui/cpu.jls` (12K). None of these live in an "examples" directory and none are curated or captioned for public demo use — they are test fixtures and a developer showcase. The actual curated example set is #548 (FEAT-C27-2, "an Examples menu ships at least ten curated circuits... plus the RV32I showcase"), which is **still open** and unimplemented (confirmed via `issue_read`). #548 AC-2 requires the set to include "the RV32I showcase" — by a wide margin the heaviest circuit the project has or will have. Yet #833's `ordering_after: []` is empty, and so is its parent #572's `ordering_after: []` — even though the capstone one level up, #516 (CAP-32), explicitly lists `ordering_after: ["#511 CAP-27 PF-2 (the curated example library is the content this demo serves)"]`. The dependency is acknowledged at the capstone and silently dropped at the feature and task level. As written, AC-1/AC-2 can be satisfied today by wrapping three arbitrary test fixtures — none representative of what #548/#573 will eventually ship — and reporting a clean pass. That "go" would not transfer to the real corpus (which per #548 AC-2 must include RV32I), reproducing exactly the gap the sibling review of #573 (finding 4, `issue-reviews/issue-0573.adversarial.md`) independently flags on the consuming end. Recommend: add `ordering_after: ["#548"]` (or explicitly declare that this spike is allowed to substitute placeholder circuits and name which ones, with a documented re-run obligation once #548 lands).

**2. AC-5's licensing capture has no consumer or decision rule.**
Quoted: "AC-5: Payload size and any licensing constraint of the wrapping toolchain are recorded alongside the timings — they bear on the verdict as much as the seconds do." That claim is aspirational, not enforced: #572's own go/no-go criterion (its AC-4, and KC-32-1 in #516) is defined purely by the ≤15s timing threshold and interaction fidelity — nowhere does either issue say licensing or payload size can force a no-go. Verified against CheerpJ's own licensing documentation (leaningtech.com / cheerpj.com, checked live): the free Community License's unlimited/unmetered terms are scoped to loading the CheerpJ runtime **from Leaning Technologies' own CDN domain** (`cjrtnc.leaningtech.com`); self-hosting or broader commercial use sits under a separate paid Commercial License (annual/perpetual subscriptions). So AC-5's own data could show that the free tier ties this GPLv3, single-maintainer, volunteer project to a third-party CDN dependency at every page load (in tension with the capstone's "static files only... nothing that can die" framing), or that a self-hosted, CDN-free deployment requires a paid license — and neither fact has anywhere to land. A spike could report "≤15s, go" while the licensing column quietly says "requires a commercial subscription this project cannot fund," and nothing downstream is obligated to treat that as blocking. Recommend #572 (not #833) add an explicit AC: "the recorded licensing constraint is sufficient on its own to force no-go, independent of the timing numbers."

**3. "Machine and network conditions" are unconstrained, making the headline number gameable toward false positives.**
Quoted: "browser, version, machine and network conditions stated" (AC-2). Stating the conditions after the fact is not the same as constraining them beforehand. #516 (CAP-32) names the target audience explicitly: "the K-12/GCSE and browser-student segments." A spike author who runs the timing on a fast development laptop over wired broadband can produce a technically-compliant, fully-documented ≤15s "pass" that says nothing about whether a school Chromebook on classroom WiFi — the actual audience — clears the bar. AC-3's repeatability requirement (twice per circuit) guards against noise on one machine, not against an unrepresentative machine. Recommend the AC name at minimum a floor (e.g., "measured on a mid-tier consumer laptop, not a workstation," or a throttled-network profile in browser devtools) rather than leaving hardware selection entirely to the implementer.

**4. A concrete, checkout-grounded rendering risk is never mentioned: FlatLaf under CheerpJ.**
`ARCHITECTURE.md` records that JLS's default look-and-feel is FlatLaf (`com.formdev.flatlaf.FlatLightLaf`, "recorded 2026-07, #153"), a third-party LaF doing its own custom widget painting, layered on an editor (`SimpleEditor`, "~4k lines; most editor behavior is here") that does substantial custom `paintComponent` work. CheerpJ's AWT/Swing translation is generally strongest against the stock cross-platform (Metal) LaF; custom look-and-feels are a known source of divergence for CheerpJ-wrapped desktop apps. Neither #833 nor #572 mentions verifying rendering against FlatLaf specifically, or falling back to the documented `-Djls.laf=metal` escape hatch (README, "Optional development tools" / ARCHITECTURE.md) if FlatLaf misbehaves in-browser. A spike that wraps the jar unmodified and happens to render "well enough" to eyeball is not the same as one that checked this known risk class.

**5. "Click-to-interactive" is left ambiguous in a direction that favors an optimistic pass.**
#833 deliberately excludes interaction-fidelity testing (that's #572 AC-2, filed as separate scope). But "interactive" in AC-2's own timing metric is then undefined: does the clock stop when the GUI has visually painted, or when input actually responds? CheerpJ's JIT/class-loading warm-up is known to lag first paint on complex apps. Without a stated operational definition (e.g., "time to first successful click-and-response on a toolbar button"), an implementer can legitimately stop the clock at first paint and report a number that looks like a "go" while the app is still not usable for several more seconds — the exact failure mode AC-3's repeatability requirement cannot catch, because it only guards against noise, not against a lenient definition applied consistently.

**6. The task-feature link is informal only, so a partial answer to #572 can pass unnoticed.**
#833's YAML declares `part_of_feature: 572`, but GitHub's own hierarchy disagrees: `get_sub_issues` on #572 returns empty and `get_parent` on #833 returns null — there is no enforced parent/child link. #572 has four ACs beyond what #833 covers (interaction fidelity, the read-only/no-backend framing, and — critically — AC-4's explicit written go/no-go with a *ranked* fallback if CheerpJ fails). If #833 is closed as "done" once its own five ACs are met, nothing in the tracker prevents the impression that the feasibility question is answered when only the timing half of it is.

## What's solid

- AC-1/AC-5's "no backend, static files only" framing is consistent with #572 AC-5 and capstone #516 AC-3 — no scope creep here, correctly bounded to a spike.
- AC-3 (repeat each measurement twice, record both figures) is genuine, cheap protection against a single lucky/unlucky run — better discipline than several sibling issues in this filing batch.
- AC-4 (commit the wrapper build steps as a script/workflow) matches this project's established pattern of one build recipe used both locally and by CI (README: "`scripts/build-installer.sh` is the single recipe used both locally and by CI") and correctly rules out a spike that exists only on the author's disk.
- The task correctly declines to make the go/no-go call itself, leaving that to #572 — a clean separation of "measure" from "decide" in principle, even though finding 2 shows the decision framework it feeds is incomplete.

## Recommendation

Do not start #833 until either #548 lands (so "three biggest example circuits" has a real referent) or the issue names, in writing, which stand-in circuits are acceptable and commits to re-running the spike once #548 ships. Add an explicit decision rule to #572 for what licensing/payload findings from AC-5 do to the verdict, since AC-5 as worded promises weight it has no mechanism to apply. Tighten "machine and network conditions" to at least a floor consistent with the capstone's named audience, and give "click-to-interactive" an operational definition before any number gets treated as authoritative.
