# Issue #854: TASK-C579-4: the store page is a shop window — description and three screenshots from the shared set — and the per-release review cost is written down against the kill threshold
- Lens: visionary
- Date: 2026-08-08
- Reviewer: Claude Opus 5
- Verdict: endorse-with-reframing

## What this issue is really for

Two unrelated things wear one task number. The first is **desktop metadata**: an
AppStream metainfo document carrying JLS's name, summary, description and
screenshots. The second is **channel accounting**: a per-cycle maintainer-week
figure checked against KC-34-1. Both are worth doing. Neither is really a
Flathub sub-task, and filing them as one makes the smaller channel own two
project-wide assets.

## The seam is cut in the wrong place

`docs/standards-adoption/10-desktop-and-housekeeping.md` already contains a
finished, eight-step implementation plan for exactly the artifact AC-1/AC-2
demand: `resources/packaging/io.github.anadon.JLS.metainfo.xml.in`, rendered by
a `render_metainfo()` helper next to `package()` in `scripts/build-installer.sh`,
installed to `/usr/share/metainfo/io.github.anadon.JLS.metainfo.xml` in the deb
and the AppDir, validated by `appstreamcli validate --pedantic` in CI, and
golden-tested by a new `test/jls/PackagingMetadataTest.java` that also pins the
`<id>` against `--mac-package-identifier` and the `<mediatype>` against
`resources/packaging/jls-association-linux.properties`. That plan is scoped to
the **five existing installer legs** and is deliberately Flathub-independent.
It has no owning issue — searching the tracker for AppStream returns only #854
and #852. So the one durable, cross-channel piece of desktop metadata JLS lacks
currently exists only as a bullet inside a Flatpak task.

The consequence is sharp and, I think, decisive: **AC-5 can kill this issue's
own AC-1 and AC-2.** If the recorded cost exceeds 0.5 mw and the channel is
dropped, the metainfo file, its CI validation and its golden test go out with
the Flatpak manifest, and the deb, rpm, AppImage, the flake's `jls.desktop`, and
any future nixpkgs/PPA packager keep nothing. A capstone-level asset must not be
disposable as a side effect of one channel's arithmetic.

**Reframing (the one I would build):** file the metainfo work as its own task
under CAP-34 or CAP-35, sized ~1.5 mw per the standards doc's own estimate, with
AC-2's CI validation attached to it. Then #854 shrinks to what is genuinely
Flathub-specific — the manifest consuming that already-shipped file, plus the
screenshot URLs — and becomes perhaps 0.2 mw instead of 0.5. Every other Linux
channel gains discoverability metadata for free, and the standards doc's own
observation applies: the AppImage path (AppImageHub, AppImage-aware integration
daemons) is "the one real, immediate consumer," available without fighting a
sandbox at all.

## The description has five copies and no source

AC-1 says "the shared project description" as though a shared one exists. Today
the blurb lives in `pom.xml` `<description>`, in `$DESCRIPTION` passed to
jpackage (`scripts/build-installer.sh:183`), in `APPLICATION_DESCRIPTION` in
`resources/packaging/resource-dir-linux/JLS.desktop:10`, in README ¶1, and — once
CAP-34's other legs land — in a winget `ShortDescription` and a Homebrew cask
`desc`. #854 would add a sixth. The elegant version of this issue is a **product
identity source**: one small file naming id, name, summary, long description,
homepage, categories, MIME type and the screenshot selection, from which the
metainfo, the `.desktop` substitutions, the jpackage flags and the store
manifests are all rendered, with `PackagingMetadataTest` failing when any
consumer drifts. That test is already half-specified in the standards doc (it is
told to assert three files agree that "currently have nothing keeping them in
sync"); extending it to the description text is nearly free once the template
exists. This is CAP-35's thesis — one source, several targets, drift-proofed —
applied to packaging metadata rather than prose, and it is the change that makes
"shared with CAP-27" a mechanism instead of an intention.

## Screenshots: the hard part is the part the issue omits

AC-1 and AC-3 are largely already owned. #796 mandates a committed capture
manifest regenerated by one command on the headless-sway rig; #798 AC-3 names
"the CAP-34 store listings" as a consumer that must draw from the emitted set,
with a check that fails when a consumer references an image outside it. #854
restating "regenerable, not hand-taken" risks a second, weaker mechanism beside
that check. Better: state that the `<screenshots>` block is **generated** from
#586's manifest (entries flagged store-eligible), so "the source of each
recorded" is satisfied by the manifest rather than by prose in a PR description.

What #854 does not mention at all is the thing that will actually cost time.
AppStream `<image>` elements are **URLs** — the spec has no file-relative or
embedded form, and Flathub resolves them at build time over HTTPS. The standards
doc enumerates the options (omit; pinned `raw.githubusercontent.com`; a Pages
site that does not exist). The right long-term answer is CAP-35 PF-2's hosted
versioned manual becoming the canonical screenshot host, with per-release URLs
that the capture pipeline publishes; the right interim answer is pinned raw URLs
emitted by the pipeline rather than typed by hand. Either way this belongs in the
acceptance criteria, because a stale or 404'd screenshot URL is how store
listings actually rot.

Undeclared ordering, while I am here: `ordering_after` names only TASK-C579-3,
but AC-3 makes #796 a hard predecessor of this issue's own criteria.

## The kill rule has a cost term and no benefit term

AC-4/AC-5 ask for maintainer-weeks "in the same place the other channels record
theirs." No such place exists; CAP-34's PF-4 KPI script is where it should live
and is itself unfiled. That is fine as an ordering note but it means AC-4 is
asking a task to write into a ledger nobody has built.

The larger problem is the rule's shape. KC-34-1 is cost-only: >0.5 mw per cycle
and the channel dies. But CAP-34's whole point is that release-asset downloads
become the adoption KPI — a benefit measure the project is about to start
collecting — and Flathub is the one channel that publishes per-app install
counts through a public API, so its benefit term is nearly free to obtain. A
cost-only threshold will preferentially kill the channel that succeeds: more
users mean more attributable triage, review churn and breakage reports. I am
**explicitly disregarding AC-5's absolute 0.5 mw threshold as the decision
rule** and proposing instead that the ledger record both terms and that the
recommendation be written in maintainer-weeks per thousand installs, compared
across channels. The threshold's home is KC-34-1 in #518, so the correct move
from #854 is a comment upward proposing the amendment, not a unilateral rewrite
here. What #854 should keep verbatim is its best sentence: dropping is a
legitimate outcome and silence is not.

## The unrecorded reversal

The same standards document says, in a headline table, **"Do not pursue
Flathub,"** and gives three reasons that are still true: a second packaging
pipeline with none of the `SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH` / double-build reproducibility
plumbing carrying over; a sandbox at odds with JLS's actual job — the `.jls~`
checkpoint written beside the user's file (`src/jls/edit/Editor.java`,
`src/jls/edit/SimpleEditor.java`) and a `flatpak run` prefix that invalidates
every command line in README.md and `docs/batch-interface.md`; and a
contradiction of the recorded single-jar deployment model. It ends with "Record
the decline the way `ARCHITECTURE.md` § Recorded decisions records the others."
CAP-34 then reversed the verdict on #510's evidence — plausibly correctly — but
`ARCHITECTURE.md` § "Recorded decisions" carries neither the decline nor the
reversal, and nothing in #579 or its four tasks references the research doc that
said no.

JLS's most distinctive asset is not its simulator; it is that its reasoning is
written down and revisitable — i18n, help delivery, FlatLaf, the plugin trust
boundary all carry rationale plus a reopen trigger. Four open tasks that
silently overrule a research verdict pull against that. The cheapest fix is one
paragraph in `ARCHITECTURE.md` recording the reversal with its evidence (#510's
install score, the incumbent's four channels) and its mitigations (#852's
portal-mediated association and minimal permissions; #849's checksum-pinned wrap
of the attested artifact, which answers the "second pipeline" objection).
Because #854 is the task that supplies the *only* remaining justification for the
channel — discoverability — it is the natural place to demand that record, and
the decision it would inform is precisely AC-5's.

## Verdict

Endorse the outcome; reframe the ownership. Extract the AppStream metainfo (and
its CI validation and golden test) into a channel-independent task so it cannot
be killed by AC-5; generate the description and screenshots from a single
product-identity source and #586's manifest rather than copying them; name the
screenshot hosting decision as an acceptance criterion; and push the kill rule
upward to become cost-per-install rather than cost alone. What remains here — a
Flatpak manifest pointing at metadata that already exists — is small, correct,
and survives its own kill criterion.
