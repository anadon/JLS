# Issue #532: FEAT-C23-3: "why did this change?" walks any transition back through the scheduled-event graph and shows the reconvergent paths with their unequal accumulated per-gate delays
- Lens: adversarial
- Date: 2026-08-08
- Reviewer: Claude Sonnet 5
- Verdict: needs-rework

## Findings, most severe first

### 1. CRITICAL — the outcome statement misdescribes the codebase: there is no producer-to-consumer relation to "retain," and the stated granularity constraint cannot support AC-2 as worded
The Outcome section claims: *"the simulator already computes the per-event schedule and per-element `propDelay` and then discards the producer-to-consumer relation the moment an event fires; this feature retains that relation."* That implies the linkage exists transiently and merely needs capturing before GC. It does not exist at all, transiently or otherwise:

- `SimEvent.PinChanged` is a zero-field record (`src/jls/sim/SimEvent.java:30-31`): `public record PinChanged() implements Payload {}`. Every `PinChanged` instance is equal to every other (confirmed by `SimEvent.equals`, `SimEvent.java:161-172`, which delegates to `todo.equals(oth.todo)`).
- The only site that posts `PinChanged` to a downstream element, `WireNet.java:507-508` (`sim.post(new SimEvent(now, (Reacts) element, new SimEvent.PinChanged()))`), carries no reference to the upstream event, wire, or pin that changed.
- `Reacts.react(long now, Simulator sim, SimEvent.Payload todo)` (`src/jls/sim/Reacts.java:27-28`) gives the reacting element nothing but "some input changed, go re-read all your pins" — there is no per-pin identity in the call at all.

So a "producer-to-consumer relation" must be *synthesized*, not retained — a materially harder task than the issue's framing suggests, and the mw band (4-6, copied from CAP-23's own per-PF table — see finding 6) was never re-derived against this fact.

Worse, the issue's own hedge in Boundary/reference notes — *"Granularity follows whatever the scheduler already keys on — no synthetic granularity the engine does not have"* — is honest, but it also concedes the problem: what the scheduler "keys on" for a `PinChanged` event is `(time, element, PinChanged())`, which does not distinguish *which* upstream input pin, wire, or path caused the reaction, even for a two-input gate sitting at the reconvergence point the whole issue is built around. AC-2 nonetheless promises *"Reconvergent fan-in renders as distinct paths with per-path accumulated delay totals"* — a per-path claim the current event keying cannot ground without additional design (e.g., per-pin event payloads or a topology-based backward walk) that this issue neither specifies nor budgets for.
**Recommendation:** Either (a) specify the actual mechanism for capturing producer identity (e.g., a "currently reacting event" context on `Simulator` that tags every `post()` call made during a `react()` with a parent pointer, plus a design for resolving *which* input pin was decisive when an element has more than one), and re-cost against that added mechanism, or (b) scope AC-1/AC-2 down to element-level granularity explicitly and drop or soften the "distinct reconvergent paths" claim to what element-level attribution can actually support.

### 2. HIGH — filed in acknowledged, unresolved violation of the maintainer's own concurrently-stated planning ratchet
#508 (created 2026-08-03T23:27:16Z, same author) states under Process findings: *"Planning ratchet: no new tier:feature/tier:task until two capstones close."* #532 (`tier:feature`) was filed roughly 3.5 hours later (2026-08-04T03:11:08Z) with no capstone closures in between — CAP-23 (#504) itself is still open. This is not a reviewer inference: the filer's own comment on #504 (2026-08-04T03:12:12Z) says so directly under "Contradictions / tensions found": *"#508 planning ratchet vs today's directive: #508 recommends 'no new tier:feature/tier:task until two capstones close'; the complete-coverage directive overrides it for this filing pass — noting the conflict rather than resolving it."* An issue that documents, at filing time, an unresolved conflict with the project's own just-stated governance decision is a process defect independent of the feature's technical merits.
**Recommendation:** Either retroactively justify the override in #508 itself (a comment explaining why coverage-completeness outranks the ratchet for this one pass) or hold #532 until the ratchet's precondition (two capstone closures) is met, per #508's own text.

### 3. MEDIUM — AC-5's "stable seam" guarantee is unilateral: #476 does not commit to providing one, and #532's own machine block omits the dependency it relies on
AC-5 requires: *"The inspector reads a stable seam over the event structures, not the concrete queues, so TASK-0063's (#476) calendar-queue replacement does not break it."* Checked against #476 (TASK-0063) directly: its §7.4 "Internal interfaces provided — public" contract for `jls.sim.CalendarQueue` is `add/poll/peek/isEmpty/size/clear` only — no observer hook, no event-stream tap, nothing an external cause-chain retainer could attach to. #476's own text nowhere mentions #532, PF-3, or a seam obligation; the only cross-reference is #504's capstone-level note that #476 "touches the event structures PF-3 walks," which is descriptive, not a contract. Meanwhile #532's own machine block (`ordering_after: [FEAT-C23-2]`) does not list #476 at all, even though the Boundary notes' prose treats landing after #476's seam as load-bearing ("Per #508, this lands after #476's seam"). A field other tooling in this corpus treats as authoritative (see #504's mermaid graph, #476's own YAML block) is silently incomplete here.
**Recommendation:** Add #476 to `ordering_after` (or an explicit `blocked_by`), and get a REPLAN comment on #476 acknowledging the seam obligation before #532 is implemented — otherwise whichever of the two lands second inherits an unplanned retrofit.

### 4. MEDIUM — no thread-safety design for a buffer that must be written from the sim thread and read from the EDT
ARCHITECTURE.md's Threading model section is explicit: *"Interactive simulation runs on a dedicated thread (the 'Runner' thread...). Control state shared between the EDT and the sim thread... is `volatile`... UI work initiated from the sim thread is routed through `SwingUtilities.invokeLater`... Follow this discipline for any new sim-thread → UI interaction."* The ring buffer AC-4 requires (populated during every `react()`/`post()` on the Runner thread, read and rendered by the inspector on the EDT when open) is exactly this pattern, yet #532 says nothing about synchronization, visibility, or `invokeLater` routing for the buffer — not in the acceptance criteria, not in the boundary notes. AC-4's "zero cost when closed" is stated as a performance ratchet but says nothing about correctness under concurrent access when open.
**Recommendation:** Add an explicit criterion (or inherit CAP-23's AC-5 `ChronogramClosedCostTest` pattern) covering the buffer's cross-thread contract, naming the same discipline ARCHITECTURE.md already mandates.

### 5. MEDIUM — AC #4's "re-scope... before building further" is a kill-switch dressed as a testable acceptance criterion
AC-4 reads: *"if bounded retention cannot keep the chain for the very glitch the analyzer caught, re-scope to trigger-window retention per KC-23-2 before building further."* This is not falsifiable as an acceptance criterion — there is no named test that fails if retention silently drops the seeded glitch's chain, and "before building further" gates nothing that a merge process would check. As worded, a PR could ship a ring buffer that loses the target glitch's chain, add a comment citing KC-23-2 and "re-scoping" in prose, and never be blocked by CI or review from claiming AC-4 "addressed." Contrast with AC-3, which is properly test-gated (`CauseChainCompletenessTest`, "invariant-checked, not eyeballed").
**Recommendation:** Fold the KC-23-2 condition into `CauseChainCompletenessTest` itself (e.g., the test must fail loudly, not silently degrade, if the seeded glitch's chain is incomplete under the committed buffer size) rather than leaving it as a prose-only escape hatch.

### 6. LOW — `band_mw: 4-6` is copied verbatim from CAP-23's own per-PF costing, not independently derived
CAP-23 (#504) Cost section: *"PF-3: 4–6 mw (event-graph retention + rendering; the retention ring buffer is the design risk)."* #532's machine block carries the identical `4-6` band. Consistent with the corpus-wide pattern noted by #508 itself ("3 of 4 sampled capstones had bands below their own row sums" — a related but not identical finding), and worth flagging here because finding 1 shows the actual retention problem is harder than "retain a relation that already exists," which is exactly the kind of gap a re-derived estimate would surface.
**Recommendation:** Re-cost PF-3 once the causal-attribution mechanism (finding 1) is actually designed, rather than carrying the capstone-level placeholder forward unchanged.

### 7. LOW — AC-3's "seeded two-path glitch" fixture is unpinned
Unlike #476's P3, which names three specific ordering-hazard fixtures (cross-coupled latch, tri-state bus, zero-delay ring) that must all appear in the corpus, AC-3 here just says "a seeded two-path glitch" with no seed value, no named fixture file, and no statement of whether the two paths' delays are pinned. A loosely specified fixture that can be adjusted until the test passes is a weaker guarantee than the rest of this issue's invariant-testing rhetoric ("invariant-checked, not eyeballed") aims for.
**Recommendation:** Name the fixture circuit (or its seed) here, matching #476's practice for its own ordering-hazard corpus.

- The granularity hedge ("no synthetic granularity the engine does not have") shows real self-awareness of the data-model limit identified in finding 1, even though it does not resolve the tension with AC-2.

## Verdict rationale
The feature concept is coherent and the capstone-level scoping is honest in places, but as filed it rests on a factually wrong premise about what the event model currently provides (finding 1 — this is not a nitpick: it changes the difficulty and honesty of AC-2's "distinct reconvergent paths" claim), depends on a seam (#476) that the depended-on issue does not commit to and that #532's own machine block doesn't even list, is silent on a cross-thread contract ARCHITECTURE.md explicitly requires for this exact shape of interaction, and was filed against the maintainer's own concurrently-recorded planning ratchet with the conflict left open rather than resolved. None of this is fatal to the concept, but none of it should be discovered mid-implementation either — hence needs-rework rather than should-not-proceed.
