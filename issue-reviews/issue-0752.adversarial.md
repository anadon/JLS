## Findings, most severe first

**1. The same ordering-cycle defect the maintainer already found and fixed on #577/#509 today is present here, uncorrected.**
Trace the graph: #752 (`ordering_after: ["TASK-C575-3", 578]`) will not start
until #578 (FEAT-C33-4) is substantively done. But #578's own AC-1 ("kit
layout and metadata specified in tree ... derived from the worked Donzellini
pack") is owned by TASK-C578-1 (#767), whose review on disk states plainly:
"derived from the worked Donzellini pack (#575) rather than invented ahead of
it" — i.e., #767 needs #575's pack to exist first. And #575's own AC-5
("content provenance is clean and auditable ... under the kit content
license") is exactly what #752 delivers. So: #752 waits on #578 → #578's AC-1
(#767) waits on #575 → #575's AC-5 is #752. That is a cycle, structurally
identical to the one the maintainer found and corrected via the
2026-08-08 "ORDERING CORRECTION" comment on #577 today ("this issue and #509
form a dependency cycle; the edge is narrowed to break it"). #752 has zero
comments — it has not received the equivalent fix. Recommendation: split
`ordering_after` by criterion the same way #577's correction did (e.g., "kit
layout convention" (#767) is not required before AC-1/AC-2 of this issue, only
the specific content-license value (#772) is), so #752 isn't blocked on a
feature that is partly blocked on #752's own parent.

**2. AC-1/AC-2's "original, no DEEDS asset copied" claim is unverifiable as specified, and it rests on self-attestation with no comparison material.**
The issue's own framing states the stakes: "the boundary between 'maps to
chapter 7' and 'reproduces chapter 7' has to be written down, not assumed."
But the only mechanism specified is "A provenance statement in the pack
records, per lab, that its prose and circuits are original, and names the
author" — i.e., a first-person claim, not an audit. DEEDS is proprietary
courseware (the issue itself calls the `.pbs` files "closed"); nothing in
this repo, and nothing #752 proposes to add, gives anyone a copy of DEEDS
content to diff the labs against. "The check confirms no shipped lab file
carries third-party content" (AC-4) checks for the *presence of a license
record*, not for actual non-copying — a lab could paraphrase DEEDS prose
closely and the check would still pass as long as someone wrote "original"
in the provenance file. Recommendation: either scope AC-1/AC-2 explicitly as
"a recorded attestation, not an independent originality audit" (so nobody
downstream treats it as more than it is), or add a concrete verification step
(e.g., named non-author reviewer with DEEDS familiarity signs off per lab,
mirroring TASK-C575-4's non-author review mechanism at #751).

**3. Compounding #2: every artifact in this issue cluster is machine-generated, and AC-1's "names the author" doesn't address AI-authorship/copyright-eligibility risk for the license grant in AC-3.**
Every boundary-note comment fetched on #575/#577/#578 during this review ends
with "_Generated by Claude Code_." If the labs' prose and circuits are
authored the same way (a reasonable expectation given the rest of this task
cluster's process), "names the author" either names a human who did not
author the words, or names an AI system — and copyright offices in multiple
jurisdictions decline to register works without a human author, which
directly undermines AC-3 ("the pack carries the kit content license"): a
license only conveys rights the licensor actually holds. The issue is silent
on this. Recommendation: state explicitly who the legal author of record is
(the maintainer, adopting the AI-drafted text as their own work) before AC-3
is treated as satisfied.

**4. AC-4's "check" is undefined tooling with no owner, format, or CI wiring, and its boundary against #578's own validator (#769) is unstated.**
"A check confirms no shipped lab file carries third-party content without an
accompanying license record" names no artifact (script? JUnit test? CI step?)
and no schema for what "an accompanying license record" looks like per file.
Meanwhile TASK-C578-2 (#769) is already building "a validator [that] checks a
kit directory against the convention and reports what is missing or
malformed by name; CI runs it over every shipped kit" — a check over exactly
this pack. #752 doesn't say whether its AC-4 check is a new one-off, or a
field the #769 validator is expected to also enforce. Left unstated, this
check could ship as a five-line grep that always passes (nothing is ever
"flagged" as third-party) and technically satisfy the letter of AC-4.
Recommendation: name where this check lives (dedicated test vs. folded into
#769's validator) and what a failure looks like.

**5. No ordering against #751 (TASK-C575-4, the review process that can *pull* labs) creates a stale-provenance risk.**
#751 and #752 are siblings that both key only off #748 (`ordering_after:
["TASK-C575-3"]`); neither orders after the other. #751's whole point is that
"a lab that fails two consecutive reviews is pulled from the pack" — i.e.,
the lab roster can still shrink after #748 lands. If #752's per-lab
provenance statement and the AC-4 check run before #751 finishes, they cover
labs that may later be pulled (stale statement, or the check silently passing
over content that no longer ships); if #752 runs after #751, it implicitly
depends on it without saying so. Recommendation: add #751 to `ordering_after`
explicitly, matching the precedent the maintainer set on #767 (ordered after
both a spec task and 575) and on the #577 correction (splitting edges by
criterion rather than leaving an implicit assumption).

**6. Minor: "the kit content license defined by #578" overstates what #578 itself does.**
#578 (FEAT-C33-4) requires *that* a license exists (its own AC-4: "Kit
content carries a stated open license... distinct from the code license") but
does not itself choose one — that concrete choice is TASK-C578-3 (#772),
which per its own on-disk review is gated behind #769 and #509 and is not
named anywhere in #752. Pointing `ordering_after` at the coarse feature #578
rather than the specific task #772 is the same granularity problem flagged
in finding #1. Recommendation: cite #772 by number, not just #578.

**7. Minor: effort band looks light for what AC-4 actually asks for.**
`band_mw: 0.25-0.5` is the smallest of all five TASK-C575-* tasks (siblings
run 0.5–1.5), yet AC-4 asks for new, presumably-automated tooling ("a check
confirms...") in addition to writing prose statements — closer in kind to
the validator work banded at 0.5–1 for #769. Recommendation: re-band once
AC-4's tooling is scoped, or explicitly confine AC-4 to a manual/documented
checklist rather than automation if the band is meant to stay this small.

## What's solid

- The "no `.pbs` file or DEEDS-derived asset is present in the tree" clause
  of AC-2 is genuinely concrete and mechanically testable (a file-presence
  grep/glob), unlike the vaguer "no third-party content" language in AC-4.
- Distinguishing a kit content license from JLS's GPLv3 code license (AC-3)
  is a real and necessary distinction — `README.md`'s "License and
  provenance" section covers only code, and lab prose/circuits are a
  different kind of asset with a different natural license family (e.g.
  CC-BY variants), so this can't just inherit the existing `LICENSE` file.
- The Boundary section's scoping-out of #578 (general policy) and #577
  (separate CSE 260M corpus) is consistent with, and correctly reflects, the
  maintainer's own boundary-note adjudication comments on #575/#577/#578.
