## Findings, most severe first

**1. "Non-author" is never defined to exclude another AI agent, which guts the stated purpose.**
The Outcome states the point of this task in one sentence: "without it the
incentive is to declare eight labs and let two of them be unusable." But the
only qualifying bar for the reviewer is "a non-author" — not "a human," not
"someone approximating the target student's background." Every process
artifact touched while researching this cluster (the #575 boundary comment,
the #767/#752 reviews) is signed "_Generated by Claude Code_," i.e. this
project's own working method already uses AI agents to produce and adjudicate
its planning documents. Nothing in #751 stops the "non-author completion"
from being performed by a second AI session that loads the lab prose, walks
through it, and reports a fabricated or optimistic elapsed time — which is
exactly the gameable failure mode the issue exists to prevent, just moved one
level up. Recommendation: state explicitly that the reviewer must be a human
with no access to the lab's answer key/reference solution, and ideally one
whose background approximates the target student (see #2).

**2. n=1 human sample, with no skill-calibration requirement, is weak evidence for a "budget" meant to hold across a whole class.**
AC-2 requires only "at least one recorded non-author completion... within its
stated time budget." A single reviewer's elapsed time is a poor proxy for
how a Donzellini-course undergraduate will perform — a strong reviewer
(e.g., a JLS contributor already fluent with the editor) will systematically
undershoot real student time, letting an over-optimistic budget pass on one
lucky run. The issue never requires the reviewer to be unfamiliar with JLS,
or requires more than one sample, yet the consequence of a pass is "ship as
declared" and the consequence of two fails is "pull the lab" — a high-stakes
binary resting on very thin data. Recommendation: either require the
reviewer profile to be stated per review (tool familiarity, domain
background) so a downstream reader can weigh the sample, or raise the bar to
more than one passing completion before a budget is trusted.

**3. AC-3 ("what counts as a failure") is circular and self-satisfying — the acceptance criterion can pass with a protocol that makes failure nearly impossible.**
"A written review protocol specifies what a non-author does, what they
record, and what counts as a failure" is satisfied by writing *any*
definition, including a deliberately toothless one (e.g., "failure = time
exceeds budget by 500%"). The issue supplies no floor — no reference
threshold like "budget is wrong if actual time exceeds it by more than
25%," no requirement that the threshold be justified. As written, a
protocol document that defines "failure" so loosely that no lab ever fails
twice would still tick this checkbox, defeating the stated Outcome ("keeps
the count honest") while satisfying the letter of the AC. Recommendation:
either put a numeric tolerance in the issue itself (e.g., budget is
considered wrong past N% overrun) or require the protocol's threshold to be
justified in the same written document, and have a second party (not the
protocol's author) sign off on the threshold before labs are reviewed
against it.

**4. Deferred-to-the-end review sequencing creates a late-stage rework/undershoot risk against #575's own AC-1 (≥8 labs).**
`ordering_after: ["TASK-C575-3"]` means every lab across all three content
tasks (#744, #746, #748) is written before any non-author review happens.
#748's own AC (the datapath labs, "bringing the shipped total to at least
eight labs") is written assuming the earlier labs already survived review —
but they haven't been reviewed yet at that point, since #751 hasn't run.
If review then pulls two of the earlier (#744/#746) labs on grounds of
failing twice, the pack can drop below #575 AC-1's "at least 8 labs" floor
*after* #748 has already been declared complete, with no stated fallback
(write a replacement lab? re-open #748? accept fewer than 8?). Running
review incrementally as each of #744/#746/#748 lands would catch this before
downstream work (chapter-coverage tables, CI wiring, #578's worked-instance
claim) is built on labs that later get pulled. Recommendation: either order
per-content-task reviews after each of #744/#746/#748 individually, or add
an explicit contingency AC for what happens if pulled labs drop the count
below 8.

**5. No coordination with #578's kit-convention/validator work for where "recorded time in tree" lives.**
AC-2 requires "the recorded time in tree" but #751's `ordering_after` never
references #578 (FEAT-C33-4) or its layout-defining subtask, even though
#578 AC-1 requires "the kit layout and metadata... specified in tree" and
AC-2 requires a validator that "reports what is missing or malformed by
name." If #751's review records are invented independently of that schema,
they risk exactly the retrofit problem the sibling review on #752
(finding #1) already identified for this same task cluster: a kit-convention
task and a content/process task each assuming they can proceed independently
when their artifacts must actually interlock. Recommendation: name where the
review record lives relative to #578's kit layout (a `reviews/` or
`schedule`/`rubric` slot in the convention), or explicitly order after the
relevant #578 subtask.

**6. "KC-33-2" is cited as binding authority for the core rule (two-fails-pulled, not padded) but is not a locatable artifact anywhere in this repository.**
Both #751's Outcome ("without it the incentive is to declare eight labs and
let two of them be unusable (KC-33-2)") and its AC-3 lean on "KC-33-2" as if
it were a settled, referenceable decision. A repo-wide grep for `KC-33`
(case-insensitive) returns zero matches outside this issue text itself —
there is no doc, comment, or other issue in tree that defines what KC-33-2
says or where it was decided. For a task whose entire point is auditable
rigor ("without it the incentive is to declare eight labs and let two of
them be unusable"), citing an unlocatable identifier as its own justification
is a credibility gap: a reviewer cannot check whether #751 correctly
implements KC-33-2 or subtly narrows/widens it. Recommendation: link or
inline the KC-33-2 source, or drop the citation and state the rule as this
issue's own decision.

**7. Effort band plausibly undersized relative to the actual work implied.**
`band_mw: 0.5-1` covers not just writing the review protocol but *performing*
at least one full non-author completion of every shipped lab (≥8, per #575
AC-1) plus a second completion for any that fail once, plus updating time
budgets where wrong (AC-4) and writing up each result "in tree." Sibling task
bands for comparable content work run 1–1.5 mw (#744, #746, #748) for
*writing* 2–3 labs each; #751 asks one person to *play through* the entire
finished pack (potentially 16+ timed sessions in the worst case) for less
band than any single content task. `band_mw` is itself undefined anywhere in
tree (also true across the sibling cluster), so this can't be checked against
a stated unit, but the relative sizing against siblings is suspect on its
face. Recommendation: re-band once the expected number of review sessions is
scoped, or cap the worst-case reviewer burden explicitly (e.g., a lab that
fails once is pulled immediately rather than requiring a second full
completion).
