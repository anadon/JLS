## What this task is actually for

Read past the numbers and #623 is the *gate* of FEAT-059 (#490), not a test-writing
chore. #490 sequences this feature last of CAP-18's three rungs precisely because it
is the only rung that commits unwithdrawable public surface (a frozen `SaveTags` row,
a K9 palette obligation), and K18-1 — evaluated *here*, per criterion 4 — is the stop
condition protecting that surface. So the real question is not "are these seven
voltages right." It is: **does this task give the programme a gate that can fire?**

It does not, and that is the finding.

## The gate is wired to a switch that is bolted open

Criterion 1 asks for two assertions: byte-identical to a golden, and agreement with
"the closed-form lattice" to 1e-12. Criterion 3 then admits, honestly, that because
the implementation *is* the closed-form superposition (TASK-C490-1 #618 AC-1), the
second assertion catches transcription, regeneration and float-ordering only. Put the
two together: the corpus is generated by `f`, the check evaluates `f`, and 1e-12 is
the distance from `f(x)` to `f(x)`. K18-1's trigger condition — "the corpus disagrees
with the lattice beyond 1e-12" — is reachable only by a typo in a duplicated formula.
The title's phrase "two independent assertions" is contradicted by criterion 3 three
paragraphs later.

This is checkable in the project's own terms. #490 invariant 7 requires PIT mutation
thresholds. Two assertions over the same computation kill exactly the same mutant set:
flip the sign in `Gamma_s` and both fail; neither adds a single point of mutation
score over the other. The second assertion is, by the project's own quality gate,
free of information.

Meanwhile the repo already states the correct doctrine and #623 does not cite it.
`test/jls/ElementSimulationGoldenTest.java:32-35`:

> Expected values are computed independently of the implementation (issue #158
> section 10) … never by running the simulator and copying what it printed.

A golden of an analytically-defined waveform, checked against that same analytic
definition, is the copying pattern with an extra file.

## Alternative A — build an oracle that can fail (the primary reframe)

Replace criterion 1's second assertion with checks that are *derivable without
re-implementing the superposition*. All three are cheap and all three sit inside the
task's 0.5-1 mw band:

1. **Metamorphic relations.** These need no second implementation and no golden:
   - scale invariance: `V(t; a·V_src, Z0·c, R_s·c, R_L·c, T_d·b) = a · V(t/b; …)`;
   - `R_s = Z0` ⇒ exactly one series term and a flat `V_0` (termination degeneracy);
   - `R_L = Z0` ⇒ `Gamma_L = 0`, far end flat at 2.750 V, ring absent;
   - end-swap symmetry: exchanging `R_s`/`R_L` maps the near-end trace onto the
     far-end trace under the stated relabelling.
   A wrong `Gamma` sign or a swapped reflection coefficient survives criterion 1 as
   written and dies here.
2. **Structural/causal properties of the waveform**, asserted rather than tabulated:
   far-end voltage is *identically* zero for `t < T_d`; the waveform is piecewise
   linear with breakpoints only at `(2k+1)T_d` and `(2k+1)T_d + t_r`; `|V_k − V_final|`
   contracts by exactly `|Gamma_s|` per round trip; and `|V| ≤ V_src(1+|Gamma_L|)/(1−|Gamma_s Gamma_L|)`.
   These are the assumptions the closed form rests on; asserting them is what makes a
   modelling error visible.
3. **The rejected lumped ladder, promoted from prose to executable evidence.** #490
   rejects an RLGC ladder as the *model* on measured ngspice data (+19.60% at N=25
   down to +4.47% at N=440, converging as 1/N). That measurement lives only in an
   issue body — nothing in the tree re-checks it, and ARCHITECTURE.md's whole
   discipline is "specifics carry a `file`/method anchor so you can verify rather than
   trust." A ~40-line in-test ladder run at N = 25/55/110/220/440, asserting monotone
   convergence toward the closed form and the recorded overshoot decay, is a genuinely
   *different algorithm* reaching the same answer. That is the independent check
   criterion 3 says does not exist here — and it simultaneously pins the
   element-versus-ladder decision so it cannot rot.

The repo also has a stronger seam than any of these and #623 never looks at it:
`proofs/SpatialIndexCorrectness.agda` plus `test/jls/ProofBridgeTest.java`, where an
idealised model is machine-checked and *each of its assumptions* is pinned by a named
test. A lossless line with resistive terminations driven by a piecewise-linear source
is exactly the kind of eight-line theorem that pattern was built for. If a second
proof is ever worth writing in this project, the geometric superposition and its
truncation bound `N = ceil(log tol / log|Gamma_s Gamma_L|)` are the best candidate in
the tree.

## Alternative B — the golden should be a VCD real variable, not an invented corpus

TASK-C490-2 (#620) invents a "headless CSV form" and #623 freezes a corpus in it. But
IEEE 1364-2001 VCD, which `docs/batch-interface.md` §4 already specifies and
`test/jls/VcdExportGoldenTest.java` already pins, has a real-variable type (`$var real
1 ! …`, `r5.5 !`) that GTKWave and Surfer — both named in README — read natively. The
reflection lab's headless artifact should be a VCD real var, and #623's golden should
be a new case in the existing `VcdExportGoldenTest`, not a bespoke format with a
bespoke corpus.

This is not decoration. `docs/batch-interface.md` §4.3 says "JLS values are two-state
plus high impedance," and `VcdExportGoldenTest.vcdIsStructurallyWellFormedAndTwoStatePlusHiZ`
enforces `$var wire` with values in {0,1,z}. A real-valued signal collides with that
pin head-on. **#623 is the task where that collision surfaces and it is not mentioned
anywhere in the issue.** Reframing the golden as a VCD-real golden turns the collision
into the task's central deliverable — widening the documented value domain, once,
deliberately, with the existing structural test amended — instead of leaving it to
detonate under whoever executes #620 or #625.

## Arithmetic I checked, and one number that is wrong

The lattice `V_k = 3.3(1 − (−2/3)^k)` reproduces 5.5000, 1.8333, 4.2778, 2.6481,
3.7346, 3.0103, 3.4931 exactly. Good.

**4.3675 V is not a term of that lattice and cannot be checked by it.** I get it from
a *different* closed form — superposed clamped ramps, `V(t) = 5.5·Σ_k (−2/3)^k r(t −
(2k+1)·345.6 ps)` — peaking at `t = T_d + t_r`: `5.5·(1 − (2/3)(308.8/1000)) = 4.3677 V`.
Criterion 1 says "the closed-form lattice" as if there were one oracle; there are two,
and criterion 2's whole lesson lives in the one criterion 1 does not name.

**Criterion 2's third figure looks wrong.** Same ramp superposition at `t_r = 5 ns`,
peak again at `t = T_d + t_r = 5345.6 ps`, eight active terms:
`1 − 0.5745 + 0.3216 − 0.1734 + 0.0883 − 0.0407 + 0.0150 − 0.0019 = 0.6344`, so
`V = 5.5 × 0.6344 = 3.489 V = 105.7%` of the rail — not "under 105%". A test written
to the issue as filed fails, and under criterion 4 that failure reads as K18-1 firing
and triggers a REPLAN on #490 and #313 for a mis-stated threshold. Re-derive it; and
better, state the third case as the **property** it is meant to express — peak
overshoot strictly decreasing in `t_r`, tending to 100% as `t_r/2T_d → ∞` — which is
robust to ramp shape in a way a 105% magic number is not.

**The 3.1914 V "supersession" is under-investigated.** "No integer k" is true but
uninformative. `3.1914/3.3 = 0.96709`, and `V_8 = 3.3(1 − (2/3)^8) = 3.17124` — 0.64%
away. A number that close to a real lattice term is far more likely a later sample, a
ramp-smeared plateau, or a lossy run than an invention. Criterion 5 asks for the value
to be recorded as superseded; what a reader actually needs recorded is its
**provenance**, or an explicit "provenance unknown, nearest lattice term is V_8 =
3.1712." Declaring a disagreeing measurement superseded by the model it disagrees with
is the exact move #490 forbids in criterion 1's "the golden may never be regenerated
to make the analytic check pass."

## What I would keep

The refusal to regenerate the golden to satisfy the analytic check; the insistence
that the edge-rate collapse be a test rather than a claim; and criterion 3's honesty
about what the second assertion buys. That last one is the best sentence in the issue
— it is also, followed to its conclusion, the reason to rewrite the criteria above it.

## Disregarded acceptance criteria, explicitly

I am disregarding criteria 1 and 4 as written. The goal they serve — a stop condition
that protects unwithdrawable surface — is right and is the reason this task exists.
The mechanism cannot serve it: an assertion comparing an implementation against its
own defining formula cannot gate anything. Re-aim #623 at (a) metamorphic and
structural property assertions, (b) the ladder-convergence oracle, (c) a VCD-real
golden inside the existing VCD suite, and (d) an explicit disposition of the two-state
VCD pin. Then K18-1 becomes a gate that can fire, and #490's permanence ordering —
the capstone's best decision — is actually enforced rather than merely declared.
