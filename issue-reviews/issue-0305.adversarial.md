# Issue #305: CAP-12: a drawn mixed-signal circuit turns a recorded photoplethysmogram into a live beat rate — the point at which JLS stops being a digital tool
- Lens: adversarial
- Date: 2026-08-08
- Reviewer: Claude Sonnet 5
- Verdict: needs-rework

## Summary

This is a "capstone" tracking issue in a large, self-referential planning apparatus (19 capstones #295-#313, 57 features #314-#370) proposing that JLS — currently a purely digital, event-driven logic simulator — grow a full analog SPICE-class continuous-time solver (MNA, Newton-Raphson, adaptive timestep, transistor models, vendor `.subckt` ingestion) in order to demo a heart-rate monitor. The file/line citations against the live codebase that I could check are accurate. The problem is scale, unverifiable provenance for the numbers driving that scale, and a couple of load-bearing criteria that are softer than they read.

## Findings, most severe first

**1. (High) The evidentiary spine — an `evidence_commit` and an entire `docs/plan/**` document tree — does not exist in this repository.** The issue's machine block pins every code claim to `evidence_commit: 2d0ca9dcd9db78b36c3caf4c6c57dd8701862cc7`, and repeatedly cites `docs/plan/capstones/CAP-12-heart-rate-monitor.md` and (via #331/#351) `docs/plan/features/FEAT-049-*.md` and `docs/plan/evidence/analog-determination.md`/`BRIEF.md` as already-landed, in-tree source documents. `git cat-file -e 2d0ca9dcd9db78b36c3caf4c6c57dd8701862cc7` and `git cat-file -e 3a81a4a7d6a0f108ec201e632732d308cc02b3fc` (the commit #351 cites for its cost derivation) both fail in this checkout, and `git log --all --diff-filter=A --name-only | grep -i docs/plan` across all 271 locally-reachable commits returns nothing — no `docs/plan/` path has ever existed in the history available here. (Caveat: the repo is a shallow clone, so this is not proof the commits never existed upstream — but it is exactly the situation the issue's own DoD checklist worries about for one cited document ("`11-analog-determination.md`... is an out-of-tree corpus input... must be committed") while treating the others as settled fact.) Every claim I could independently verify against current `src/`/`docs/` (Element.java's sealed `permits`, Put's `LogicElement`-typed owner, `Simulator.now`, `ElementRegistry` count, `ArchitectureRulesTest`/archunit, the two `continue-on-error: true` CI lines) checked out exactly. But the cost bands (61.5-94 mw), the "D8/D9/D10 maintainer decisions," and the kill-criteria recomputation all trace back to documents I cannot find anywhere in this repo's history. **Recommendation:** before treating any Cost/DoD figure as authoritative, confirm `docs/plan/**` and both cited commits are reachable on the real default branch (not just asserted in issue prose); if they are not, the entire cost/sufficiency apparatus for this capstone (and its siblings #331, #351, #303, #308, #309) needs to be re-derived from something actually in tree.

**2. (High) Scope is disproportionate to the project by roughly two orders of magnitude.** The "standalone" cost for this capstone's six required features alone is stated as 61.5-94 maintainer-weeks (over a year of full-time solo work), and that's before the other 18 capstones sharing the same analog spine. `ARCHITECTURE.md` itself records "JLS is a single-maintainer pedagogy tool" (Internationalization section) and a **recorded decision** as of 2026-07-26 (#221) that "the discrete-event interpreter remains JLS's **only** simulation execution strategy... a second strategy is premature optimization until CPU-scale designs are actually common," with an explicit revisit trigger that says nothing about analog. This issue proposes an entirely new execution paradigm — a from-scratch Newton-Raphson MNA solver with cross-platform bit-identical determinism, a 200-circuit convergence corpus, transistor models, and a nightly differential oracle against a real external SPICE — justified by a single PPG-heart-rate demo circuit that does not need general analog capability. The issue's own "Cost" section even flags the "easiest analog capstone, not the hardest" framing as an *engineering-risk* statement, not a cost one — the cost is dominated by shared infrastructure (FEAT-046, FEAT-049) regardless. Nothing in the issue argues why a project whose README frames it as "students draw circuits from gates, wires, registers, memories, state machines" needs to become a SPICE competitor to teach one PPG lab exercise. **Recommendation:** either scope this down to the minimum needed for the stated pedagogy goal (e.g., a much smaller linear-only solver with no transistor/vendor-card ingestion, no nightly external-oracle lane) or get explicit maintainer sign-off that a multi-year analog programme is in scope for a single-maintainer educational tool before filing 57 downstream feature issues against it.

**3. (Medium) AC-1's headline tolerance derivation rests on an admittedly unresolved premise.** The T²/120 derivation, and every downstream number (8.33 ms at 60 BPM, "8 samples of the 1 kHz lattice"), depends on "the reported quantity is integer BPM" — but that is Open Question 8, explicitly "Blocks acceptance... not filing." The issue presents the derivation with full confidence and precision while its own machine block says the premise it rests on isn't settled. If OQ-8 resolves to option (b) (one decimal place) the issue itself notes the tolerance "is not achievable on the 1 kHz lattice the measurement below uses" — i.e. a plausible resolution of an open question breaks the acceptance criterion's own feasibility claim. **Recommendation:** resolve OQ-8 before this issue is treated as having a stated (rather than merely stateable) AC-1 tolerance.

**4. (Medium) AC-2's "required" claim and its DoD checkbox are not the same bar, and the gap is on record but unclosed.** §3 concedes "AC-2's four-platform claim runs today only on advisory jobs" (`.github/workflows/ci.yml:156` and `:263`, confirmed both `continue-on-error: true`), and that promotion to a required lane belongs to a *different* issue (#265) that is not in `requires_features` or `blocked_by` for #305 — so #305 can close without #265 landing. The DoD bullet only demands the lanes be "confirmed to have **executed** rather than skipped or silently failed" — weaker than "passed" — leaving a path where a closer ticks the DoD box on advisory infrastructure that ran and reported a failure loudly (satisfying the literal DoD text) while the actual byte-identity claim AC-2 asserts remains unproven on required infrastructure. **Recommendation:** either add #265 to `blocked_by`, or tighten the DoD bullet to require AC-2's test itself pass on the lanes it names, not merely "execute."

**5. (Medium) Internal contradiction in the licensing paragraph.** "Every ngspice component is absorbable into a GPL-3.0-or-later Java project" is stated as a blanket premise, then immediately narrowed: "The genuine hazard is narrow: GPL-incompatible licenses. Scope the models, not the solver." If every component were truly absorbable there would be no such hazard to scope around. #351's own Cost section proposes porting from five separate upstream projects (ngspice, XSPICE, Sparse1.3, SpiceSharp, CircuitJS1), each carrying its own licensing history; asserting blanket compatibility without a per-project audit is exactly the kind of unverified legal claim an adversarial reviewer should flag before any porting work starts. **Recommendation:** do the license audit per absorbed project/file before "port with attribution" is treated as a settled plan, not after.

**6. (Low) Process overhead is compounding on itself.** Two of #305's three comments are pure bookkeeping (resolving `planned_features` to filed numbers, re-walking a DAG, withdrawing/reinstating graph edges) with zero code and zero movement on the actual deliverable; the same machinery is mirrored across #331 and #351 (verified: their `blocked_by`/`blocks` match #305's claims exactly, so the graph is at least internally consistent). Consistent bureaucracy is not free bureaucracy — this is real maintainer/agent time that could go to the "demo slice" the issue itself calls "the mandatory calibration experiment" (AC-7, §Cost) needed before any of these cost bands can be trusted.

## What's solid

- The file/line citations against current `src/` and `docs/` that I checked (Element.java:17-18 sealed permits, Put.java:57 constructor signature, Simulator.java:36, ElementRegistry.java device count, `docs/simulation-semantics.md:26`, the two CI `continue-on-error` lines, `ArchitectureRulesTest`/archunit dependency) are all accurate.
- The "determinism of the solve is not determinism of the verdict" risk analysis (a 1 nV perturbation moving a threshold crossing by ~1.6e5 ticks) is a genuinely sharp catch, and binding AC-1 to beat-count-and-interval-within-tolerance rather than exact-tick equality is the right response to it.
- The recomputed KC-12-1 (catching that the original 24-mw kill threshold would fire before the deliverable could possibly exist, since it's 26-39% of the real required sum) is a legitimate self-correction of an internally inconsistent kill criterion.
