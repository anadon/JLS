# Issue #732: TASK-C555-1: `docs/performance.md` publishes JLS's throughput with the full method — and names at least one place a competitor is faster
- Lens: adversarial
- Date: 2026-08-08
- Reviewer: Claude Sonnet 5
- Verdict: needs-rework

## Summary of what's being asked

`docs/performance.md` (does not yet exist — confirmed by `find`/`ls` on `docs/`)
should state throughput numbers (events/s, cycles/s) sourced from the committed
suite of #554, with full methodology, a stated reproduction tolerance, and at
least one honest admission that a competitor is faster somewhere. It is
`TASK-C555-1`, one of two tasks under feature #555 (FEAT-C28-2), itself one of
four features under capstone #512 (CAP-28).

## Findings, most severe first

**1. (Critical — feasibility) The hard prerequisite, #554, has not delivered a committed suite; the only existing tooling is explicitly disclaimed as scratch work.** #732's `ordering_after: [554]` is correct in spirit — numbers must come from a committed instrument, not an ad hoc run — but #554 is open, and its own 2026-08-08 comment states plainly: *"None of the instruments are in the repository. `git grep -l "KernelProbe\|class Census\|NoDedup" origin/master -- src test riscv` returns nothing."* The one committed harness, `riscv/bench_kernel.py` (present at `/home/user/JLS/riscv/bench_kernel.py`), documents itself in its own docstring as *"Not part of the verification suite... it produced the kernel measurements in `docs/capability-roadmap/keystone-c-performance.md`; re-run it before and after any such change rather than trusting those numbers"* — i.e. a scratch script with no machine-readable output, not the AC-1 "committed command" #732 requires. Starting #732 today means either blocking on #554 or hand-rolling the exact ad hoc measurement discipline the task exists to eliminate. Recommend: do not schedule #732 until #554 merges a suite meeting its own AC-1/AC-4 (committed command, machine-readable output).

**2. (High — untestable/gameable AC) "the numbers are taken from the suite's machine-readable output rather than transcribed" cannot be checked by anyone reviewing the PR.** Quoted directly from the issue body's second bullet. There is no specified artifact — no committed JSON/CSV alongside the doc, no CI step that regenerates the suite's output and diffs it against the numbers in `docs/performance.md` — so "transcribed" and "generated" are byte-identical outcomes to a reviewer. A contributor can run the suite, read a printed line, and type it into markdown by hand, which is exactly the failure mode the sentence is trying to rule out, and the AC as worded cannot distinguish that from the honest path. Recommend: require a committed or CI-regenerated machine-readable output file that a script cross-checks against the prose numbers, turning this into a mechanical gate instead of a promise.

**3. (High — gameable, contradicts the doc's own honesty premise) The competitor-loss requirement has no methodology attached to it, unlike JLS's own numbers.** "At least one place where a competitor is faster is named, with the reason" carries none of the rigor (hardware, JDK, flags, node counts, tolerance) the same bullet list demands for JLS's own throughput claims. A single unsubstantiated sentence ("Digital is probably faster on tiny circuits because of JIT warm-up") satisfies the checkbox literally while adding a second unbacked public claim to a document whose entire purpose (per the issue's own framing — "a performance page with no losses in it is read as marketing and dismissed") is to be *more* rigorous than marketing, not merely to contain a concession. Recommend: either require the competitor figure to carry the same measured rigor as JLS's own number, or explicitly scope it as "unverified, pending #560" and mark it as such in the doc.

**4. (Medium — scope/sequencing conflict with a sibling issue) The same "competitor is faster" territory is separately and more rigorously owned by #560 (FEAT-C28-4), and #732 does not order after it or reference it.** #732's own Boundary section says *"the head-to-head table is #560"*, and #512 (CAP-28) AC-5 requires #560 to publish *"same-machine, same-workload... at least one workload where a competitor wins"* with pinned competitor configs. If #732 lands first and picks a lightweight, unmeasured competitor anecdote, and #560 later lands with a rigorous same-machine comparison naming a *different* losing workload (or contradicting the earlier claim), `docs/performance.md` will disagree with the eventual head-to-head page with nothing in either issue requiring reconciliation. Recommend: add an explicit follow-up obligation (or a tracked TODO in the doc) to reconcile #732's competitor claim once #560 lands.

**5. (Medium — underspecified target) #732 never names which competitor.** It says only "a competitor," while its parent #555 and capstone #512 both specifically name Digital (and #512 additionally names Logisim-Evolution for the head-to-head in #560). Read in isolation — which is how a task-tier issue is meant to be actionable — an implementer has no mandate for which tool to compare against, and could pick an obscure or non-representative one and still satisfy the letter of the AC. Recommend: inherit the competitor name(s) explicitly from #555/#512 into #732's acceptance criteria rather than leaving it as "a competitor."

**6. (Medium — untestable tolerance) "the tolerance is stated" (quoting CAP-28 AC-2 verbatim) is satisfied by writing any number down, with no requirement that it be empirically derived.** The roadmap document this task will presumably draw from (`docs/capability-roadmap/keystone-c-performance.md`) already documents real sources of run-to-run noise on this exact workload — SigSim's O(n²) setup dominating short runs, "82 young GCs across 12 reps," and an explicit caveat that "Ratios are robust; absolute nanoseconds are machine-specific." A tolerance chosen without measuring variance (e.g., an author's guess of "±2%") would tick the box while being fiction on a benchmark this noisy. Recommend: require the tolerance to be derived from repeated runs of the committed suite (stddev over N reps), not asserted.

**7. (Low — incentive mismatch, not a defect in the text) Kill criterion KC-28-1 on the parent capstone (#512) explicitly tolerates JLS being ">5× behind Digital,"** which is the right philosophy for the program as a whole, but combined with finding 3 it means #732's single-sentence "honest framing" requirement is structurally cheap to satisfy with the least damaging example available rather than the most representative one (e.g. the CPU-scale flagship workload where the roadmap doc's own profiling shows 95% of loop time is queue/value bookkeeping, not logic). The AC cannot distinguish a token disclosure from a representative one.

**8. (Low — process, repo-wide not unique to this issue) The `part_of_feature: 555` relationship is asserted only in freeform YAML, not in GitHub's native issue graph.** `issue_read get` on #732 returns `has_parent: false`, and `get_sub_issues` on #555 returns `[]` — same for sibling #733. Any tooling or triage pass that queries GitHub's structured hierarchy (rather than parsing issue-body YAML) will miss both tasks under their feature. Worth a mention since this issue's own acceptance criteria are otherwise mechanically precise; the containing relationship is not.

## What's solid

- **Boundary discipline is real and cross-checked.** #732 vs #733 (README claim + sweep) vs #560 (head-to-head) vs #557 (staleness) is a clean, non-overlapping split, confirmed by independently reading #733's and #554's own dedup-pass comment, which explicitly re-audited this exact chain for duplicate ownership and found none.
- **Depending on #554 rather than measuring ad hoc is the right call in principle.** The dependency direction is correct; the problem is only that #554 hasn't actually landed a usable instrument yet (finding 1), not that the ordering itself is wrong.
- **Citation of CAP-28 AC-1/AC-2 is accurate.** Verified against #512's body: AC-1 ("a cited throughput number... produced by a committed command") and AC-2 ("an independent party can reproduce the number within a stated tolerance") map correctly onto #732's first three bullets.
- **No licensing/security hazard identified.** Naming Digital or Logisim-Evolution (both open-source) as faster on a stated workload carries no meaningful legal exposure.

## Recommendation

Do not begin implementation until #554 lands a suite that actually satisfies its own AC-1/AC-4 (a committed, machine-readable-output command) — otherwise this task can only be completed by writing the exact kind of ad hoc, transcribed number it is chartered to prevent. Once unblocked, tighten the acceptance criteria before work starts: make "not transcribed" mechanically checkable (a diffed machine-readable artifact), name the competitor explicitly, require the competitor figure to carry comparable rigor to JLS's own numbers (or be marked provisional pending #560), and require the stated tolerance to be derived from measured variance rather than asserted.
