# Issue #886: FEAT-C32-4: a circuit becomes a link a colleague opens — the whole design travelling inside the URL, with no server, no account and a refusal when it will not fit
- Lens: adversarial
- Date: 2026-08-08
- Reviewer: Claude Sonnet 5
- Verdict: needs-rework

## Summary

The feature (client-side, URL-fragment circuit sharing off the CAP-32 demo) is a defensible, small addition once its substrate exists, and most of its acceptance criteria are concretely testable. But the issue has one load-bearing internal contradiction between its own Outcome statement and its own Open Question 2, a declared hard dependency (#572) that omits a second dependency the issue's own text says is blocking, and a kill criterion that doesn't cover its worst-case branch. None of these are fatal to the idea, but as filed the issue could be "satisfied" by an implementation that never lets a user share their own circuit at all — which is the opposite of what the Outcome promises.

## Findings, most severe first

**1. The Outcome promises sharing a user's own circuit; the recommended resolution of Open Question 2 forecloses it, and the issue does not notice the conflict.**
Outcome: *"A person with a circuit open produces a URL... The circuit travels inside the link."* That "circuit open" is naturally the user's own work-in-progress circuit in the desktop editor. But Open Question 2 asks "Where does the affordance live in the desktop GUI — or does it?" and recommends: *"The cheaper and more honest default is the demo page only, which keeps this feature entirely inside CAP-32 and adds nothing to the Swing surface."* The demo page, per #573 AC-2 and #516 AC-2 (both quoted verbatim in this issue's own `related` set), is *"read-only by construction: no save, no upload, no user content."* If the affordance lives only on a demo page that by contract accepts no user content, there is no path by which a user's own circuit — drawn in the desktop editor, which is where every real circuit exists — ever reaches the link-encoding step. The two most-plausible readings of "demo page only" (a) it only ever links curated demo circuits, in which case the Outcome's "their own circuit" claim is false, or (b) the demo page starts accepting client-side-uploaded circuit files to encode, in which case it silently expands #573/#516's read-only "no user content" contract that this same issue elsewhere treats as untouchable ("this feature adds one entry path to a page that already exists," Boundary section, "Not #573"). AC-6's acceptance criterion doesn't catch this either — it only asks that "the sharing affordance is reachable and honest about what it is," which says nothing about which product surface the affordance is on.
*Recommendation:* Resolve Open Question 2 with an explicit mechanism, not a recommendation-in-passing. If the desktop editor is the producer (the only reading consistent with the Outcome statement), say so and add the required GUI-surface cost to the band; if the demo page is the producer, rewrite the Outcome to say "a curated example becomes a link," not "a person with a circuit open," and reconcile with #573/#516 AC-2's "no user content" language explicitly rather than leaving the tension unaddressed.

**2. AC-3's stated blocking dependency is absent from the machine-readable header, and the true dependency it cites is confused with an already-shipped property.**
The YAML block declares `blocked_by: [572]` only. But Open Question 1 says of the recommended encoding: *"It depends on #334/#437's canonical-text work being the thing encoded — a link produced from a non-canonical serialization will not be stable across platforms and AC-3 fails."* Both #334 and #437 are open (verified above — #334 has zero landed children, #437 is itself blocked on #436). If AC-3 genuinely cannot pass before #334/#437 land, that is a real blocking dependency omitted from `blocked_by`, and nothing stops #886 from being picked up and executed against #572 alone, with an implementer reasonably concluding AC-3 is satisfiable today. Separately, the premise may be wrong: #334's own "Related work" section credits #166 (closed) with *"canonical byte-identical saves"* — i.e., deterministic, byte-identical `Circuit.save` output already ships, independent of #334/#437 (which are about diff-friendly *stable-id references* and the *plain-text default container*, not determinism per se). If #166 already gives the determinism AC-3 needs, the stated dependency on #334/#437 is unnecessary friction that could stall filing/execution for no real reason; if it does not, the issue needs to say precisely which property of #334/#437 (not yet landed) AC-3 actually requires.
*Recommendation:* Either add `334, 437` to `blocked_by` and justify why #166's determinism guarantee is insufficient, or drop the claimed dependency and cite #166 directly as the encoding's determinism basis.

**3. KC-32-4-1 assumes "re-plan" is always available if #572's go/no-go fails, but one of #572's two named fallbacks makes that impossible.**
KC-32-4-1: *"if #572's go/no-go rejects the browser substrate, this feature is void as written... It re-plans against whatever fallback #572 chose."* #572's two pre-declared fallbacks are (a) headless-rendered interactive SVG with a JS signal player driven by pre-computed VCD, and (b) recorded video walkthroughs. Fallback (a) is still an interactive, parameterizable page — a link-encoded circuit could plausibly still be decoded and rendered client-side. Fallback (b), a recorded video, is a fixed artifact with no interactivity and no way to parameterize by an arbitrary user-supplied circuit — there is no "re-plan" that turns a pre-recorded video into a live target for an arbitrary shared circuit. KC-32-4-1 as written treats both fallbacks as equally re-plannable, which is false for (b).
*Recommendation:* Split the kill criterion: on fallback (a), re-plan against the SVG/VCD player; on fallback (b), close #886 as void with that reason recorded, exactly as KC-32-4-1's own second sentence already allows for a different trigger ("or it closes with that reason recorded") — just make explicit that (b) is the branch that always closes it, not one it might survive.

**4. AC-2's "measured, published, enforced" ceiling treats a third-party, unversioned property as a fixed engineering constant, with no revisit trigger.**
AC-2 requires the ceiling to be "derived from measurement across the browsers CAP-32 already tests plus at least one messaging client that rewrites links, and the number is written down with how it was obtained." Messaging clients' link-rewriting behavior (WhatsApp, Slack, iMessage, Teams, etc.) is not a stable, versioned contract — vendors change URL preview/rewrite/truncation behavior without notice, unlike the browsers CAP-32 already pins. Every other cross-cutting risk in this codebase's open issues (e.g. #572's CheerpJ go/no-go, #882's `TimeBase` precision choice) names an explicit revisit trigger for exactly this kind of externally-controlled fact; #886 does not. As written, AC-2 can be satisfied once, at filing time, against a messaging client that later changes behavior — the acceptance criterion would still read "passed" in the repo's history while the real-world guarantee ("a link that reliably works when pasted into chat") quietly degrades. This is exactly the "acceptance criteria that can pass while the real goal fails" pattern the adversarial lens is asked to hunt.
*Recommendation:* Name a revisit trigger (e.g., "a bug report that a previously-fitting link is rejected on arrival" or a periodic re-measurement cadence) and record it the same way #572/#882 do for their own externally-sourced facts.

**5. Cost band is asserted, not derived, and looks optimistic against the codebase's own precedent for hostile-input work.**
`band_mw: "1-2"` matches #572 and #573 exactly, but #886 additionally commits to: a hostile-input decoder that is "bounded in size and nesting before it allocates anything" (AC-5) — comparable in seriousness to the `UntrustedFileHardeningTest`/`MAX_CIRCUIT_TEXT_BYTES` machinery #38 built for the file loader (ARCHITECTURE.md's "Error-reporting contracts" section, `LoadError` taxonomy) — plus cross-browser *and* cross-messaging-client empirical measurement (AC-2), plus a deterministic round-trip test suite (AC-3), plus new UI copy and affordance work (AC-6) wherever Open Question 2 lands it. The issue offers no cost derivation (contrast #334/#500, which price each task against a stated LoC-per-maintainer-week calibration); it simply reuses the sibling features' number.
*Recommendation:* Derive the band from the same calibration #334/#500 use, or explicitly note it's inherited "because most of the cost rides infrastructure CAP-32 already funds" (which the Why section gestures at but doesn't quantify per-AC).

**6. AC-4 cites #835 as establishing the read-only-by-construction rule, but #835 is not in this issue's `related` list.**
AC-4: *"the same rule #835 (TASK-C572-2) states for the demo itself."* Every other cross-issue citation in this document's body (#572, #573, #574, #500, #510, #163, #882, #351, #346) appears in `related: [510, 516, 500, 574, 551, 334, 437]` — except #835 and #163, both cited in prose but absent from the machine-readable list. This is a minor traceability gap by this corpus's own convention (every other issue reviewed in this pass treats `related` as the complete cross-reference set), but it matters here because #835's actual content was not independently verifiable in this review and the acceptance criterion leans on it.
*Recommendation:* Add #835 and #163 to `related`, or fold their citations into the existing entries if they're duplicates/sub-issues.

## What's solid

- **AC-1's core architectural claim (fragment never sent to server) is correct and testable** — this is standard, well-understood browser behavior, and gating the whole feature's existence on it ("the moment a design would have to be POSTed somewhere, this feature is out of scope") is a clean, enforceable line.
- **AC-5's "hostile input" framing for the decoder** (bounded before allocation, reject-never-repair) matches the codebase's existing discipline for untrusted `.jls` loads (#38) and is the right posture.
- **KC-32-4-3 (no shortener/redirector/third-party link service)** is well-reasoned and correctly cites the simulator.io precedent from the #510 survey it's built on.
- **The Boundary section is genuinely disciplined** — five explicit non-goals (not #574, not #573, not collaboration/#163, not #500, not persistence) each with a one-line reason, which is exactly the kind of scope-creep defense this lens looks for and mostly doesn't need to flag here.
- **AC-3's byte-identical round-trip requirement** is a strong, falsifiable criterion once its actual dependency (finding 2) is nailed down correctly.

## Verdict rationale

The idea and most of the acceptance criteria are sound; the two defects that actually need resolving before execution are structural (finding 1: does the desktop editor or the demo page produce the link, and does that square with the demo's own no-user-content contract) and traceability (finding 2: an undeclared or misattributed hard dependency for AC-3). Both are answerable in a single planning pass without re-scoping the feature, which is why this is `needs-rework` rather than `should-not-proceed`.
