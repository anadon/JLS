# Issue #499: Virtual-hardware / virtual-logic parity, part 1 of 3: what parity means, the two tiers and their honest wall-clock bands, the prior art, and layers L0-L4
- Lens: adversarial
- Date: 2026-08-08
- Reviewer: Claude Sonnet 5
- Verdict: needs-rework

This is not a work-item issue; it is a 56 KB inlined document fragment (part 1 of 3, continued in #497 and #498), filed by the repo owner to "rescue" `docs/virtual-hardware-parity.md` from a branch about to be deleted. It carries no acceptance criteria of its own — the numbers, kill criteria and milestones it defines are consumed by other issues (#379, #407, #392, #459, #456, #477, #478, #458, #479, #308, #425, #482, #331, #495, #493/#494/#496). Its code citations are unusually accurate: spot-checked `src/jls/JLSInfo.java:69`, `src/jls/elem/Memory.java:1224`, `src/jls/FileAbstractor.java:65`, `src/jls/sim/Simulator.java:195,217,224-233,252`, and `src/jls/JlsModules.java:32` all match verbatim or within the stated line range. That precision, however, makes the places where the document's own numbers don't reconcile more concerning, not less.

## Findings, most severe first

**1. A headline "current speed" figure is internally unreconciled with the document's own inputs.** §1.4 states: *"The drawn machine runs at **18,800–19,500 simulated cycles/s** today (measured engine constant ÷ estimated events per cycle...)."* The engine constant is fixed elsewhere in the same document at R = 3.14×10⁶ events/s. Dividing backward, 3.14×10⁶ ÷ 18,800–19,500 implies an events/cycle constant of **~161–167**. But §4 L0(b) lists exactly four candidate events/cycle values that were actually measured or used elsewhere in this same document: **121.5, 243.1, 245.5, and 388.4** (the last of which is the one that reproduces §2.2's other headline figure, 8,090 cycles/s: 3.14×10⁶ ÷ 388.4 ≈ 8,085, matching "8,090... measured in-tree" almost exactly). None of the four is anywhere near 161–167. The 161 figure does appear later, in L3, used for an unrelated handover-window estimate ("~161 events/cycle"), but §1.4 never says that's the value it used, and the document never states why "today" has two different measured cycles/s figures (8,090 vs. 18,800–19,500) that differ by 2.3×. Downstream issue #425 is filed specifically against "§1.3 and §1.5's wall-clock band," which is derived from this unreconciled pair.
   *Recommendation:* before any issue treats §1.3/§1.4/§1.5 as citable, add one sentence naming which events/cycle value backs each of the "today" figures, or delete whichever one is stale.

**2. L0(b) reasons in a circle: it assumes the answer that L0's own measurement gate exists to establish.** L0's stated purpose is "measures [nine unmeasured constants]... nothing below is honestly costed until it lands." Yet inside L0(b) itself: *"A boot **cannot** be TestGen-driven: `SigSim.initSim` pre-posts every stimulus transition during elaboration, so a boot needs ~1.9×10⁸ resident `SimEvent`s — **estimated at over 12 GB of heap**... An internal `Clock`... is therefore a **hard prerequisite**."* This conclusion — build an internal-Clock machine instead of a TestGen-driven one — is derived from an "order-of-magnitude estimate, not a measurement," inside the section whose entire job is to replace order-of-magnitude estimates with measurements. If the 12 GB estimate is off by an order of magnitude (plausible, since it depends on the very heap-footprint-per-`SimEvent` constant nothing in this document claims to have measured precisely), the "hard prerequisite" it drives may not be one.
   *Recommendation:* reframe L0(b)'s Clock-based redesign as the *first thing L0 tests*, not as a conclusion L0 is built to confirm.

**3. The "rescue" itself is an archival downgrade, not a fix, for the exact problem it names.** The block quote at the top states the motivation: content on a to-be-deleted branch "preserves nothing," so it is inlined into an issue instead. But a GitHub issue body is not more durable than a branch: it is not cloned with the repo, not code-reviewed, not diffable in the normal `git log` sense, editable or lockable by anyone with issue-write access, and — per the issue's own labeling — explicitly "non-normative," so it carries no protection against silent edits the way a committed file under `docs/` would via `git blame`/PR review. The stated fix for "branch-only documents get lost" is "put them somewhere equally uncommitted." Nothing in the issue explains why this wasn't simply committed to `docs/virtual-hardware-parity.md` on `master` (flagged non-normative in its own status line, exactly as it is today) instead of split across three issue bodies that eleven-plus other issues then cite by GitHub issue number for line-numbered anchors that no longer exist in any file.
   *Recommendation:* commit the rescued text to `docs/` (even under a `proposals/` or `rejected/` subdirectory) as a small PR; close #499/#497/#498 pointing at the commit. This is strictly more durable and auditable than the issue-body form and doesn't require every citing issue to keep resolving `:line-N` references against a synthetic `*(original file lines N–M)*` marker inserted during the split.

**4. No acceptance criteria attach to closing this issue, and "part of 3" creates a silent multi-issue dependency the tracker doesn't model.** #499 has no checklist, no "Definition of Done," and is not linked as a parent/child of #497 or #498 through GitHub's own sub-issue mechanism (`has_parent: false`, `has_children: false` on all three). A reader landing on #499 alone has no way to discover #497/#498 are required reading except by scrolling to the prose table — GitHub's issue search or triage view won't surface that dependency. If someone closes #499 for administrative cleanup ("this is just docs, not a task") independent of #497/#498, the eleven-plus citing issues silently lose the anchor they cite.
   *Recommendation:* use `sub_issue_write`/parent-child linking for #497/#498 under #499 (or a tracking issue), and add an explicit "do not close until #497 and #498 are also resolved together" note.

**5. Cost and effort figures that gate real engineering decisions are self-admittedly unsupported, yet are already being cited as acceptance-adjacent numbers by other issues.** §1.4: *"The evidence base prices it at 'roughly 30–45 maintainer-weeks'; **no work breakdown supporting that figure exists anywhere**."* The document is honest about this, but the honesty doesn't stop the number from being load-bearing: it is the only cost estimate offered for "make the structural tier live," and it's the kind of figure a maintainer or reviewer skimming the issue will anchor on regardless of the caveat two sentences later.
   *Recommendation:* strip unsupported point/range estimates that have no traceable derivation, or mark them inline as "unsupported — do not cite" rather than relying on prose that a skimming reader will miss.

**6. The audit claim underpinning the whole rescue operation ("~200 filed issues, 77 citing branch-only documents") is asserted, not shown.** No methodology, script, or issue-search query is given, and it is not falsifiable from the issue itself.
   *Recommendation:* link the actual search query/script used, or drop the count and just state which issues cite this specific document (which is otherwise done well).

## What's solid
- Code-anchored claims (file:line citations) checked against HEAD are accurate, including exact line ranges for `Simulator.java`, `Memory.java`, `FileAbstractor.java`, `JLSInfo.java`, and the `27 files declare public void react` / `LogicElement.java` throw claim (verified: grep returns 27).
- The document is unusually forthcoming about its own weak points — it flags four specific unmeasured constants up front (§ intro) and repeats the caveat at every figure that depends on them, which is good practice most issues in this repo do not bother with.
- #497 and #498 do exist and are open, so the "part 1/2/3" continuity claim is at least not a dangling reference today.
- The prior-art comparison (§3) is well-sourced to named external documents/papers and clearly separates "measured here" from "quoted from vendor/paper," which is a real discipline most competitive proposals skip.

## Verdict rationale
The issue succeeds at literal text preservation and is honest about most of its uncertainty, but it has a genuine, evidence-backed internal arithmetic inconsistency in its own headline figure (finding 1), reasons circularly in the one place (L0(b)) where circularity is most dangerous (finding 2), and picked an archival mechanism that does not actually solve the durability problem it exists to solve (finding 3). Given that at least a dozen other issues already cite specific numbers and section anchors from this document as if they were fixed, these defects should be resolved — or explicitly flagged inline as unresolved — before more issues accrete on top of them.
