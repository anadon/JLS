# Issue #513: CAP-29: a course arriving from Digital, CircuitVerse or Falstad opens its existing circuits in JLS with every loss named — the .circ path gains three siblings on shared loss-report infrastructure
- Lens: adversarial
- Date: 2026-08-08
- Reviewer: Claude Sonnet 5
- Verdict: needs-rework

## Summary of what's being proposed

CAP-29 is a capstone that generalizes CAP-16's (#311) planned `.circ`/Logisim-Evolution loss-naming import-report machinery into a format-agnostic contract, then bolts on three more importers (`.dig`/Digital, `.cv`/CircuitVerse JSON, Falstad text) plus `.dig` test-vector translation and migration docs. All six planned features (PF-1..PF-6) were filed within the same session as separate issues (#556, #558, #559, #561, #562, #553) per the sole comment. Nothing in this scope has landed; the body is a "lean filing" that deliberately skips the full capstone template (§1 Outcome Statement, §2 Sufficiency, mermaid DAG, Cost table, Completion Criteria checklist) that its own sibling #311 uses.

## Findings, most severe first

**1. (High) A stated hard dependency of PF-2 is entirely absent from `ordering_after`, and that dependency's own chain is three-deep.** PF-2's description states: "generics/parameterized circuits map or refuse by name until FEAT-017 lands." FEAT-017 is issue #357, confirmed open, and its own machine block reads `blocked_by: [318, 319, 340]` — three more open features (FEAT-014, FEAT-013, FEAT-016) that must land first. None of #357, #318, #319, or #340 appears anywhere in CAP-29's `ordering_after` (which lists only #311/FEAT-025 and #314/FEAT-002). An implementer who starts PF-2 (#558) using only the stated ordering will discover this gate mid-implementation rather than before scoping. Recommendation: add #357 to `ordering_after` (and note its own three-deep closure), or explicitly scope generics-refusal as day-one behavior so FEAT-017 is not a blocker at all.

**2. (High) The capstone's own `ordering_after` line contains a factual error that was flagged but never fixed.** The body says: `ordering_after: ["#311 CAP-16's FEAT-025 (the .circ importer and its loss-naming report land first...)", ...]`. The one comment on the issue explicitly corrects this: "this capstone's `ordering_after` cites '#311 CAP-16's FEAT-025' — FEAT-025 is actually issue #323 (#311 is CAP-16 itself...)." No `REPLAN:` or body edit followed — the comment says "recording the correct pointer here rather than editing the body." A reader who follows the literal reference will look at #311 (a 40-section capstone tracking issue) rather than #323 (the actual feature to watch for landing). Recommendation: correct the body directly; a comment nobody reads before starting work does not fix a wrong pointer in the source of truth.

**3. (High) AC-1 is gameable and inconsistent with the rigor AC-4 claims to inherit.** AC-4 states: "the CAP-16 silent-difference standard applies to every format." But CAP-16 (#311) AC-1 requires a **committed corpus of ≥30 `.circ` files from ≥3 independent public course repositories** with a per-construct coverage table, gated by a numerically-triggered kill criterion (KC-16-1: >50% of files needing out-of-subset constructs re-costs the whole capstone). CAP-29's AC-1 instead reads: "One real published circuit per format imports with zero unexplained losses (every loss named, located, explained)." A single hand-picked, trivially simple circuit per format (e.g., two gates and a wire) satisfies this criterion vacuously — there's nothing to name because nothing was lost — while telling you nothing about whether the importer handles real course material, which is the entire stated purpose of the capstone ("An instructor with a decade of course material..."). Recommendation: either adopt CAP-16's corpus-scale AC-1 verbatim per format, or state explicitly why a single-file bar is sufficient for these three formats and CAP-16's was not.

**4. (Medium-High) The PF-2/PF-3 cost bands are not credible against the sibling capstone's own re-derivation for a comparable importer.** CAP-16 priced its `.circ` reader alone (FEAT-025, one format) at **6-12 mw** (feature-document band) or **12-18 mw** ("competing estimate," which CAP-16's own Cost section instructs readers to "treat... until the demo slice's two-day measurement is run"). CAP-29 prices `.dig` (PF-2, XML, generics/parameterization, embedded test vectors) at **4-6 mw** and `.cv` (PF-3, JSON) at **3-5 mw** — both below even CAP-16's *low* band for a single comparable reader, on the stated theory that "the marginal cost of each additional format is small" once PF-1's shared infra exists. That theory is unverified: PF-1 itself is only 1-2 mw and untried, and CAP-16's own reader hasn't shipped yet to prove the shared shape actually generalizes cheaply. If PF-1 underdelivers or the `.dig`/`.cv` formats have their own geometry/connectivity idiosyncrasies (as `.circ` did, per CAP-16 §3 risk 2), PF-2/PF-3 have only the generic KC-29-1 (1.5× stop-loss, no measurement protocol) as a backstop — far weaker than CAP-16's explicit two-day corpus measurement and numeric trigger. Recommendation: run a comparable two-day corpus measurement for at least one of `.dig`/`.cv` before committing to these bands, mirroring CAP-16's own discipline.

**5. (Medium) AC-5 cites an unshipped pattern as an established "standard."** AC-5 requires: "XML paths ship XXE-proof (CAP-16 AC-5 standard)." CAP-16's AC-5 (`CircXxeHardeningTest`) is itself unimplemented — CAP-16 is open, its `.circ` reader (#323) hasn't landed, and CAP-16's own Background section states "the `.circ` reader introduces the **first** XML parse in shipped code" (confirmed: `grep -rl "javax.xml\|DocumentBuilder\|XMLStreamReader\|org.w3c.dom" src/` returns nothing at HEAD). If `.dig` (PF-2, also XML via XStream) lands before `.circ`, CAP-29 — not CAP-16 — would introduce the first XML parser in shipped code, and "the CAP-16 AC-5 standard" it's citing wouldn't exist yet to copy. This is citing a plan as a precedent. Recommendation: state the XXE vector list and hardening approach directly in PF-2's own scope rather than deferring to a sibling standard that may not have landed first.

**6. (Medium) No license/provenance question is raised for any of the three new formats, despite the sibling capstone raising exactly this issue for `.circ`.** CAP-16's Open Questions 1-2 and KC-16-5 flag that absorbing Logisim-Evolution's GPLv3-only (no "or later") port-geometry data changes JLS's own license posture project-wide, and that this must be resolved with upstream before absorption. CAP-29 says nothing about whether `.dig`'s (Digital, GPL) or CircuitVerse's (check license) element-geometry/connectivity logic carries the same hazard. Even if JSON/text formats prove less geometry-encumbered than Logisim's XML, the capstone should say so explicitly rather than silently omit the question its own sibling treated as ship-blocking.

**7. (Medium) AC-2's verification mechanism is unspecified.** "A .dig circuit with embedded tests imports and its translated vectors pass under `-t` with the same verdicts Digital reports" requires comparing against Digital's own simulation output. No mechanism is named: is Digital run in CI as an oracle, are verdicts taken from a fixture's documented expected output, or is this a one-time manual comparison? CAP-16's analogous AC-3 (`CircVectorParityTest`) at least names the batch engine it replays through; CAP-29's AC-2 names no equivalent test or oracle-acquisition method. Recommendation: name the test and how "same verdicts" is captured and pinned (a golden file, not a live Digital dependency in CI).

**8. (Low-Medium) Process/rigor mismatch with the template this capstone borrows standards from.** The issue explicitly self-describes as a "lean filing" (`filed_by: session review directive 2026-08-04 — lean filing`), skipping the full capstone template §1 Outcome Statement, §2 Required Feature Set & Sufficiency, mermaid DAG, per-task Cost table, and Completion Criteria checklist that #311, #314, and #357 all carry. Yet it borrows those siblings' standards by reference (AC-4 "CAP-16 silent-difference standard," AC-5 "CAP-16 AC-5 standard," #38 hostile-input standard) without the structural apparatus (Sufficiency argument, DAG walk) that let those siblings catch gaps like Finding 1 above before filing. The lean template is arguably fine for a capstone this size, but it also means the missing-dependency and stale-body problems found here went uncaught by exactly the mechanism (DAG re-walk, REPLAN discipline) that would normally catch them elsewhere in this tracker.

**9. (Low) The body is already stale relative to its own comment.** `planned_features: [PF-1 unfiled, PF-2 unfiled, PF-3 unfiled, PF-4 unfiled, PF-5 unfiled, PF-6 unfiled]` in the machine block is contradicted by the same-day comment reporting all six filed. Per this repo's own convention (seen throughout #311, #314, #357), every response is expected to end in a `REPLAN:` comment regenerating the machine block; this comment does not, so the body's `planned_features` list is misleading to anyone who reads only the issue body.

**10. (Low) KC-29-1 has no measurement protocol.** "Per-format stop-loss at 1.5× its band" doesn't say when this is checked (mid-implementation? at PF completion?) or by what mechanism a format is judged to have "blown through." Contrast CAP-16's KC-16-1, which names a specific corpus size, a specific percentage trigger, and a specific consequence. As written, KC-29-1 is not falsifiable/actionable in the way a kill criterion needs to be.

## What's solid

- The demo-slice choice (PF-4 Falstad, "smallest format, proves the shared-report generalization") is a sensible, cheap way to de-risk PF-1's generalization claim before funding the larger PF-2/PF-3.
- AC-3 ("report contract is one schema across all importers, golden-tested") is concrete and testable as stated.
- The sibling-merge conflict rule ("if CAP-16's REPLAN prefers absorbing this scope instead, merge there — lower number wins") is unambiguous and cheap to apply.
- Citing #38's hostile-input hardening precedent for every new parser is the right instinct given this repo's documented history of malicious circuit-file attachments (SECURITY.md, referenced in #38).

## Bottom line

The overall shape — generalize CAP-16's report infra, then add cheap-marginal-cost importers — is reasonable. But the acceptance criteria are gameable relative to the standard the issue itself invokes (Finding 3), a stated hard prerequisite is missing from the dependency list with a three-deep closure behind it (Finding 1), a factual pointer error was identified but left uncorrected in the source of truth (Finding 2), and the cost bands for the two largest planned features undercut the sibling capstone's own re-derived estimate for a comparable reader without new evidence (Finding 4). These are the kind of gaps that surface mid-implementation rather than at scoping time. Recommend a rework pass: fix the ordering_after pointer, add the FEAT-017 chain, tighten AC-1 to corpus scale (or justify not doing so), and either measure or explicitly caveat the PF-2/PF-3 cost bands before funding starts.
