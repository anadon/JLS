# Issue #786: TASK-C591-2: a complete draft exists, reviewed by someone outside the author, with a reproducibility appendix a reviewer runs without contacting us
- Lens: adversarial
- Date: 2026-08-08
- Reviewer: Claude Sonnet 5
- Verdict: needs-rework

## Summary

This is a non-code, writing-and-publication task: produce a peer-review-track paper draft (grading contract, determinism argument, reproducibility appendix, related work) for the FEAT-C36-4 venue submission (#591), review it externally, and ship a committed, unaided-reproducible appendix. The prose is unusually well specified for what "done" means in narrative terms, but several of its concrete dependencies do not exist yet, its human-process criteria are gameable, and its scope-to-estimate ratio is implausible for a single-maintainer project.

## Findings, most severe first

**1. The task's inputs do not exist in the repository yet — the draft cannot honestly be written to spec today.**
The issue requires related-work claims to meet "#588's citation standard" and the draft to cover "the grading contract, the determinism argument, the reproducibility appendix" material that is supposed to flow from #589 (the white paper) per #591's ordering (`ordering_after: #520 PF-2 — the white paper is the technical core the submission is built from`). I checked the tree: `docs/comparisons/`, `docs/whitepapers/`, and `docs/performance.md` (the AC-1 deliverable of #512) all return zero matches — none of #588, #589, or #512/#560's published-numbers doc exist yet; all four cited source issues are `state: open`. AC-3 of #786 ("no new benchmarking is opened here; any measurement comes from #512 / #560 or the #588 appendices") is therefore currently unsatisfiable without either (a) blocking indefinitely on three other open, unstarted issues, or (b) someone quietly doing the "no new benchmarking" work anyway under a different label to produce numbers the draft needs. The issue's own `ordering_after: ["TASK-C591-1", 589, 588]` field acknowledges the sequencing but the issue does not state what happens if #786 is picked up before those land — a real risk given all of these are sitting in the same open backlog with no visible scheduling enforcement.
*Recommendation:* Add an explicit blocking precondition ("do not start drafting until #588 and #589 are merged, and until #512/#560 have at least one published number") or, if genuinely intended to run in parallel, say so and define what the draft cites in the interim (draft placeholders, TBD markers) so "complete draft" isn't quietly redefined downward.

**2. "Reviewed by at least one person outside the author" is gameable and unstaffed.**
ARCHITECTURE.md states plainly: "JLS is a single-maintainer pedagogy tool" (README/ARCHITECTURE, i18n section). No named external reviewer exists anywhere in #786, #591, or #520. The only candidate human touched by any of this cluster is Dr. Siever (#509), whose course pilot is explicitly speculative ("is interested in this fork if it becomes well enough matured" — not yet materialized). The acceptance criterion only requires that "the review comments and their dispositions are recorded" — it does not require the comments be substantive, that the reviewer have any relevant expertise, or that any comment actually change the draft. A single drive-by "looks fine" from any acquaintance, logged as "disposition: acknowledged, no change," technically satisfies this AC while delivering zero of the intended quality signal.
*Recommendation:* Name a reviewer pool or explicit sourcing plan (e.g., "solicit review from Dr. Siever or a CS-education mailing list"), and require the AC to demonstrate at least one substantive revision made in response to review, not merely a log entry.

**3. Effort estimate (`band_mw: 0.5-1`) is not credible for the stated scope.**
The task combines: writing a complete multi-section academic draft (grading contract + determinism argument + reproducibility appendix description + related work with per-claim citations), engineering and committing a runnable reproducibility appendix (fixtures, vectors, commands, expected output) that a stranger can execute unaided, securing and incorporating an outside review with recorded dispositions, and doing all of this only after three prerequisite documents land. Sibling issue #589 (just the white paper prose, no appendix, no outside review) is separately estimated at "1-2 mw," and #588 (two comparison notes with appendices) at "2-3 mw." #786 asks for a superset of that work (draft + review + appendix) in less time than either predecessor's narrower scope. This is a feasibility red flag, not merely a scheduling nuisance — it invites either a rushed, low-quality artifact that checks the boxes, or silent scope-cutting (e.g., a thin appendix that "runs" but proves nothing).
*Recommendation:* Re-estimate against #589/#588's actuals once those land, or split the reproducibility-appendix engineering into its own tracked task with its own budget.

**4. AC-4's reproducibility bar is underspecified against the project's own documented reproducibility caveats.**
AC-4 says a third party "obtains the paper's stated output," and the issue frames this as proof that "a paper about determinism whose artifact needs an email is self-refuting." But the project's own README already carries a load-bearing caveat directly on point: installers are explicitly *not* byte-reproducible ("the native packaging tools embed wall-clock state... rebuilding the same commit yourself will produce different checksums") while only the jar and `bom.json` are byte-reproducible. If the paper's determinism argument leans on simulation output rather than build artifacts, that caveat may not apply — but the issue never scopes which invariant the appendix is meant to demonstrate (byte-identical simulation output vs. byte-identical build vs. "same verdict"), so "obtaining the paper's stated output" is exactly the kind of criterion that could pass on a loose reading ("verdict matched") while failing a strict one ("bytes matched") that the paper's rhetorical claim implies.
*Recommendation:* Pin AC-4 to a specific, named invariant (e.g., "batch `-t` verdict and VCD output are byte-identical across two independent machines/JDKs," referencing `docs/batch-interface.md` / `docs/simulation-semantics.md`) rather than the vague "obtains ... output."

**5. No deliverable path is specified, unlike its siblings.**
#589 pins its deliverable to `docs/whitepapers/grading-contract.md`; #588 pins its deliverables to `docs/comparisons/grading-determinism.md` and `docs/comparisons/timing-honesty.md` plus named appendix directories. #786 says only that a "draft exists" and an appendix "is committed" — no path, no naming convention, no statement of whether the draft lives in-tree at all (vs. an external manuscript system, e.g. Overleaf, referenced only by link). Given the "reproducibility appendix is committed" language, presumably it lands in this repo, but that's an inference, not a requirement.
*Recommendation:* Name the paths explicitly, matching the convention #588/#589 already set (e.g. `docs/papers/grading-contract-submission-draft.md` and `docs/papers/appendix/`).

**6. Mixed-type `ordering_after` list is a latent tooling hazard.**
The YAML front-matter has `ordering_after: ["TASK-C591-1", 589, 588]` — one entry is a task-ID string, the other two are bare issue numbers, even though "TASK-C591-1" and issue #785 are the same referent (confirmed via search). Any script or bot resolving these dependency lists by issue number (as the other numeric entries imply) will silently fail to resolve the first entry. Minor on its own, but this project clearly has machine-parsed conventions across the whole TASK/FEAT/CAP hierarchy, so an inconsistent reference format in one field is a real (if small) process risk.
*Recommendation:* Use `785` consistently, or state the convention (task-id strings are intentionally distinct from issue-number ints) if that's deliberate.

**7. AC-3 phrasing quietly outsources verification of "meets #588's citation standard" to a standard that is itself an open issue's prose, not a ratified rule.**
"#588's citation standard" refers to AC-3 of #588 ("No competitor behavior is asserted without a link to their issue tracker, docs, or release notes"), which is currently just an acceptance criterion of an unimplemented issue, not a committed style guide. If #588 lands with a different final citation convention than its current issue text describes (plausible — issues get revised in execution), #786's AC references a moving target with no fallback if the target shifts.
*Recommendation:* Either freeze the citation standard as a short, separately linkable rule (e.g., in `docs/comparisons/README.md` once #588 lands) or have #786 cite the concrete convention rather than "#588's standard" abstractly.

## What's solid

- The checklist-style acceptance criteria (checkboxes) are concrete and closeable, unlike many prose-only issues in this tracker — one line each, easy to verify against.
- Explicitly scoping out new benchmarking ("No new benchmarking is opened here") is the right instinct to prevent scope creep into #512/#560's territory, even though (per finding #1) it currently creates a hard blocking dependency instead.
- The "Boundary" section cleanly separates drafting (this issue) from venue selection (#785) and submission (TASK-C591-3), which is good issue hygiene and avoids conflating three very different kinds of work.
- The self-refuting-artifact framing ("a paper about determinism whose artifact needs an email is self-refuting") is a genuinely good design principle for the reproducibility appendix, even though its enforcement criterion (finding #4) needs tightening.

## Verdict rationale

`needs-rework`: the issue is coherently scoped relative to its sibling tasks and has real, checkable acceptance criteria, but it is currently sequenced ahead of the artifacts it depends on (finding #1), carries an ungated human-review criterion that can be satisfied trivially (finding #2), and is estimated far below what its own combined scope (draft + engineered appendix + external review) plausibly costs (finding #3). None of these are fatal to the concept, but starting work on #786 today, as written, risks producing a technically-checked-off draft that cites nothing real and was "reviewed" by a rubber stamp.
