# Issue #514: CAP-30: an outside developer's first PR merges within a week, and JLS retains external contributors — the developer base Digital's decline is stranding has somewhere to land
- Lens: visionary
- Date: 2026-08-08
- Reviewer: Claude Opus 5
- Verdict: endorse-with-reframing

## What this issue is really for

Stated: recruit external contributors, with Digital's rejected-PR pool as the reachable
seed. Underneath that: **JLS is one person, and every rival in #510's table that is one
person is dying.** #510 §5 diagnoses bus-factor-1 as the mechanism of Digital's collapse
(97.7% of commits one man, 26 open PRs, no successor plan) and then prescribes, for JLS,
a recruitment program measured in *arrivals*. Three merged PRs and a sub-48h median
response do not reduce bus factor by one. The capstone measures the leading indicator and
never names the lagging one it exists to move.

This project has already written its bus-factor-1 down as a *design constraint* rather
than a problem: `SECURITY.md`'s rpm/AppImage signing rationale (README lines 62–70) argues
that "a single-maintainer signing key would add rotation/revocation risk." That is a
correct call under the current facts, and it is also the only place in the repo where
being one person is treated as permanent. CAP-30 is the one issue positioned to challenge
that, and as written it does not.

**Concrete addition (AC-7):** *one person other than the maintainer has independently
reproduced a tagged release and published the matching `.buildinfo`/hashes.* This is
elegant because the asset already exists — `docs/reproducibility.md` ships the
independent-rebuild recipe, and CI already re-checks jar/BOM byte-reproducibility — and
because it is the ideal first contribution for exactly the person this capstone is
courting: an hour of work, un-rejectable, zero merge risk, and it *validates a claim the
project makes to the world* rather than consuming maintainer review budget. It converts
a shipped strength into an on-ramp. A second such act — a non-maintainer with merge rights
on a scoped area (say `docs/`, or the importer family) who has exercised them — is the
real "somewhere to land."

## Reframing 1 — the first-contact surface is the tracker, not `SimpleEditor` (I am disregarding AC-5)

AC-5 pins the capstone on "largest file in `jls.edit` under 1,500 lines," inherited from
#510 §2's code-inspection-duel argument. Verified: `SimpleEditor.java` is 5,852 lines,
`StateMachineDialog.java` 1,929, `InteractiveSimulator.java` 1,437. The adversarial pass
already showed AC-5 is unsatisfiable by the feature it consumes. My objection is prior to
that: **code inspection is not where this funnel leaks.**

A developer arriving from Digital's tracker hits, in order: README → issues → build →
code. Step two is `is:open` = **605 issues**, essentially all authored by one account, most
carrying a YAML machine block, `AC-n`/`KC-n`/`PF-n` vocabulary, and comment threads
signed "_Generated by Claude Code_" arguing about roster contiguity. `.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/`
holds three files totalling ~40 KB of planning-corpus instructions and nothing a human
would fill in. #510 §5 named this precisely — "the spec-prose tracker reads as an internal
monologue" — and CAP-30's answer (PF-1) is to add two more templates *alongside* the three,
making the chooser six wide. That treats a symptom of the corpus being in the wrong place.

**The seam to cut along: the planning corpus is documentation, and this project is
extraordinary at documentation.** `docs/` already holds ~25 specs, three of them normative
(`simulation-semantics.md`, `batch-interface.md`, `file-format.md`), plus `ARCHITECTURE.md`
and `docs/plan/evidence/BRIEF.md` (referenced by the review comments but absent from the
tree). Move task/feature/capstone bodies into `docs/plan/` — versioned, diffable, reviewable
in PRs, which is strictly better for a corpus whose own rules are about roster/graph
consistency — and let the **issue tracker be the human surface**. PF-1's "two audiences,
two template families" problem disappears instead of being managed; PF-2's shelf of fifteen
becomes findable because it sits among tens of issues instead of six hundred; AC-1's
"a drive-by human bug report arrives without reading any planning template" becomes true by
construction.

If moving the corpus is too large a swing, take the ninety-minute version and keep the
outcome: **partition by label and publish the filtered view.** README and CONTRIBUTING
link `is:open -label:tier:task -label:tier:feature -label:tier:capstone` as *the* issues
link; the labels already exist. Either way, the capstone's binding acceptance criterion
should be about what a stranger sees on the issues tab, not about a line count in
`jls.edit`.

Keep the decomposition — #316/#84 is justified on maintainability and headless testability
(CONTRIBUTING lines 106–108: `jls.edit` is deliberately unfloored *because* the editor
isn't headlessly testable until it decomposes). Just strike it from **this** capstone's
acceptance path, so a contributor-attraction program is not gated on a multi-month refactor
whose own rationale is unrelated. And note what actually wins the inspection duel and costs
an afternoon: fifteen lines at the top of CONTRIBUTING saying *sealed exhaustive dispatch,
NullAway/JSpecify ratchet, blocking weekly mutation gate, byte-reproducible jar, three
normative specs, golden simulation tests*. Every one of those is true today and none of
them is visible at the moment of inspection.

## Reframing 2 — PF-5 inverts the recruitment it is meant to serve

PF-5 (4–7 mw — roughly half the capstone's 8–14 mw band) proposes building Digital's
rejected contributors' features *for* them: dark mode, live subcircuit dive, rebindable
keys. Digital's #1470 was a **keybindings PR** and #1464 a **UI PR**; these are people whose
motivation is expressed as code they wanted merged. Shipping their feature before inviting
them removes the itch that would bring them here, and spends the capstone's largest budget
line on the feature with the weakest causal link to its own outcome.

**Invert it.** Convert PF-5 from 4–7 mw of implementation into ~0.5 mw of *design*: file
live-subcircuit-dive and keybinding-settings as scoped, sketched, explicitly **reserved**
issues — approach outlined, acceptance stated, files named — and invite the named authors
to implement them here with a stated review commitment. The recruiting act is merging their
PR, not pre-empting it. This also discharges KC-30-1 by construction (nothing ships "only
to poach" if the poachee ships it), and it leaves dark mode where it belongs: #289, on its
own merits, independent of this capstone. If nobody comes, the features return to the
roadmap having cost a design sketch rather than seven engineer-weeks.

## Reframing 3 — PF-4 is the best feature here and is framed as marketing

PF-4 is described as "the plugin story Digital never offered." Its real justification is
stronger and native: **it is the fourth normative contract document.** JLS's architectural
signature — the thing that distinguishes it from every tool in #510's matrix — is that its
external surfaces are written down as specs with stability statements (`batch-interface.md`
is explicitly "a documented stability contract"; `file-format.md` and
`simulation-semantics.md` the same). `docs/extension-points.md` already exists as the
inventory. Publishing the extension API as `docs/extension-api.md` in that series, with the
same versioning language, is the move most aligned with the project's whole arc, and it is
the *only* feature in this capstone that opens a contributor pipeline that has ever worked
in this niche: **contributors are downstream of users.** Logisim-Evolution's 147
contributors sit under 7.4k stars; Digital's angry PR authors were Digital's users first.
An instructor who needs a custom element for a course and can build it against a stable API
is a developer JLS created rather than imported. Promote PF-4 and AC-6 to the head of the
required set; demote the poaching play behind them.

## Reframing 4 — KC-30-2 is confounded by the survey that motivates it

KC-30-2 fires if two quarters pass after PF-1..4 with zero external PRs, concluding "the
outreach premise is falsified." But #510 §4 says the universal gates — shop window
(screenshots, gallery, comparison table), first-run experience (the launch is an empty
`JTabbedPane`), discoverable examples, published benchmark — cost *days* and "gate
everything else in this survey," and CAP-30 requires **none** of them. README carries zero
screenshots today; `examples/` holds only `autograde`. If the funnel is empty because no
one can evaluate the tool, KC-30-2 will attribute the emptiness to outreach and shut down
the one strategy the survey found. That is a false negative produced by a kill criterion
firing on an unmeasured confound.

**One-line fix:** KC-30-2's clock starts only once the §4 gates are live, or the capstone
declares an ordering edge onto them. The gates cost days; the confound costs the strategy.

## What I would keep unchanged

- The Outcome paragraph is the best-written thing in the issue and needs no edit: *finds,
  picks a labeled issue, builds with one command, gets CI on their fork, review in 48h,
  merged in a week, three retained a year later.* That is a real user story for a
  contributor, and it is falsifiable.
- PF-2's fork-CI leg (AC-3) is the highest value-per-hour item in the whole capstone: it
  removes the maintainer from the critical path of a stranger's first attempt, which is the
  single most common place first PRs die.
- PF-6's "close the loop with the two bounced 2026 authors" costs an hour and is owed on
  its own terms, independent of any recruitment strategy.
- KC-30-1 is well-formed and should survive Reframing 2 unchanged.

## Verdict rationale

Endorse-with-reframing, not rethink: the premise is sound and unusually well-evidenced —
the window at Digital is real, time-limited, and this is the correct project to claim it —
and the Outcome, KC-30-1, PF-2's fork-CI leg, PF-4 and PF-6 are all work that strengthens
the project's arc. But roughly half the budget sits in PF-5, whose causal logic runs
backwards; the acceptance path is pinned to a line count while the actual first-contact
surface is a 605-issue machine tracker the capstone barely touches; the best feature is
justified as marketing rather than as the fourth member of the project's normative-contract
family; and the kill criterion will likely fire for a reason it does not measure. Fix those
four and the capstone gets cheaper, faster, and more likely to produce the thing it wants.
