# Issue #338: FEAT-010 (RESIDUAL): every installer leg is a required check, its runtime is pinned by digest, and each format's reproducibility is either proven or bounded in writing
- Lens: adversarial
- Date: 2026-08-08
- Reviewer: Claude Sonnet 5
- Verdict: needs-rework

## Summary

This is a feature-level "acceptance surface" issue sitting above two already-filed task issues (#443, #471). The body is internally elaborate — YAML machine block, a mermaid DAG, a LaTeX definition of a reproducibility residual — and most of its file/line citations check out against the repo (verified below). But the issue's own comment thread already documents an unresolved internal contradiction, at least two stale/incorrect claims in the body that cannot be fixed ("body edits are forbidden by this programme"), and several acceptance criteria that are gameable as written. An adversarial reviewer should not treat the comments as having already fixed the body: a reader who reads only the body (which is what most tooling and most future agents will do) gets wrong information.

## Findings, most severe first

**1. Unresolved internal contradiction about the critical-path dependency, flagged by the issue's own comment but not adjudicated.** §6 asserts as fact: *"Critical path: TASK-0027 → TASK-0028. This is a **real** dependency, not convention: a byte-comparison or signature check that runs on a `continue-on-error` leg cannot fail the build, so TASK-0028's criteria are unassertable until the matrix is armed."* Comment 1 (2026-08-04) reports that the filed #471 says the opposite: *"`ci.yml`'s reproducibility jobs invoke `scripts/build-installer.sh` directly and do not read `release.yml`'s flags"* — i.e., independent. The comment explicitly says this "needs an adjudication in a `REPLAN:` here" and that it is "not resolved by this dedup pass." No such adjudication comment exists in the 4 comments fetched. §6's sequencing claim, which drives the entire "arm first, then verify" ordering rationale (and the cost argument against doing the tasks in parallel), is currently unverified and contradicted by a sibling artifact. *Recommendation:* resolve the contradiction before treating §6 as authoritative; until then, do not block scheduling #471's work on #443 landing first.

**2. The issue body contains claims already known to be stale, and the stated process forbids correcting them in place.** The machine block lists `TASK-0027 — promote... (unfiled)` and `TASK-0028 — publish... (unfiled)`, and §2's roster table repeats "(unfiled)". Comment 1 states plainly: *"The `planned_tasks` block in the body lists TASK-0027 and TASK-0028 as unfiled... **It is stale.** Body edits are forbidden by this programme, so the correction lives here."* Both tasks were filed 2026-08-03, before this issue's own comment thread even finished. A tracker whose top-level scope declaration is known-wrong and whose fix policy is "leave the body wrong, hope readers find the right comment" is an acceptance-criteria/process defect in its own right: §5's IC-3 and IC-1 checks say "does not exist; TASK-002x builds it" — also stale, since #443/#471 exist. *Recommendation:* either lift the no-body-edit rule for factual corrections (filed/unfiled status, not scope) or add a pinned "STATUS" section that supersedes the machine block, rather than relying on a reader traversing all comments in order.

**3. Criterion 6 / IC-6 (BOM guard) is ambiguous enough to be satisfied by the wrong check.** §1 criterion 6 says: *"A bill-of-materials guard fails the build when a dependency changes without the record changing."* Verified in the repo: `.github/workflows/ci.yml` already runs a double-build BOM determinism check (`Build reference jar and BOM`, `sha256sum jls-*.jar bom.json`, lines ~812-846) — a **byte-determinism-across-two-builds** check, not a **dependency-drift** check. Comment 1 catches exactly this: *"IC-6 is a different predicate and remains unbuilt... A future pass reading 'BOM guard' in two places must not collapse them, and must not read #184's landing as discharging IC-6."* That caveat lives only in a comment. Anyone implementing or verifying IC-6 by grepping for "BOM guard" in `ci.yml` and finding the shipped job would plausibly (and wrongly) mark criterion 6 done. *Recommendation:* rename or explicitly disambiguate the two checks in the issue body itself (not just a comment) — e.g. "BOM double-build guard" vs. "BOM drift guard" — since this is precisely the kind of gameable-acceptance-criterion risk the adversarial lens exists to catch.

**4. IC-1's mechanical check does not test what the issue's own invariant requires, and is gameable.** IC-1: *"parse `.github/workflows/release.yml` with a YAML parser and count matrix entries whose `experimental` is `true`... Observe: 0."* But the workflow's own comment, quoted approvingly in the Background section, states the actual intent: *"green on a real run — flip experimental to false then"* (`.github/workflows/release.yml:280-285`, confirmed present in the checked-out file). A pure YAML-count check passes the instant someone flips four booleans to `false`, with zero evidence any leg has ever gone green on real hardware. IC-4 (the clean-machine walk) is supposed to be that evidence, but IC-4 is explicitly a **manual procedure** tracked by a separate issue (#284) with no automated gate tying it to IC-1. Nothing in §5 prevents IC-1 going green while IC-4 never runs. *Recommendation:* make IC-1's check consume IC-4's transcript (e.g., require a CI artifact from a real run per leg before the flag flip is accepted), or explicitly note in the issue that IC-1 by itself is necessary-but-not-sufficient and must not be read as proof of leg reliability.

**5. IC-3's "proven or bounded" escape hatch has no lower bound on bound quality, and the issue names its own precedent for gaming it.** §5 IC-3 requires "each record stating either byte-identity or a named bound." Open Question 2 explicitly recommends "publish the residual, narrow the claim, close" and cites #190 as "the precedent." Nothing in criterion 4's formal definition (the $R(f) \le \beta_f$ inequality) constrains $\beta_f$ to be tight, meaningful, or investigated beyond a superficial byte-range description. A record reading "residual: substantial, reason: unknown, bound: total artifact size" formally satisfies IC-3's "one-to-one mapping, each record stating byte-identity or a named bound" while delivering none of the value promised to "a distribution packager or an independent rebuilder" in the Intended Audience section. *Recommendation:* add a minimum-bar criterion for what counts as an acceptable bound (e.g., named byte ranges plus a root-cause hypothesis, not just a number), or explicitly accept the risk and say so.

**6. Feasibility risk in the environment executing this work: the JBR digest cannot be obtained from the same kind of sandboxed environment doing the planning.** `scripts/build-installer.sh:284` (confirmed) states the JBR download URL "is not reachable from this authoring environment, 403 from the egress proxy," and the fix requires running `curl ... | sha256sum` "from an unproxied machine." TASK-0027's cost line for this item (§ Cost: "0.5–1 wk... the cost is obtaining and verifying digests from an unproxied machine") acknowledges the constraint but the issue never names who has such a machine, nor whether an autonomous agent (which is how most of this issue's own machinery — the DAG passes, the dedup sweeps, the "Generated by Claude Code" comments — appears to be produced) can complete this step at all. This is exactly the kind of hidden environmental assumption an adversarial pass should surface: a chunk of "planned engineering work" is gated on manual human/network access nothing in the issue schedules or assigns.

**7. Proportionality: this is a large, self-referential process apparatus wrapped around a small amount of actual remaining work, for a project that describes itself as a "single-maintainer pedagogy tool."** Confirmed: `ARCHITECTURE.md:243` — "is a single-maintainer pedagogy tool serving an English-language [audience]." The issue itself re-estimates the *engineering* remainder at 3.5–7 maintainer-weeks (§ Cost), yet the coordination overhead visible in just this one issue — a formal DAG walk with a cycle-freedom proof, a LaTeX residual definition, four "Generated by Claude Code" boundary/dedup comments cross-referencing a dozen other issues (#82, #101, #134, #184, #185, #188, #189, #190, #191, #284, #285, #296, #300, #317, #443, #471, #580, #818, #819, #855...) — is itself a nontrivial and growing maintenance surface for one maintainer to keep coherent. The DoD's closing checklist item ("Each of #82, #188, #185, #184, #191, #134, #284, #285 is closed or given an explicit disposition") already needs updating per comment 2 to add #818 and #819. *Recommendation:* the maintainer should weigh whether the tracking apparatus is earning its cost, independent of whether the underlying CI work (arm the matrix, pin the digest, add BOM drift detection) is worth doing — those are not the same question, and this issue conflates them.

**8. DoD's own sequencing rule is already violated.** The Completion Criteria include: *"Open Questions 1–4 answered on this issue before the second child is filed."* Both children (#443, #471) are filed (2026-08-03, per comment 1), and no comment in the four fetched records an explicit answer to Open Questions 1–4 (only "recommended defaults" are listed in the body, which is not the same as a decision recorded as required by the Re-planning Protocol's "Every response ends in a `REPLAN:` comment"). If the recommended defaults are meant to *be* the answers, the issue should say so plainly rather than leaving a DoD checkbox that reads as currently unmet by the issue's own filed state.

## Verdict rationale

The issue is not fabricated or dishonest — it is unusually well-grounded in real file/line citations for a planning document, and it self-reports several of its own weaknesses (the §6 contradiction, the stale unfiled/filed status, the IC-6 ambiguity) rather than hiding them. But "self-reported and left unresolved across a four-comment thread with no fix applied to the body" is still a defect from the point of view of anyone who picks up this issue to act on it: the contradiction in finding 1 is load-bearing for scheduling, findings 3–5 are directly the "acceptance criteria that can pass while the goal fails" pattern, and finding 8 shows the DoD is already inconsistent with the issue's own filed state. This needs a cleanup/adjudication pass before it's safe to execute against as written.
