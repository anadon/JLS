# Issue #826: TASK-C569-2: each seam is labelled frozen, evolving or internal, and a breaking change owes a stated notice period — the guarantee Digital never gave its component authors
- Lens: adversarial
- Date: 2026-08-08
- Reviewer: Claude Sonnet 5
- Verdict: needs-rework

## Summary of the ask

AC-1–AC-5 ask for: a stability label (frozen/evolving/internal) on every seam from TASK-C569-1 (#825), a published deprecation-notice policy, a build check pinning frozen-seam signatures, an honesty constraint on what the project can actually support, and agreement between the new stability record and the #223 extension-point catalog. Read against the actual state of #223, #825, and `ARCHITECTURE.md`, the AC set has real structural problems.

## Findings, most severe first

**1. AC-5's "agreement" requirement is close to unsatisfiable as written, because the #223 catalog it must agree with is only 4/7 typed.**
`docs/extension-points.md` currently lists seven seam rows; three (`hdl.importer`, `app.command`, `gui.theme`) are `pending` with no contract type yet ("contract to be defined" / "shim contract over `jls.module.Activation`" / "theme/preferences object replacing `JLSInfo` statics"). #223's own body states these three are explicitly out of its scope ("the *contracts* of the three pending seams... owned by #61/#62... #84... #76"). A stability label of frozen/evolving/internal presumes a contract exists to label. AC-1 says every seam "listed by TASK-C569-1" gets a label, and #825's own AC-1 is ambiguous about whether pending, type-less rows are "listed" at all (#825 AC-1 asks for "type signature and one worked example each" — impossible for a seam with no type yet). So #826 inherits an unresolved question from its own prerequisite: do the 3 pending rows get a stability label of "internal" by default, get skipped, or block #826's AC-5 entirely until #61/#62/#84/#76 land? As written, AC-5 ("a disagreement is a build failure") can be met today only by either (a) waiting on four unrelated, unscheduled issues to land first — turning a documentation task into a hidden multi-issue dependency chain — or (b) inventing an undocumented convention (e.g., "pending catalog rows are exempt from AC-5") that the issue never states. *Recommendation:* AC-5 should explicitly define how pending catalog rows are represented in the stability record (e.g., a fourth pseudo-label "pending — no contract yet, not applicable"), rather than leaving the crawl toward "agreement" to be improvised later.

**2. AC-3 conflates a machine-checkable signature pin with a non-machine-checkable policy-compliance check, which makes the acceptance criterion partly gameable.**
Quoted: "A committed check pins the signatures of frozen seams: changing one without updating the stability record and following the policy fails the build." Pinning a signature (e.g. a golden reflection dump compared on `mvn verify`) is buildable and is a genuinely solid, testable AC on its own — nothing like it exists yet (`ExtensionPointCatalogTest` checks doc/code agreement and closed-contract-ness, not signature stability across commits; confirmed by reading `test/jls/ExtensionPointCatalogTest.java`). But "and following the policy" cannot be verified by a build: whether a maintainer gave the promised notice period, in the promised channel, is a human/process fact, not a compileable artifact. As written, a PR could pass CI by updating the stability record file in the same commit that breaks a frozen signature — satisfying the letter of "updating the stability record" — while giving zero actual notice, which is precisely the failure mode AC-3 exists to prevent. *Recommendation:* split AC-3 into (a) a signature-pin test (machine-enforced, keep as is) and (b) a process checklist item in the PR/release template for policy compliance (human-enforced, explicitly not claimed as build-enforced).

**3. The issue's own framing assumes an external "plugin" audience that the project's recorded architecture decision says does not exist yet.**
The title and Outcome speak of "component authors," "an outside developer... before writing a plugin," and treat this as protecting third-party plugin investment. But `ARCHITECTURE.md`'s recorded decisions say: "Plugin mechanism: removed (5.0.0, #80)... no plugin or manifest is known to exist," and the #222 plugin-trust-boundary decision states external element providers (#212) are "staged" behind "an explicit... 'trusted extension' opt-in" whose own demand gate has not opened, while out-of-process/untrusted-provider support "is reserved for a future... case; it is not built speculatively." Today's four typed-now seams (`elem.element-provider`, `gui.palette-contributor`, `hdl.exporter`, `collab.op-observer`) are consumed by first-party, in-tree modules (`CoreModule`, `GuiModule`, `HdlModule`, `CollabModule` per #223's evidence), not by any known external consumer. Publishing a formal notice-period *guarantee* to an audience that does not exist yet is exactly the kind of speculative build-out #222 explicitly rejects ("not built speculatively"). *Recommendation:* either narrow the Outcome to say plainly that today's audience is first-party in-tree modules (consistent with AC-4's "honesty" framing) and that the "outside developer" framing applies once #212 actually ships, or fold this task's timing into #212's gate.

**4. AC-4 is an unfalsifiable, unreviewable acceptance criterion.**
"The policy states honestly what the project can carry at its current maintainer capacity — a guarantee it cannot honour is not published" has no test surface: nothing in the AC set, and nothing plausible to add, can machine-verify "honesty" or "capacity to honour a guarantee." This is a good editorial principle for whoever writes the policy, but as a listed acceptance criterion sitting next to four testable ACs it reads as if it will be verified the same way and it cannot be — a reviewer could reject or accept the policy on pure subjective judgment while claiming AC-4 is "met." *Recommendation:* move AC-4 to non-normative guidance in the issue body (it already functions as guidance for AC-2's content) and drop it from the numbered acceptance-criteria list, or replace it with a concrete proxy (e.g., "the policy's minimum notice period is stated as *0 days* for any seam the CONTRIBUTING.md single-maintainer-bandwidth section does not name as actively watched" — something a reviewer can point at).

**5. Scope/cost risk: build-enforced signature pinning is new tooling with no existing precedent in this codebase, understated by the "0.5-1" effort band.**
`band_mw: "0.5-1"` (same band as sibling task #825, which explicitly promises to "add no new mechanism" per its own AC-5). #826's AC-3, by contrast, requires a *new* mechanism: nothing in the repo today diffs public-API signatures against a golden/frozen baseline (searched `test/jls/ExtensionPointCatalogTest.java` and the `jls.module` sources — the only existing check is doc/code-row agreement and closed-contract-type-ness, not signature-freeze-over-time). Designing and landing a real signature-pinning test (what counts as a "signature" — method list? annotation on default methods? added-but-source-compatible members?) is a non-trivial design task in its own right, not a half-to-one-unit documentation-adjacent task. *Recommendation:* size AC-3 separately, or scope it down for v1 to "no `elem.ExtensionPoint` contract type/id may change without a corresponding stability-record diff" rather than a full signature freeze.

**6. Minor: the title's comparison to "Digital" is an unverifiable editorial claim baked into a permanent issue title.**
"the guarantee Digital never gave its component authors" references a different, real open-source project (hneemann/Digital) making a factual claim about its API-stability practices with no citation, and it isn't a load-bearing part of any acceptance criterion. It doesn't affect feasibility, but it's the kind of claim a reviewer can't verify from this repo and shouldn't need to in order to review this issue. *Recommendation:* keep flavor text like this out of the title/AC surface, or footnote the comparison with a source.

## What's solid

- AC-2 (a published deprecation policy stating what counts as a breaking change, the notice period, and the announcement channel) is concrete and independently testable as a document-existence + content-shape check.
- The signature-pinning *idea* in AC-3, taken alone (ignoring the "following the policy" clause), is a genuinely good, buildable safeguard once scoped to what's mechanically checkable.
- `ordering_after: ["TASK-C569-1"]` correctly sequences #826 after #825 rather than letting the two tasks race on an undefined enumeration.

## Bottom line

The core intent — give the four already-typed seams (`elem.element-provider`, `gui.palette-contributor`, `hdl.exporter`, `collab.op-observer`) an honest stability label and a build-enforced signature pin — is achievable and worth doing. But AC-1/AC-5 don't say what happens to #223's three pending rows, AC-3 bundles a testable claim with an untestable one, AC-4 isn't reviewable as written, and the issue's own "plugin" framing overstates today's audience against the project's recorded #222 decision. Rework the AC set (especially AC-5's pending-row semantics and AC-3's split) before scheduling implementation.
