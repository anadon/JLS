# Issue #728: TASK-C554-2: the benchmark fixtures are tracked in-tree outside `riscv/`, including at least two circuits below CPU scale
- Lens: visionary
- Date: 2026-08-08
- Reviewer: Claude Opus 5
- Verdict: endorse-with-reframing

## What this issue is actually for

Stripped of its bookkeeping, #728 makes one claim: *JLS's performance story must not
be a single number that describes a processor.* That claim is right, and it is the
most valuable thing in the whole CAP-28 chain. Everything downstream — #726's command,
#730's machine-readable output, #555's `docs/performance.md`, #557's staleness lane,
#560's head-to-head — inherits its credibility from whether the workloads underneath
are meaningful. This task owns the workloads.

But the issue expresses that claim as *"commit two more `.jls` files outside `riscv/`
and write down their node counts."* That is the smallest possible discharge of the
claim, and it borrows its shape from a different problem.

## 1. The tracked-blob framing is inherited from #413 and does not transfer

#413's insistence on tracked bytes is a strong argument in its own context: the CPU-scale
anchor's only generator is `riscv/bench_kernel.py`, one of the 26 files D5 deletes, so
after the deletion the bytes are the *only* recoverable form of that circuit (#413 O7,
H3). The bytes must be tracked because the generator will not exist.

For the sub-CPU-scale fixtures the premise is simply absent. Their generator does not
have to be Python, does not have to live in `riscv/`, and does not have to be deleted.
It already exists: `test/jls/CircuitTextBuilder.java` has ~30 factory verbs —
`register`, `memory`, `mux`, `adder`, `clock`, `splitter`/`binder`, `subCircuit`,
`wire` — emitting the real save format, loaded through the real loader, and
`test/jls/SizeMeasurement.java` is standing precedent for a *measurement* harness that
constructs its own workload in code (a 300-`NotGate` chain), deliberately named so
surefire skips it and it runs on demand.

D5 itself says the approach survives even though the files do not: *"The **approach**
those files embody (programmatic word-level construction) may survive; the files may
not."* #728 re-homes the artifacts and quietly discards the approach.

**A committed generator dominates a committed blob here on every axis the issue cares
about.** `riscv/` deleted still leaves the suite green — more decisively, because
there is no file to lose. The census stops being a hand-maintained table beside a blob
that can silently drift from it, and becomes a *derived* property asserted against
pinned constants — the same guard #413 P3 wants, minus the artifact. And if
byte-identity is genuinely wanted, pin the SHA-256 of the emitted text: this project
already runs on that discipline (`DeterministicSaveTest`, a byte-reproducible jar, the
`.buildinfo` recipe in `docs/reproducibility.md`). Pinning a hash of generated bytes is
strictly stronger than storing bytes nobody can diff.

## 2. "At least two fixtures" is the wrong shape — the right shape is a family

`events/cycle(C) ≈ α(C) · β · |L(C)|` is #413 §7.10's own model of what a fixture
contributes. Two isolated points below CPU scale cannot separate α from β, cannot show
whether the engine degrades with size, and produce exactly the artifact CAP-28 exists
to avoid: three numbers describing three filenames.

The evidence base already knows the right shape. `docs/capability-roadmap/keystone-c-performance.md`
§2 does not publish one figure — it publishes `k500`/`k1000`/`k2000` at 1504/3004/6004
cycles with `initSimulation` and `runEventLoop` broken out. That is a *scaling table*,
and it is what makes the 318 ns/event figure interpretable. Once fixtures are generated,
size is a parameter and a sweep costs nothing; once they are blobs, every additional
point is another 120 KB tracked file and a new census record, so the suite will stay at
exactly the two the acceptance criteria demand, forever.

This matters beyond publication. ARCHITECTURE.md's recorded decision — *"discrete-event
interpreter is the sole strategy"* — carries the revisit trigger *"a concrete CPU-scale
design … unusably slow interactively."* A scaling curve is the only instrument that can
ever fire or refute that trigger, and lf-02 (compiled evaluation) is unarguable without
one. A two-point benchmark cannot say anything about it.

## 3. The census should be the *design input*, not the receipt

AC-3 treats the census as attribution — "so a number can be attributed to a workload
rather than to a filename." Good, but backwards. Keystone-c's event census shows the
CPU workload's callback mix is `Mux 875k / Register 428k / Splitter 250k / AndGate 207k
/ Memory 67k`. Throughput is a function of that mix at least as much as of node count.
Fixtures picked as "a counter and a memory loop" are a guess; fixtures picked so their
censuses *span* that histogram are a basis. Say that explicitly, and the two dimensions
become scale × mix rather than a bag of circuits — which is what turns three numbers
into a model.

## 4. Census is a missing shared primitive, and it wants to be a shipped capability

`git grep` finds no `Census` and no `KernelProbe` anywhere in `src/` or `test/`. The
tool that produced keystone-c's census was a scratchpad file outside the tree. Yet
#413 needs a census (P3), #728 needs one (AC-3), #442 needs event counts, and #730
needs them as fields. Three issues are each about to grow a private element counter.

Make it one thing, and make it a *product* surface rather than a test helper: a `-census`
flag on `JLSStart.FLAGS` (or a `Census` in `jls.sim`) that emits the element histogram,
net count, max width and subcircuit count for any `.jls`. Cost is small — the data is
already in the loaded `Circuit`. Payoff: the census in the perf doc is generated output
rather than a markdown table that drifts; instructors and autograders get a way to
characterize a submitted circuit; and it lands squarely on lf-07's "JLS as a platform"
arc, where the current programmatic surface is *one verb*.

## 5. The alignment observation the issue never makes

JLS is, by its README's first sentence, an educational tool. It ships **zero** example
circuits. `examples/` contains one Python autograder script; the only `.jls` files in
the repository are three test fixtures and a legacy directory.

#728 is about to author the first hand-designed circuits this fork has ever created —
a counter and a memory loop, the two circuits every intro course actually assigns — and
file them under `test/fixtures/` as benchmark-private blobs. That is a missed
opportunity of the cheapest possible kind. The same circuits, published under
`examples/` with a paragraph each, are (a) the shipped example gallery the project has
never had, (b) workloads whose published numbers a student can relate to a circuit they
have drawn, and (c) fixtures with a second reason to exist, so they do not rot when the
perf lane changes shape.

The counter-argument will be #413 O6: the destination is "forced" because `.gitignore`
line 8 ignores `*.jls` with a single exemption for `test/fixtures/**`. That is not a
law of nature — it is one unexamined line, and `!examples/**/*.jls` is one more. Note
that the ignore rule exists to keep developer scratch circuits out of commits; it was
never a statement about where the project's canonical circuits belong.

## Disregarding part of the acceptance criteria, and why

I am disregarding AC-1's implicit requirement that each fixture be a *committed file*,
and AC-2's "at least two." The first imports #413's constraint into a context whose
premise (the generator dies) does not hold, and it costs the suite its ability to
sweep. The second sets a floor so low it will become the ceiling. AC-4 (consume #413's
anchor rather than re-homing it) is correct and should be kept verbatim; that boundary
is real and the #554 comment thread is right that merging the two tasks would be an error.

## Restated outcome

> The suite's sub-CPU-scale workloads are produced by a committed, deterministic in-tree
> generator whose parameters include size, so the suite reports a scaling series rather
> than isolated points; the fixture set is chosen so its element-mix censuses span the
> CPU workload's callback histogram, not merely its node count; the census is emitted by
> a single shared census facility rather than transcribed by hand; and the CPU-scale
> anchor is consumed from #413's re-homing unchanged.

Acceptance, concretely:
- A run with `riscv/` deleted passes — trivially, because no measured workload lives there.
- At least one generated family with ≥3 sizes spanning at least a decade of node count;
  each reported figure states node count, element histogram and clocking regime.
- Emitted circuit text is pinned by hash, so a generator change that re-bases a published
  number fails loudly (the guard #413 P3 wants, without the blob).
- The census used by #728, #413 and #442 is one facility, not three counters.
- The hand-authored small circuits, if any survive as artifacts, ship under `examples/`
  with prose, and the benchmark suite is one of their consumers rather than their owner.

## What not to change

The ordering edge to #413, the refusal to re-home the anchor twice, and the disclaimer
against building a second gate (that is #442) are all correct and should be preserved
exactly as written.
