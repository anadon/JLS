## Summary of what this issue actually is

## Findings, most severe first

**1. [CRITICAL] The entire evidentiary corpus this issue cites for sufficiency and cost does not exist in this repository, and the cited commit does not exist either.**
Nearly every "measured, not assumed" claim in the Background, Cost, and Open-Question-8
sections is sourced to `docs/plan/REGISTRY.md`, `docs/plan/capstones/CAP-18-*.md`,
`docs/plan/features/FEAT-*.md`, and `docs/plan/evidence/{highfreq-determination.md,BRIEF.md}`
at `evidence_commit: 2d0ca9dcd9db78b36c3caf4c6c57dd8701862cc7`. I verified:
- `git cat-file -t 2d0ca9dcd9db78b36c3caf4c6c57dd8701862cc7` → `fatal: could not get object info` — this commit is not in the repository.
- `find . -iname "*REGISTRY*" -o -iname "*capstone*"` (excluding `.git`) → nothing under `docs/`.
- `git log --all --diff-filter=A --name-only | grep -c '^docs/plan/'` → **0** — `docs/plan/` has never existed at any point in this repository's history, on any branch.
- The `.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/capstone.md`/`feature.md` templates and `docs/capability-roadmap/README.md` (the real planning infrastructure) contain zero references to `docs/plan` or `REGISTRY.md`.

Yet the issue prints specific command transcripts against these nonexistent paths as fact
(`$ git ls-tree --name-only 2d0ca9d docs/plan/features/ | wc -l` → `57`,
`docs/plan/REGISTRY.md:72`, `:74`, `:86`, `:111`, `:112`, `:118`), and derives the required-set
cost bands (35–60 mw), the cumulative band (51–89 mw), the "60 features (FEAT-001..FEAT-060)"
registry count, and the per-rung cost reconciliations from them. None of this is checkable
against the actual repo; on the evidence available it is fabricated. This undermines the
entire §2 (Sufficiency) and Cost section, and the issue's own Completion Criteria item —
"Every cited evidence document and permalink resolves on the default branch at close" —
already fails today. **Recommendation:** either restore `docs/plan/` to the repo (if it exists
elsewhere, e.g. a private planning branch, that needs to be stated explicitly and linked), or
strip every citation to it and rebuild the cost/sufficiency argument from artifacts that
actually exist (`docs/capability-roadmap/*` is real and could anchor this instead).

**2. [HIGH] The self-audits are circular and prove nothing about correctness.** The
"ADJUDICATED" and "COVERAGE VERIFICATION" comments check this issue's dependency graph
against the *other* 57 issues' machine blocks in the same generation process
("0 asymmetries across the 57 machine blocks") and declare the graph internally consistent.
Internal consistency between machine-generated blocks says nothing about whether the
underlying costs, scope, or physical claims are actually right — it is the same process
checking its own arithmetic. Treat these comments as "no self-contradiction found," not as
independent verification.

**3. [HIGH] Feasibility/proportionality: 51–89 maintainer-weeks (per the issue's own,
unverifiable figures) for one lab lesson, on a project ARCHITECTURE.md itself calls "a
single-maintainer pedagogy tool."** That's roughly one to two years of full-time solo work,
gated behind thirteen other issues (8 required + 5 ordering prerequisites), before this
capstone can close — and a chunk of the payoff (AC-4/AC-5, the KiCad DRC integration) depends
on a tool JLS does not control. K18-6 only re-triggers a stop if the *demo slice* (the lint
alone, 3–6 mw) blows its 12-week budget 2x; there is no equivalent kill criterion tied to the
full 51–89 mw cumulative figure itself turning out to be 2x wrong, even though that number is
what actually decides whether funding the whole capstone is rational.

**4. [MEDIUM] The outcome statement oversells what AC-4/AC-5 can check.** The abstract claims
"the electrical intent...leaves JLS as a constraint file a real board tool enforces," but §3
risk 3 itself documents that KiCad's `DRC_CONSTRAINT_T`/`NETCLASS` model has no impedance
field — so what's actually exportable and checkable is a **length** constraint, not the
controlled-impedance constraint the framing implies. Open Question 3 acknowledges this and
punts the fix to #487 rather than correcting §1 step 5's language now, so a reader taking the
Outcome Statement at face value would expect more than the acceptance criteria can deliver.

**5. [MEDIUM] The rung ordering (lint → constraint export → element) is a governance
convention, not a technical gate.** The issue is explicit that FEAT-059 (#490) could
technically be built before FEAT-060 (#487) or FEAT-058 (#486) "and it would work" — the
`blocked_by` edges from #487→#486 and #490→#487 are stated to be largely "permanence"
(irreversibility) ordering, enforced only by a REPLAN comment convention. Nothing in the DoD
checklist or CI actually prevents a contributor from picking up #490 first; the safeguard is
social, not mechanical, despite §3 risk 1 calling this "the biggest risk in this capstone."

**6. [OK] The physics is genuinely correct and self-correcting.** The geometric reflection
series `V_k = 3.3·(1 − (−2/3)^k)` reproduces the claimed 5.5000 / 1.8333 / 4.2778 / 2.6481 V
sequence exactly, and the issue catches and corrects an error in its own cited upstream source
(3.1914 V → the correct 3.7346 V for k=5). This is careful, checkable work.

**7. [OK] Every claim checkable against actual source files in HEAD is accurate.**
Verified directly: `WireNet.java`'s field set (no length/impedance field; single
`@Nullable BitSet value` at line 405; `ends`/`wires`/`bits`/`hasinput`/`triState` at
lines 22–30), `Element.java`'s split between the file-local `id` (line 21) and the permanent

(`grep -c "new ElementType("` → 35, `grep -c "entry(Group\."` → 32) all match verbatim. The
`src/jls/hdl/board/{Board,Boards,PcfEmitter,PinBindings}.java` precedent cited for the
constraint-emitter shape is real. The required-feature issues #486, #487, #490 exist, are
open, and their titles match this issue's claimed content.

**8. [LOW] Cost is not proportional to the audience the abstract opens with.** "Intended
Audience & Impact" leads with first-years hitting an unexplained breadboard failure — but
that payoff is delivered entirely by the cheapest rung (FEAT-058, 3–6 mw), while the bulk of
the claimed cost (the KiCad export and closed-form T-line lab, plus the shared FEAT-013/014
spine) buys a signal-integrity workflow that AC-6/K18-4 deliberately keep invisible to that
same first-year audience. Not a contradiction — it's declared intentional — but worth naming:
most of the money funds an audience segment ("students taking a design to a board") narrower
than the one used to motivate the issue.

## Bottom line

The technical core (physics, the code-level "falsification guard" facts, the acceptance
criteria structure) is solid and the required feature issues are real and coherently linked.
But the sufficiency and cost arguments that are supposed to justify starting and eventually
closing this capstone rest on citations to a `docs/plan/` document tree and a commit hash that
do not exist anywhere in this repository. That is a correctness defect in the issue itself,
not a nitpick — it means the 35–60/51–89 mw figures, the "60 features" registry count, and the
per-rung cost reconciliations cannot currently be verified by anyone reading this repo, and on
the available evidence look fabricated. Fix that before treating this issue's numbers as
planning input; the underlying three-rung feature slate (#486/#487/#490) can likely proceed on
its own merits regardless.
