# Issue #592: FEAT-C37-1: the editor's ergonomic standing stops being an opinion — a published, cited parity catalog grades every interaction against the three incumbents before a single fix is funded
- Lens: adversarial
- Date: 2026-08-08
- Reviewer: Claude Sonnet 5
- Verdict: sound-with-concerns

## Summary of what's being asked

A documentation-only feature (no editor code) that publishes a scored `docs/` catalog of JLS editor ergonomics vs. Logisim-Evolution, Digital and CircuitVerse, each row cited and graded HAVE/GAP/REFUSE, gating all funding in #521 PF-2..PF-5. Five ACs; two prior review comments already on the issue.

## Findings, most severe first

**1. AC-1's mandatory citation list contradicts the issue's own "three incumbents" framing — CircuitVerse is named as a comparison target but never as a citation source.** The title and Outcome both commit to grading "against the three incumbents" — Logisim-Evolution, Digital, and CircuitVerse — and #521 (the parent capstone this feature serves) repeats "vs the three incumbents" verbatim in its PF-1 description. Yet AC-1's actual, checkable citation set is: *"Digital #882 / #1308 / #1129, Logisim-Evolution #88 / #1234, Issie width-inference messages, LogicCircuit bus ergonomics, bsiever-fork #18 and #4."* CircuitVerse appears nowhere in that list, while two sources that are not among the "three incumbents" (Issie, LogicCircuit) are. This traces back to #521's own `evidence:` field, which has the identical gap — so the defect is inherited, not newly introduced — but #592 is the issue that turns it into a binding acceptance criterion. As written, a catalog satisfies AC-1's letter while never citing a single CircuitVerse complaint, silently dropping one-third of the promised comparison surface (or conversely, an author who *does* add CircuitVerse rows gets no AC credit for doing the thing the Outcome actually promises). Recommendation: add CircuitVerse citations to AC-1's required set (candidates already surfaced in #510's teardown: CircuitVerse #1412 delay/timing, #349 subcircuit node disconnect, #5328 Verilog round-trip, #34 crash-and-lose-work) before work starts, or explicitly narrow the Outcome/title to the sources AC-1 actually requires.

**2. Two of the issue's own review comments identify concrete scoring defects that were "recorded rather than edited in" — meaning the current AC text does not require the fixes its own review pass says are necessary.** Comment 1 (pass 1, 2026-08-04) states the catalog's rows must carry "the owning feature number" per row (#593/#594/#595/#596/#570) or #596's forever-bucket bucket "can re-own #593/#594/#595 scope by simply scoring a row for it" — and explicitly frames this as a "recommended acceptance-criterion sharpening at authoring time, recorded rather than edited in." Comment 2 (pass 2) finds a specific row — Digital #1308 "wire coloring" — that "will misfire without it," and a second defect: the row has two plausible readings (state-colour theming, owned by #542/#76; vs. user-assignable per-wire colour, which is new format-changing scope) and "neither reading can be scored from the row as currently described." Neither AC-1 through AC-5 was subsequently edited to require an owning-feature column or to require rows to disambiguate multi-reading complaints. This means the issue can be closed as AC-compliant while reproducing exactly the failure mode its own maintainer-generated review already predicted and named. Recommendation: fold both recommendations into the ACs (e.g., "AC-6: every row carries an owning-feature id from {593,594,595,596,570,542,76}" and "AC-7: a row citing a complaint with more than one plausible reading states which reading it grades") rather than leaving them as comment-only guidance a future closer can ignore in good faith.

**3. The "acceptance vehicle" citation in the Boundary notes points to an issue that is now closed as a duplicate.** The body states: *"Acceptance vehicle: #91 (UI harness) and #441 TASK-0020 (headless interaction machine) are what make the graded rows assertable."* #441 was closed `state_reason: duplicate` (superseded by #84, "task absorbed into task", nothing dropped) — the supersession decision was recorded the same day #592 was filed (2026-08-04) and the close itself landed 2026-08-08. The underlying scope is preserved at #84, so this is not a loss of capability, but a reader following #592's citation today lands on a closed issue with no forwarding pointer in #592 itself. Since AC-3/AC-4's assertability claims lean on "which of the two will pin it" (#91 vs. #441/TASK-0020), an author filling in that per-row column against a dead issue number will either cite something closed or have to independently discover #84 is now the live target. Recommendation: update the Boundary note to cite #84 (residual: extract the 9-state mouse interaction machine) instead of #441, or note explicitly that #441's scope now lives there.

**4. AC-5's "timed baseline" has no stated protocol, so it is trivially gameable in either direction.** *"AC-5: A timed baseline for the CAP-37 AC-4 task (build a 4-bit counter from scratch) is recorded in the catalog, so the 'after is not slower' claim has a before."* There is no specified: who performs the task (a switcher-representative user vs. the catalog's own author, who has every incentive to record a slow baseline that later work can easily beat), how many trials, hardware/environment controls, or whether it is captured via the #91 harness (scripted, reproducible, replayable) or an unwitnessed human stopwatch. A single self-timed, self-reported number by the same person who benefits from an easy "not slower" comparison later satisfies the AC's text while providing no real evidential value — this is exactly the "gameable acceptance criteria" pattern: the letter can pass while the goal (a real before/after ergonomics signal) fails. Recommendation: specify n≥3 trials, a named or scripted actor, and require the baseline method to be reproducible (ideally via the #91/#441-successor harness) rather than a bare number in a table cell.

**5. AC-2's blanket "not scored is not an allowed grade" invites shallow REFUSE-by-fiat to satisfy the letter of the AC.** Forcing every row to a terminal HAVE/GAP/REFUSE with no "needs investigation" escape valve means an author under time pressure (recall the whole feature is budgeted 1-1.5 mw) can default ambiguous or hard-to-research rows to REFUSE with a thin one-line "reason in prose" — AC-2's only quality bar — rather than doing the comparative research the catalog exists to produce. Comment 2's own finding #1 (the Digital #1308 dual-reading row) is a live example of exactly this risk materializing before the catalog is even written. Recommendation: require REFUSE reasons to name the specific alternative considered and rejected (not just a category), and spot-check a sample of REFUSE rows against the source complaint at review time.

**6. External citations are unverifiable from this repository and rest entirely on one prior AI-authored survey (#510) with no link-check discipline.** Digital #882/#1308/#1129, Logisim-Evolution #88/#1234, Issie's width-inference messages, LogicCircuit's bus ergonomics, and bsiever-fork #18/#4 are all issues in trackers outside `anadon/jls`; none can be checked from this checkout, and AC-1 requires only "a citation," not a verified live link. This repo already has a working precedent for exactly this hazard — `HelpTopicsTest`'s link checker (ARCHITECTURE.md, "Help delivery" section) exists because unchecked cross-references rot. #510, the sole source for these numbers, is itself a survey whose "JLS baseline row was adversarially verified against the repository" (11 of 12 confirmed) but which does not claim the same verification was done for the competitor-tracker issue numbers it cites. Recommendation: at minimum, spot-check the numbered citations against the live trackers before publishing, and note in the catalog which citations were re-verified vs. carried forward from #510 unchecked.

**7. The #316 decomposition-boundary check (KC-37-1) asks graders to reason about an architecture that does not exist yet.** Every row must be judged "implementable in the decomposed collaborators that #316 produces — not inside SimpleEditor," with unimplementable rows "flagged as blocked on #316 rather than scored as ready." Verified against `pom.xml:408-411`: `jls.edit` is still explicitly "deliberately unfloored until the #91/#84 work makes editor code testable" — i.e., the decomposition #592's boundary note requires rows to be checked against has not landed a single collaborator class yet (TASK-0020/#441 was only just folded into #84, itself still open). This makes the ready-vs-blocked judgment a speculative guess about a not-yet-built seam rather than a checkable fact, for every one of what could be dozens of rows. This isn't fatal to a documentation-only feature, but it means the catalog's "blocked on #316" column is opinion dressed as a gate, and is worth flagging as a soft spot rather than a hard one.

## What's solid

- The core deliverable — a cited, graded catalog gating fix funding — is a sound instrument for the stated goal (stop funding ergonomics fixes on vibes) and the "no editor code" scope boundary is unambiguous and easy to audit.
- `gates: [#521 PF-2..PF-5]` and `ordering_after: [#316/#84, #510]` are internally consistent with #521's own `planned_features` and `ordering_after` fields — no contradiction found there.
- AC-4's bsiever-fork #18/#4 "closed or refused by name" requirement is concrete and testable (a reviewer can grep the published catalog for the two issue numbers).
- The acceptance vehicle's *healthy* half — #91 — is real and substantial: `test/jls/ui/` has 33 files at HEAD, so "which of the two will pin it" has at least one live, verifiable target even with finding 3 above.
- The issue is honest about its own dependency risk (the boundary note calls out #316 as a hard gate on the *fixes*, not on itself) and does not overclaim what a documentation feature can deliver.
