# Issue #521: CAP-37: the editor stops losing switchers on ergonomics — a parity catalog built from the incumbents' own top complaints, closed item by item with each fix pinned by a harness test
- Lens: visionary
- Date: 2026-08-08
- Reviewer: Claude Opus 5
- Verdict: endorse-with-reframing

## What this issue is really for

Strip the capstone to its load-bearing claim: **editing feel should become a
defended property of JLS rather than an accident of a 2005 Swing canvas.** That
claim is right, and it is the one thing on the roadmap that the standards-and-
capability program (`docs/capability-roadmap/`) cannot deliver — that program
makes JLS *capable*, and capability that loses the first ten minutes converts
nobody. So the goal is aligned and worth a capstone slot.

The *route* is where I disagree. CAP-37 proposes to reach that goal by
transcribing three competitors' issue trackers into a hand-scored document
(PF-1), then funding fixes in the order that document ranks them. I am
disregarding AC-1 and AC-3 as written — a hand-scored catalog as the funding
gate — and proposing the instrument below in their place. AC-2 (refusals by
name), AC-4 (upgraded), and AC-5 (K9) survive intact.

## Objection 1: the evidence source measures the wrong population

Most-reacted open issues on Digital and Logisim-Evolution measure *what annoys
the users those tools retained*, filtered through *what those tools'
architectures made hard*. That is a proxy for a proxy. It is not the same
population as "a switcher who bounced off JLS in ten minutes," and building
from it guarantees a permanent one-lap lag: JLS becomes competitor-shaped, and
by construction never leads on the axis the capstone cares about. Digital
#1308 (wire coloring) is an eight-year-old wish precisely because it was never
load-bearing for anyone; that it accumulated reactions is a fact about issue
half-life, not about editing cost.

## The reframe: make editing cost a measured number, not a scored opinion

JLS has something no incumbent has, and CAP-37 walks straight past it. Two
things are already in the tree:

1. **A closed op vocabulary** — `src/jls/collab/op/` (21 files, `OpSink`,
   `CircuitOp`, exact inverses, `docs/operation-layer.md`). Every circuit
   mutation is converging on a validated, invertible, serializable command.
2. **A headless gesture harness** — `test/jls/ui/` is ~30 files and ~9k lines
   at HEAD (`EditorGestureSupport`, `EditorGestureTest`, `EditActionMatrixTest`,
   `KeyboardEditingFaithfulTest`, `PaletteDropTest`), far past the "Layer 1
   only" that ARCHITECTURE.md still advertises.

Put those together and "ergonomics" stops being an opinion. Define a corpus of
canonical authoring tasks (the 4-bit counter of AC-4, wire an 8-bit bus, rename
eight pins, duplicate and re-wire a slice) and measure, in CI, headlessly:

> **gestures per op** — how many primitive user actions (press, drag, release,
> dialog round-trip, keystroke) the harness must issue to produce the N
> `CircuitOp`s that constitute the finished circuit.

The denominator is semantic intent; the numerator is user cost; the ratio *is*
ergonomics, and it is a number a ratchet test can defend forever. Nothing else
in this tool category can compute it, because nothing else has both halves.

What this buys, concretely:

- **AC-4 stops being a stopwatch.** A once-measured manual timing of one task
  is unfalsifiable a year later and cannot catch a regression. As a CI ratchet
  it catches every one, on every push, with no human in the loop.
- **PF-1 becomes derived output, not a 1–1.5 mw gate.** The catalog's ranking
  is generated by sorting corpus tasks by measured overhead. The prose part
  worth keeping — REFUSE rows with reasons, the bsiever-fork rows by name — is
  an appendix to #510, not a funding committee.
- **KC-37-2's forever-bucket hazard dies structurally.** Work stops when the
  budget number stops falling. That is a stop condition arithmetic enforces,
  which is strictly better than a 1.5× per-item stop-loss adjudicated by the
  same person doing the scoring.

## Objection 2: KC-37-1 gates on the wrong boundary

The capstone's hard gate is "nothing lands inside `SimpleEditor`; wait for
#316/#84." Two problems.

First, factual drift the capstone should absorb: **#441, named in
`ordering_after` and named again in #592 as an acceptance vehicle, was closed
as a duplicate into #84 on 2026-08-08** (migration comment on
`anadon/JLS#84`). The capstone's dependency row and the coverage comment's
"every referenced issue is open" both need re-derivation.

Second, and architecturally: protecting `SimpleEditor`'s line count is not the
risk. `SimpleEditor` is already down to 16 `markChanged()` sites, and
`docs/operation-layer.md` maps each one to an op kind. The real hazard is that
parity fixes get written as *gesture-local special cases* — a compound-selection
drag that mutates in place — forking editing semantics away from the op
vocabulary that #163/#167 replication, precise undo, and headless assertion all
depend on. Restate the gate as:

> **KC-37-1′:** every parity fix that mutates a circuit is expressed as a
> `CircuitOp` (extending the vocabulary where needed, with an exact inverse) and
> submitted through `OpSink`. No new inline `markChanged()` call site. Pinned by
> a ratchet over the 16-site inventory.

That gate buys collaboration, undo, and testability at once, is checkable by
grep, and — unlike KC-37-1 — does not stall the capstone behind a refactor.
Note also that bsiever #4 (identifier rules) lives in `jls.elem` name
validation, not the editor at all: the current gate blocks work that never
touches the god class.

## Objection 3: PF-5 adds the 180th hardcoded color

`grep -c "Color\."` over `src/jls/edit/*.java` returns **179** sites, and
ARCHITECTURE.md's FlatLaf decision already records ~126 chrome/canvas sites as
"#76's follow-up," with a dark default blocked on exactly that. PF-5 proposes
to ship wire coloring — i.e. site 180 — while #570 (CAP-30) separately owns dark
mode. That is the capstone pulling against the project's own recorded arc.

The elegant move: PF-5's real content is a **semantic token layer** — signal
meaning (driven/floating/conflict/bus-width) and text role map to tokens; tokens
map to a themed palette. One structural change discharges wire coloring
(Digital #1308), monospace (#1129-class), the #76 audit, and #570's dark mode
simultaneously. Three catalog rows and a CAP-30 headline item collapse into one
seam. Zoom/viewport polish should be checked against `src/jls/edit/Viewport.java`
and `EditorZoomTest` before being funded at all — that row may already be HAVE.

## Objection 4: PF-3 is scoped as a search box; it should be one command surface

JLS already holds three registries the incumbents do not: `Palette`/
`PaletteEntry` over `ElementRegistry` (icon, tooltip, group, **help topic**),
the shared `Action` layer from #75, and the `Map.jhm` topic index that
`HelpTopicsTest` keeps truthful. A palette filter field uses one of the three.
A single keyboard-invoked **command surface** over all three — type "shift",
get the shift-register element, the rotate/flip commands that apply to it, and
its help page — is the same implementation cost, subsumes "recently used," is
keyboard-first (extending the shipped a11y arc rather than sitting beside it),
and satisfies AC-5's "no new default-visible complexity" for free because it is
invisible until invoked. That is a *lead*, not parity with Logisim #1234.

## Objection 5: PF-4 should extend a contract, not improve prose

This is the highest-leverage PF and it is mis-filed as editor polish. JLS
already has a structured-diagnostic culture: `LoadError` carries
(category, location, detail, **actionable hint**), `TellUser` is the single
message chokepoint, and `NotificationRatchetTest` keeps raw `JOptionPane` out.
Meanwhile `src/jls/collab/op/OpRejected.java` — the funnel every op refusal will
pass through — is a bare `Exception(String reason)` with no category and no
hint. Reframe PF-4 as: **give `OpRejected` the `LoadError` shape, render it
through `TellUser`, and ratchet that every rejection carries a hint.** Issie's
width-inference philosophy then becomes a contract with a test, applied at the
one place refusals already converge, rather than a copy-editing pass over N
message strings that drifts the moment N+1 appears.

## What I would keep verbatim

- The outcome statement. "A switcher edits without hitting a missing-basic" is
  the right target and is stated in user terms, not feature terms.
- AC-2's discipline: every refusal refused *by name*, in prose. That is the
  project's recorded-decision culture applied to UX, and it is exactly right.
- The instinct that the harness is the acceptance vehicle. The reframe above
  does not weaken that — it makes the harness the *measuring* instrument as well
  as the pinning one.

## Sequencing under the reframe

1. Gesture-budget instrument + task corpus over the existing `test/jls/ui/`
   harness and `OpSink` (this replaces PF-1 as the gate; comparable cost).
2. KC-37-1′ ratchet over the `markChanged()` inventory.
3. `OpRejected` → structured diagnostic (PF-4's real content).
4. Command surface (PF-3).
5. Selection/manipulation as op-vocabulary extensions (PF-2), ranked by measured
   overhead.
6. Token layer, jointly with #76 and #570 (PF-5's real content).
