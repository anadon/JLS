# Issue #406: TASK-0017: a test that fails only on macOS or Windows blocks the merge, with the display and HDL suites armed and the coverage ratchet evaluated
- Lens: adversarial
- Date: 2026-08-08
- Reviewer: Claude Sonnet 5
- Verdict: sound-with-concerns

## Summary

The evidentiary claims are unusually well-grounded: every file:line citation (`ci.yml:41,143-156,244,259-263,297`, the O6 toolchain steps, O9's `CONTRIBUTING.md:83-99`, O11's `.gitattributes`, the display-tag count) was checked against current HEAD (`5311625`) and matches exactly, down to the `OSS_CAD_SHA256` value and the "24 of 25 display-tagged classes live under `test/jls/ui/`" correction. `#374`, its stated blocker, is confirmed still open with zero `timeout-minutes` anywhere in `.github/workflows/`. This is not a sloppy issue. The problems are in scope governance, acceptance-criteria robustness, and internal ordering — not in the factual record.

## Findings, most severe first

**1. The Method section's own execution order contradicts the audit comment that is supposed to bind it.** §8 step 3 ("Measure before deciding") instructs measuring the three-platform coverage spread "with the ratchet enabled and the macOS display suite armed," *before* step 4 ("Arm the toolchains fail-closed") and step 6 (remove `-Djacoco.skip=true`). But the second issue comment (2026-08-08, "ABSORBED") explicitly carries forward from the closed-duplicate #664/#670: *"Re-order §8: arm the toolchains and the macOS display suite first, then measure the three-platform spread, then decide the epsilon. A spread measured against a suite that is still self-skipping is not the spread the floor will face."* The issue body's numbered checklist was never edited to reflect this. An executor who follows §8 literally measures the spread against a suite where the HDL toolchain is still exit-0/best-effort (so HDL-driven tests self-skip), which is exactly the "not the spread the floor will face" defect the comment identifies — yet the comment's correction lives only in prose in a second comment, not in the checkable Method list or Definition of Done. **Recommendation:** before execution, edit §8 in the issue body itself to the corrected order; do not rely on an executor cross-referencing a comment against a stale checklist.

**2. The acceptance oracle for P1–P4 is self-admittedly gameable.** §11 states outright: *"The O1 checker's job-block scan is regex-based, not a YAML parse... a reformat would break it. The shipped test should parse YAML rather than scan lines."* Yet `PlatformLaneRatchetTest` (§6, "To be built") is specified as exactly that fragile scanner, and nothing in the Completion Criteria requires replacing it with a real YAML parse — §11's own recommendation is never promoted to a numbered prediction or DoD item. A trivial reformat (e.g., re-indenting `continue-on-error` under a differently-shaped anchor, or moving the toolchain step's failure branch outside the regex's lookahead window `(?=\n      - name: )`) would make the checker pass while the underlying policy (job-wide advisory status, or toolchain fail-open) is unchanged or even regresses. This is the load-bearing test for four of nine predictions, and it is documented as brittle by the issue's own author without being hardened. **Recommendation:** make "parse YAML, not scan lines" a blocking DoD item, not a threat-to-validity aside.

**3. The 20-run promotion record (P6) is measured through a mechanism the issue itself says masks the thing being measured.** H3 asks whether `rerunFailingTestsCount = 2` (O8) is hiding cross-platform flake. Open Question 1's "recommended default" is to *keep* the retries "for one release" while accruing the 20-run record that is the entire evidentiary basis for promoting the lane to a merge-blocking, required check. That means the flake-rate claim the promotion rests on ("at most one failure in twenty consecutive runs," H1/§10) is measured with retries in place, which the issue's own §11 concedes is "a lower bound on the true rate." Promoting on a measurement admitted to understate the true failure rate, while deferring the fix (retiring retries, owned by #91) to an unspecified later release, is a real risk of promoting flake as if it were stability. **Recommendation:** either drop retries before accruing the 20-run record (option (b), rejected as the default) or state explicitly in the promotion commit that the record's flake-rate bound is a lower bound, not the true rate, so a later regression is not mistaken for the lane being newly broken.

**4. Scope ownership between #406 and its own decomposition is unresolved by a "maintainer call" that the issue never records as made.** The first comment lays out a clause-by-clause map to eight newly-filed slice issues (#661–#673) and states two mutually exclusive resolutions — (a) #406 executes as the umbrella and the slices close into it, or (b) the slices execute and #406 narrows — calling this "a maintainer call, not a dedup call." The second comment then unilaterally closes six of the eight (#661, #663, #664, #667, #669, #670) as duplicates of #406 and folds their content into #406's scope by comment, without any visible maintainer decision distinct from the audit pass itself (both comments are authored by the same account, `anadon`, the issue's own author, with no third-party ratification). Five siblings (#665, #666, #671, #672, #673) remain open with `#406` as a precondition. Whoever executes #406 today inherits: (a) new must-honor material stapled onto a comment rather than integrated into the issue body (items 1–13 of the second comment — e.g. "#111 invariant 2 is binding: Linux `build` stays sole SpotBugs authority," "no failure is silenced with a broad `assumeTrue(!isMac)`," "a run that executed zero display-tagged tests must fail rather than pass"), none of which appear in §5/§8/§14 of the body itself; and (b) a live risk of double-executing the same `ci.yml` edits if a different agent independently picks up one of the closed-duplicate numbers before noticing the closure. **Recommendation:** fold the "carried forward" items into the issue body's actual §5/§8/§14 (not just a comment) before execution, and re-verify #661/#663/#664/#667/#669/#670 are still closed-as-duplicate at pickup time.

**5. Feasibility: the Definition of Done is not achievable within a single PR/session.** P5/P6 require real GitHub Actions run IDs accrued over calendar time (20 consecutive non-cron runs per lane), P7 and P9 each require deliberately-broken throwaway branches pushed and observed, and the coverage-spread table requires a separate measurement branch run before the real edit lands. This is at minimum a multi-week, multi-PR effort with a human-in-the-loop for branch-protection registration (§7.1, explicitly "outside the tree"). Nothing in the issue is wrong about this — §11 states "two full platform lanes double runner minutes" as a cost rather than hiding it — but an executor (human or agent) treating this as a single mergeable change will either under-deliver against the 20+ checkbox Definition of Done or silently truncate scope (e.g., landing the workflow edits but never accruing/recording the 20-run window, which is exactly the failure mode #317's own promotion rule (§4 invariant 5) exists to prevent). **Recommendation:** the issue would benefit from an explicit statement of what constitutes a landable first PR (e.g., "toolchain fail-closed + display armed + ratchet, with `continue-on-error` still in place") versus what is inherently a later, separate promotion commit — the current single Method list conflates a code change with an observational campaign.

**6. Toolchain fail-closed promotion creates a merge-blocking single point of failure on a self-flagged-fragile external dependency.** O6/§11 both note "Homebrew formula names may have drifted" and that removing the `exit 0`/`::notice` fallback converts a drifted formula name or a stalled/renamed `oss-cad-suite` release asset from a silent skip into a red, required check — i.e., every PR on the repo becomes unmergeable until a maintainer notices and fixes an unrelated third-party packaging drift, on two independently-flaky external surfaces (Homebrew tap state, a pinned GitHub release asset + SHA256). The issue does sequence this correctly (arm fail-closed → verify green on a branch → only then drop `continue-on-error`, §8), which mitigates the worst case, but nothing in the Completion Criteria requires a fallback/rollback plan for what happens when this SPOF fires in production three months after promotion (e.g., is there a documented "temporarily re-add `continue-on-error`" runbook, or does the whole repo block on a Homebrew formula rename until someone reads this issue again?). **Recommendation:** add a DoD item for a short operational note (in `CONTRIBUTING.md` or the workflow comment) on how to de-block the gate if the toolchain install starts failing for a reason unrelated to the change under test.

**7. Minor: `blocked_by: [374]` is honestly stated but currently unmet, and the issue provides no guidance on what "waived per rule 10" (referenced in the DoD) would look like here specifically.** Given #374's scope (23 jobs across 6 workflow files) is much larger than #406's two jobs, an executor under time pressure might be tempted to add `timeout-minutes` only to the `windows`/`macos` jobs and call the dependency satisfied — which would violate #374's own anti-vacuity requirement (its P4: a scanner must fail on partial coverage) without #406 saying so explicitly. This is speculative but worth a one-line guard.

## What is solid

- The observation set (O1–O12) is accurate and independently reproducible against current HEAD, not just the pinned evidence commit — a genuinely rare property for an issue this large.
- The falsifiable-hypothesis structure (H1–H3) and the explicit "next move on refutation" for each (§10) is a real strength: it pre-commits to not silently re-adding `continue-on-error` on failure.
- P7's two-deliberate-branches verification method for the JDK-26-advisory-stays-advisory invariant is concrete and directly testable, not hand-wavy.
- The scope boundary against siblings (#374 timeouts, #378 lane split, #162 Linux xvfb, #91 retry retirement, #265/#111 Stage 2 taxonomy) is explicitly and consistently stated in both the body and the comments, which is unusual rigor for avoiding scope creep even though (per finding 4) the boundary against its own slice issues is less settled.

## Verdict rationale

Nothing here is a "should-not-proceed" or "needs-rework" defect in the technical plan — the workflow-edit portion (§8 steps 4–6, 9) is well-specified and low-risk. The concerns are: a stale/contradicted execution order between the body and its own comment, a self-admitted-fragile acceptance test that is never hardened in the DoD, a promotion record whose statistical basis is explicitly a lower bound by the issue's own admission, and an unresolved ownership boundary against five still-open sibling issues. These are real risks to a clean landing but are all addressable without re-scoping the task, hence **sound-with-concerns** rather than **needs-rework**.
