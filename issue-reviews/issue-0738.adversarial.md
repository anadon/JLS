# Issue #738: TASK-C557-2: a red perf lane names which published number is now a lie, so the outcome is an engine fix or a reviewed re-publication — never silence
- Lens: adversarial
- Date: 2026-08-08
- Reviewer: Claude Sonnet 5
- Verdict: needs-rework

## Summary of the ask

TASK-C557-2 (`part_of_feature: 557`) wants a red scheduled-lane run to (a) name the specific sentence in `docs/performance.md` a regression invalidates, (b) offer exactly two dispositions (engine fix, or reviewed re-baseline + re-publication), (c) treat #476/#475-originated band moves as reviewed re-baselines, and (d) fail the lane if the published-numbers set and the band set fall out of correspondence. Boundary note: "the lane and bands are TASK-C557-1 and #442" — i.e. this issue is attribution/disposition logic layered on infrastructure owned elsewhere.

## Findings, most severe first

**1. Every piece of substrate this issue depends on is currently unbuilt, including the sibling task it explicitly orders after.** `docs/performance.md` does not exist in the tree (`Glob docs/performance.md` → no match; it is `#555`'s deliverable, open, not started). `test/fixtures/simulation-budget.properties` and the ceiling-band machinery this issue reuses do not exist (`#442`'s deliverable — itself `blocked_by: [377, 379, 413]`, none shown landed). `docs/machine-calibration.md`, cited repeatedly by `#442`/`#476` as the source of clocking-regime and calibration numbers, is also absent from `docs/`. Most directly: `TASK-C557-1` (`#735`), the very lane `#738`'s boundary note says owns "the lane and bands," is itself open and unimplemented — `#738` cannot be verified, or arguably even sensibly started, until #735, #442, #555, #554, #377, #379, #413 all land. The issue's own `ordering_after` correctly lists this dependency, so this isn't a drafting error, but it means `#738` as filed is pure paper: there is no way today to check any of its acceptance criteria against real code, and the review has no artifact to ground "traced through the suite's machine-readable output" against.

**2. The acceptance criteria are unverifiable as written — no format, no test, no falsification method for the one genuinely novel claim.** The load-bearing AC is: "A lane failure names the specific published number in `docs/performance.md` that the regression invalidates, traced through the suite's machine-readable output." Compare this to the rigor of every sibling issue in this family (`#442`, `#476`, `#557`): each supplies a Data Contract, a Method with red-then-green steps, and — critically — a demand for *deliberately induced failures pasted into the PR* to prove the mechanism isn't vacuous (`#442`'s DoD: "Three deliberate red runs recorded in the PR"). `#738` has none of that. Nothing here specifies: what "traced through" means mechanically (a 1:1 id mapping in the properties file? fuzzy string matching on band names vs. doc prose?); what counts as a passing test of the *mapping's correctness* as opposed to its mere existence; or what evidence must ship in the PR to show the naming is precise rather than "print every published number whenever anything regresses." A trivial implementation — dump the whole `docs/performance.md` number list on any band failure — satisfies the literal AC text ("names ... the number") while defeating the stated purpose (closing "the only escape route" by making the failure *actionable*, i.e. specific). This is exactly the failure mode the adversarial lens is asked to hunt for: the acceptance criteria can pass while the real goal (precise attribution) fails.

**3. Stale citation, discovered live during this review: `#475` (cited in AC-3 as one of the two "legitimate engine work" sources) was closed today as a duplicate, absorbed into `#232`.** AC-3 reads: "A band move originating from legitimate engine work (#476 / #475) is handled as a reviewed re-baseline, not as a gate bypass." `#475` (TASK-0056, LogicVector) is `state: closed`, `state_reason: duplicate`, closed 2026-08-08 with the comment "Superseded by #232 ... Lower number wins, so #232 is the survivor." So one of the two named "legitimate" sources of band moves no longer exists as an independent task; the outcome survives under `#232` but the citation in `#738` is already wrong on the day it's being reviewed. This is a small thing in isolation, but it is direct, checkable evidence that this issue family's cross-links decay quickly (see also finding 4) and that nobody has swept `#738` since. A reviewer picking this up should re-derive the citation, per this repo's own "rule 6" supersession-check convention used throughout `#442`/`#476`/`#557` — `#738` carries no such re-derivation instruction itself.

**4. Format break from the rest of the C557/CAP-28 family undermines traceability.** Every sibling issue in this cluster (`#442`, `#476`, `#557`, `#554`, `#555`, `#735`) follows a heavy template — Observations with reproducible commands, Hypotheses, Predictions, Data Contract, Falsification Criteria, a full Definition-of-Done checklist — and every cross-reference in their YAML `ordering_after`/`related` blocks spells out both the tag and the issue number, e.g. `#557`'s `"#442 TASK-0026 (supplies the ceiling-band machinery ...)"`. `#738`'s own header breaks that convention: `ordering_after: ["TASK-C557-1"]` gives the task tag with **no issue number**, forcing a reader to search for it (as this review had to). `#738` also skips Observations/Predictions/Falsification/DoD entirely and is roughly 1/10th the length of its siblings for a criterion (precise attribution of a metric regression to a doc sentence) that is at least as subtle as anything in `#442`. Either this task was deliberately scoped small and the template doesn't apply, or it is under-baked relative to the rigor the rest of the family enforces on itself — the issue doesn't say which, and as filed it reads like the latter.

**5. "Exactly two endings" doesn't obviously cover the correspondence-failure case the issue itself introduces.** AC-2 says disposition has "exactly two endings — engine fix, or reviewed re-baseline plus re-publication." AC-4 adds a *third* failure class: "A published number with no corresponding band, or a band with no corresponding published number, is itself a lane failure." An orphaned band or an orphaned doc sentence is a bookkeeping mismatch, not a regression the engine caused — its fix is "add the missing band" or "add the missing doc row," which is neither cleanly "an engine fix" nor really "a reviewed re-baseline" (there's no stale number to re-baseline; something is simply missing). The issue never states whether AC-4 failures are required to resolve through the same two-ending taxonomy as AC-1 regressions, or sit outside it. Left as is, an implementer can plausibly claim any resolution "counts" as one of the two endings, which is a second, quieter way the stated verification could pass without the real intent (a disciplined, closed disposition taxonomy) being met.

**6. No safeguard against the lane being muted, which is precisely the risk this issue exists to close.** The Outcome section frames the whole task as closing "the only escape route the staleness gate has: a red lane nobody can connect to a published sentence gets muted." Yet nothing in the acceptance criteria constrains *how the lane is wired into CI* — no criterion says the lane must be required (branch-protection-blocking), non-skippable, or free of `continue-on-error`/allow-failure escape hatches, and nothing says the attribution logic itself can't be quietly disabled later without comment. The project already has a documented pattern for legitimate, reviewed escape hatches elsewhere (`config/spotbugs-exclude.xml` entries, `WAIVED:` comments per `#442`'s DoD) — but also a documented awareness that ratchets rot ("two widenings and it is decoration," `#442` H2). `#738` names the muting risk explicitly and then does nothing in its own acceptance criteria to prevent the most direct version of it (an `if: always()` or a soft-fail step). This is a gap the issue itself flags as the whole point and then leaves unaddressed.

**7. Feasibility risk inherited from the CI policy the scheduled lane must live inside, not disclosed here.** `.github/workflows/ci.yml` carries an explicit, load-bearing comment: "the nightly cron runs ONLY the gui-wayland lane" (line 4, reinforced at lines 328, 491-492, 868, 919, 925) — i.e. today's single scheduled trigger is deliberately reserved for one lane, apparently by design decision tied to issue #47. `#738` sits on top of `#735`'s "a scheduled (not per-PR-only) lane," which needs a second cron trigger or a wholly separate scheduled workflow to exist without violating that recorded invariant. `#738` itself doesn't decide this (it's `#735`'s and `#378`'s remit per the Boundary section), but the issue gives a reader no signal that the "scheduled lane" its own attribution logic will run inside is currently policy-constrained to not sharing the nightly cron slot — a reviewer approving `#738` in isolation could reasonably assume "scheduled" is a solved, neutral detail, when it is not yet resolved anywhere in the tree.

## What's solid

- Ownership boundary is stated cleanly and doesn't try to re-litigate `#442`'s band mechanics or `#735`'s scheduling — good adherence to this family's "do not build it twice" discipline.
- Requiring the doc change and the band move to land in the same review (AC-2) is a sound, concrete anti-drift mechanism, assuming the ambiguity in finding 5 is resolved.
- Treating `#476`-class engine work as a reviewed re-baseline path rather than a gate bypass is the right default and matches how `#442`/`#476` themselves describe re-baselining.

## Recommendation

Do not schedule execution yet. Before this is actionable: (a) fix the `#475`→`#232` citation now that it's stale; (b) add an issue-number to the `TASK-C557-1` reference in the YAML header to match this family's own convention; (c) specify, at minimum informally, the mechanism and a falsification test for "traced through the suite's machine-readable output" so the core claim isn't gameable by a coarse dump of all published numbers; (d) resolve whether AC-4's correspondence failures fall under the "exactly two endings" taxonomy or constitute a third path; (e) add an explicit criterion that the lane is required/non-skippable, since muting is the exact failure mode the issue is written to prevent.
