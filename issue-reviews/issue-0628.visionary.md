# Issue #628: TASK-C559-5: one real published .cv project imports as a single undoable operation with zero unexplained losses
- Lens: visionary
- Date: 2026-08-08
- Reviewer: Claude Opus 5
- Verdict: endorse-with-reframing

## What this issue is really for

#628 is the acceptance task that decides whether the `.cv` importer is real. Its
reader, per #510 §3, is a CircuitVerse instructor who grades at scale and has
been burned — by #34 (crash-and-lose-work), #1412 (delay is queue priority),
#5328 (Verilog round-trip logic wrong), and by an LTI that passes back
hand-entered grades. The task correctly refuses a fixture written to pass. That
instinct is right and worth keeping.

But the criteria it wrote to serve that instinct describe an event that happens
entirely inside the repository: a fixture imports, a totality assertion holds, a
CI lane goes red on regression. None of the four ACs can be satisfied by an
instructor and none of them can fail because an instructor could not reach the
feature. That is exactly the failure mode #510 already documented at HEAD: the
Yosys netlist importer (`src/jls/hdl/imp/NetlistImporter.java`, #61) and the
`-board`/`-pins` flow are built, tested, and **wired to no CLI flag and no menu**
— worth 3–4 on the HDL-interop axis and scoring 3 because nobody can invoke them.
`grep -rn NetlistImporter src/ test/` returns only the package's own files and its
two test classes. The `.cv` importer is on the identical trajectory, and #628 is
the last gate before it gets there.

## The reframe: this is a loader problem, not an editor problem

Three facts from the tree, together, dissolve most of this task.

1. **`NetlistImporter` already solved atomic import, elegantly.** It never
   touches a live `Circuit`. It produces `ImportResult.saveText()` — JLS
   plain-text save format — which is then loaded through the ordinary loader
   (`src/jls/hdl/imp/ImportResult.java`: "loading it and re-saving yields a
   circuit indistinguishable from a hand-drawn one"). A partial circuit is
   unrepresentable: either a complete save text exists or an `ImportException`
   was thrown. Free atomicity, free fidelity, and the round-trip tests
   (`CircuitRoundTripTest`, `AllElementsRoundTripTest`) are already the oracle.

2. **JLS has no workspace-level undo to restore.** Undo is per-editor stacks of
   `CircuitSnapshot` inside `SimpleEditor` (ARCHITECTURE.md §"The editor"), one
   deflated save-text blob per circuit. A `.cv` project is many scopes; a JLS
   file is one top `CIRCUIT` with nested subcircuits. AC-2's "undo restores the
   workspace exactly" therefore demands either import-into-current-circuit (which
   creates the partial-state hazard the same AC then forbids) or the invention of
   a workspace undo stack — well outside a 1 mw band and outside #559's 3–5.
   Note also that `AddElements` explicitly rejects subcircuit blocks
   (`docs/operation-layer.md`), so the op layer cannot express this import today
   either.

3. **"Import" is already a taken word in JLS's UI.** `JLSStart.fileImport()`
   (line 2473) means "pull a `.jls` circuit into *this* circuit as an imported
   subcircuit" — `isImported()`, `addToImportMenu`, `finishImport`. A File >
   Import that means "open a foreign project" collides with a shipped verb.

Put those together and the better seam is obvious: **a `.cv` file is a container
the loader sniffs, not a command the editor runs.** `FileAbstractor.openCircuit`
already sniffs XZ, then zip, then plain text, enforcing #38 caps in each reader
and landing failures in the `LoadError` taxonomy. Adding "JSON with
CircuitVerse's shape" as a fourth sniff arm is the same shape of change, and it
buys the whole issue at once:

- Atomicity is structural, not asserted — nothing exists until a complete
  save-text loads.
- "Undo" becomes "close the tab / don't save", which is stronger than an undo
  entry and needs no new machinery.
- The `.cv` file works **everywhere a `.jls` works**: `-b -t` grading, `-vcd`,
  `-i`, `-export`, the `ghcr.io/anadon/jls` container, double-click association.
  That is the instructor's actual conversion path, and it costs nothing extra.

I am explicitly disregarding AC-2 as written. "Single undoable operation" is a
requirement generated by choosing to mutate a live workspace; choose not to and
the requirement, and its whole mid-import-failure hazard class, ceases to exist.

## Second reframe: end-to-end should end in a grade

#559's outcome sentence stops at "gets working circuits". But #510's CircuitVerse
row says the winnable segment is *instructors who grade at scale*, and JLS's only
5/5 axis is testing/grading. Opening old circuits removes a switching cost; it is
not the conversion event. CAP-29 knows this for Digital — PF-5 translates `.dig`
embedded tests to `-t` vectors and is called "the piece that actually converts
courses". CircuitVerse has no test vectors to translate, so `.cv` has no
equivalent PF, and the gap is invisible in this task tree.

Concretely: make #628's acceptance run the imported project under batch grading.
Import an instructor's reference circuit from `.cv`, write a `-t` vector file
against its pins, grade it headlessly, and pin the verdicts. That single addition
turns "the importer is trustworthy" from an internal claim into the demo that
#510 §3 says wins the segment — and it exercises the report, the mapping, and the
simulator together, which no fixture-level assertion does.

## Third reframe: one project is an anecdote; the corpus is the asset

AC-1 and AC-4 rest on *one* real published project. CircuitVerse hosts ~1.1M
public circuits and its projects are plain downloadable JSON — sampling is nearly
free. CAP-16's KC-16-1 is already a *corpus measurement*, and KC-29-2 makes this
capstone's economics depend on it; settling for N=1 here pulls against that
sibling discipline. Sample 50–100 published projects, publish construct coverage
("94% of a 100-project sample imports with no unexplained loss; the residual is
these six constructs"), commit a handful as fixtures and keep the rest as a
scored corpus run. That yields three things one project cannot: a real input to
KC-29-1 instead of a vibe, a marketing number for the head-to-head write-up #510
asks for, and a mapping table (#622) validated against what instructors actually
build rather than what the fixture author imagined.

Related: the fixture is a third party's creative work landing in a GPL-3.0
repository that keeps `pop_GPLv3.pdf` for provenance. Record author, source URL,
retrieval date and license per fixture, or the corpus becomes a licensing
liability the moment it matters.

## Duplication and one real gap

AC-1 and AC-3 mostly restate #556 AC-1/AC-2 and #624/#626. That is acceptable for
an acceptance task, but it means #628's genuinely unique content is AC-2 and
AC-4 — and AC-2 is the one I am asking to be replaced. Its replacement should be
the reachability criterion nothing in the #559 tree currently owns: **a `.cv`
opens from the GUI and from the command line, and a test asserts it** (the
`CliFlagTableTest`/`CliSmokeTest` pattern). Without that row, #628 can go green
while the feature stays as invisible as #61's importer.

The one place the shared contract is under-tested: #556 generalizes the report
from `.circ` (#323), which has not landed, and never mentions the importer that
*has*. `ImportSummary` carries a `coercedX` count — Yosys `x` bits silently
coerced to 0. That is precisely #626's "constructs that only look preserved"
class, live at HEAD, unnamed to any user. Validating #556's schema against
`ImportSummary` as a third consumer would prove the contract is format-agnostic
rather than `.circ`-plus-one, and would retire a shipped silent difference for
roughly nothing.

## Ordering note

KC-29-1's stop-loss decision is recorded here, at the end, in a 1 mw task. By the
time #628 runs, the 3–5 mw band is spent and the kill criterion cannot fire. Move
the corpus measurement ahead of #622's mapping table so the tripwire sits where a
downgrade to "documented external-conversion recipe" is still a live option.

## Verdict

endorse-with-reframing. The task should exist and its refusal of a
written-to-pass fixture is the right instinct. Keep AC-1 (widened to a corpus)
and AC-3. Replace AC-2 with "import never mutates a live workspace: the importer
emits complete JLS save text or fails, per the `ImportResult` precedent" and add
"a `.cv` opens through the ordinary loader path, from the GUI and from the CLI,
asserted by test". Extend AC-4 so the CI fixture is graded under `-t`, not merely
loaded.
