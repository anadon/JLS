# Issue #302: CAP-07: a design drawn in JLS comes back from an open shuttle as a physical chip the student can hold
- Lens: adversarial
- Date: 2026-08-08
- Reviewer: Claude Sonnet 5
- Verdict: needs-rework

## Summary of what this issue actually is

Not a normal feature request: it is a "capstone" node in a private, machine-maintained
planning ontology (`tier:capstone`, `requires_features`, `blocked_by`/`blocks` mirrored
edges, `mw` cost bands, `REPLAN:` protocol, mermaid dependency graphs) layered on top of
GitHub issues, generated and self-audited across ~500 issues by the same account
(`anadon`, all four comments signed "Generated by Claude Code"). The actual deliverable —
"student draws a circuit, gets a fabricated chip back" — is a reasonable and interesting
idea; the surrounding apparatus is where the problems are.

## Findings, most severe first

**1. The evidentiary basis is confirmed broken by the tracker's own follow-up issue, and the issue's own Definition-of-Done would fail today.**
Issue #493 (filed 2026-08-03/04, same author) explicitly lists **#302** among the 29
issues that are "Wrong about `master` — quotes or relies on branch-only code." The
specific quote at fault: `src/jls/hdl/HdlExporter.java:465`, `SubCircuit.class,` inside a
`REJECTED = Map.of(...)` bucket. I verified directly against this checkout (HEAD
`e7731bd`, which descends from the "surviving" commit `8288226`): there is no `REJECTED`
map and no such string in `HdlExporter.java` at all — master has three buckets
(`EXPORTED`, `SKIPPED`, `TOPOLOGY`), not four, per #493 §3. Worse, and not limited to that
one line: the issue's entire **Cost** section is sourced to `docs/plan/REGISTRY.md` and
`docs/plan/capstones/CAP-07-tapeout-shuttle.md` (six "Registry line" citations,
`REGISTRY.md:75/89/92/94/108/115`). `docs/plan/` **does not exist anywhere on this
checkout** (`ls docs/plan` → no such file or directory), and #493 §2 independently confirms
it is one of 195 files that "never existed on `master`" — "unrecoverable by re-reading."
The issue's own Completion Criteria bullet — "Every cited evidence document and permalink
resolves on the default branch at close" — is therefore already false for its own Cost
section, not just for the two corpus files (`08-views-determination.md`,
`09-format-adoption-plan.md`) the issue itself flags and withdraws. The 38.5–61 mw sum,
every per-feature band, and the "Priority 15 of 18" ranking rest on a document nobody
reviewing this issue in this repository can open. **Recommendation:** before any work
starts, re-source or drop the Cost section, and re-audit every anchor against #493's
mapping rather than trusting the `2d0ca9d` citations at face value.

**2. AC-7 is not verifiable by anything in this repository's own evidence discipline.**
The issue itself calls AC-7 "the only criterion that cannot be built or faked in tree,"
satisfied by a human pasting "chip markings and stimulus transcript" into a closing
comment. Contrast this with how the rest of the project treats provenance: README.md
documents sha256 checksums, `gh attestation verify`, and cosign signing for every release
artifact specifically because unverified claims of "this came from us" are not trusted.
Closing a `tier:capstone` issue on an unfalsifiable prose claim is a real governance gap
the issue doesn't address — nothing stops a "REPLAN" or closing comment from asserting AC-7
passed with no chip that anyone else can check.

**3. Feasibility/cost is out of proportion to a single-maintainer project, and the issue's own numbers admit it.** ARCHITECTURE.md states plainly: "JLS is a
single-maintainer pedagogy tool." The Cost section's own standalone-band sum is 38.5–61
"mw" (maintainer-weeks, never actually defined in the issue body — the unit's definition
presumably lived in the now-absent `REGISTRY.md`, compounding finding #1) — 8 to 14
person-months for one capstone, spread across six separately gargantuan feature issues
(#327, #328, #336, #339, #358, #359), each of which is itself an unfiled-tasks, unshipped
multi-thousand-word spec. For a hobby/educational tool with one maintainer, committing to
an actual ASIC tapeout program (with recurring submission deadlines, `docs/plan` process
overhead, and months-long fabrication turnaround) is a large, real-world commitment the
issue frames as routine roadmap bookkeeping.

**4. Single point of failure on an external program that has already failed once, acknowledged in-issue and then set aside.** Open Question 7 states the corpus "originally named
Efabless/chipIgnite, which shut down in 2025," and the recommended default is simply to
retarget Tiny Tapeout. The issue treats this as resolved ("recommended default: name Tiny
Tapeout... write the re-pick procedure into FEAT-044"), but the underlying risk — the
entire capstone's AC-1 through AC-7 are written against one third-party program's fixed
signature and metadata schema, and that program category has already had one entrant
disappear — is not mitigated, only documented as a kill criterion (KC-07-1) to be invoked
*after* the fact. There is no contingency plan or program-independence layer, just a
promise to notice and stop.

**5. Statistically weak "zero tolerance" acceptance criterion.** AC-4/KC-07-4 require a
20-design corpus with a hard zero local-pass/remote-fail count. Twenty samples give no
meaningful confidence bound on a claim as strong as "the local pre-check agrees with the
shuttle's own checks" — a single unlucky or unrepresentative corpus could either pass by
chance on a genuinely leaky pre-check, or fail on a fluke and block the whole capstone
(per KC-07-4's own wording, "a count of one is enough"). The issue never specifies who
selects the 20 designs or what diversity they must cover (bus widths, nesting depth,
bidirectional mix), so the corpus itself is gameable — a maintainer under deadline
pressure could pick 20 trivial designs and satisfy AC-4 without demonstrating anything
about the risky cases (deep hierarchy, wide buses) the rest of the issue worries about in
§3.

**6. Tension between "pin the shuttle template by digest" and JLS's own offline/reproducible-build architecture.** Open Question 5's recommended default is to vendor
the schema with "a scheduled non-required lane that fetches and diffs," justified by "the
tree has no `ProcessBuilder` or network fetch in `src/` to model this on." That is true —
and it is true precisely because JLS's documented model (README.md "Building from
source," `docs/reproducibility.md`) is a self-contained, bit-for-bit reproducible jar with
no runtime network dependency. A vendored-but-drifting schema is a strictly weaker
guarantee than "pinned by digest" implies: between vendor-refresh cycles, the emitter can
silently target a stale schema exactly as KC-07-1 predicts, and the "digest" language in
§4 of the required feature #328 asserts more assurance than the actual (non-network)
implementation can deliver.

**7. Internal tension between the "required feature" framing and the "Demo slice."** The
Cost section's final paragraph describes a 1.5–2 mw "Demo slice" — the wrapper emitter
alone, "over a design with no bidirectional ports and an explicit reset already drawn" —
that "satisfies AC-1 outright and produces a submittable artifact before FEAT-037 or
FEAT-021 exists." But §2's "Minimality" argument insists removing FEAT-037 (reset) means
"the chip in step 7 powers up undefined," and KC-07-2 says "no reset, no capstone,"
blocking the whole capstone. The Demo slice's very existence shows a wrapper-only export
is submittable well before the "required" set is actually complete, which weakens the
rule-E minimality argument's rhetorical force even though the two are not literally
inconsistent (the demo slice depends on the student manually drawing a compliant design,
sidestepping rather than closing the reset gap).

**8. `INOUT`/hierarchy claims are accurate as of this checkout, for what that is worth.** I
independently verified (not merely trusting the issue) that `HdlModel.Direction`
(`src/jls/hdl/HdlModel.java:28-33`) has exactly `INPUT`/`OUTPUT`, and `Register.java` has
no reset input in any of its four orientations, matching the issue's claims. These two
specific technical claims hold up even though the citation mechanics elsewhere in the
issue (finding #1) do not — worth noting so the review isn't read as "everything here is
wrong."

## Parts that are solid

- The core problem statement (JLS export stops at HDL+constraints; no ASIC-shuttle path
  exists) is accurate and independently verifiable in this tree (`grep -rin
  "tinytapeout\|tt_um" src/ test/` returns nothing here too).
- The dependency walk onto six sibling feature issues, and the explicit acyclicity check
  against their machine-readable `blocked_by`/`blocks` fields, is genuinely careful process
  work, whatever one thinks of the overhead of doing it this way.
- Re-planning protocol and kill criteria (KC-07-1 through KC-07-5) are concrete,
  falsifiable trip-wires rather than vague escape hatches — a real strength relative to
  most roadmap issues.

## Recommendation

Do not treat this issue as ready to drive engineering work until (a) every Cost-section
citation is re-pointed at something that actually exists in this repository per #493's
mapping (or the numbers are withdrawn as unverifiable, as the issue itself already did for
the two corpus documents it caught), and (b) a maintainer explicitly signs off on
committing single-maintainer bandwidth to an 8–14-month, externally-gated hardware program
rather than accepting this scope on the strength of the self-generated planning
apparatus's own internal consistency checks.
